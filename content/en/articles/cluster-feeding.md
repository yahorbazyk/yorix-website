# Cluster Feeding: Not Reverse Cycling, Not a Witching-Hour Diagnosis

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Cluster feeding is a **feeding pattern**: several short feeds closer together than you expected. Cleveland Clinic's example is a baby who nurses about every hour instead of every three or four. It is common in the first days of life, and it can show up later in the day — often in the evening — or when a baby wants comfort. This page is about that pattern, and about when it is *not* ordinary.

It is not [reverse cycling](/reverse-cycling) (nights getting heavier because daytime intake is low, usually later on). It is not [night weaning](/night-weaning). It is not the [witching hour](/witching-hour) (an evening fuss window, which can overlap with cluster feeds but is not the same thing). It is not the popular "second night" bump, which is a short section on [newborn sleep schedule](/newborn-sleep-schedule), not a diagnosis.

## Short answer

Cleveland Clinic, last updated 19 September 2024, says cluster feeding around the clock is **normal and expected in the first few days**. A newborn's stomach is small; frequent feeds, sometimes about hourly, are how they get enough. That around-the-clock phase generally eases as the stomach grows and milk supply comes in. **By the end of the first week, a baby should not still be cluster feeding all day and all night.**

After that first week, more frequent feeds can still be normal at certain times (Cleveland Clinic names evenings) or during a growth spurt or a need for comfort. What is **not** reassuring is cluster feeding **24 hours a day, for days on end, in a baby older than one week**. That can be a sign they are not getting enough milk — a latch problem, a temporary drop in supply, or something else a pediatrician or lactation specialist should look at. Cleveland Clinic is direct: call if a baby older than a week is cluster feeding around the clock.

This article is written from a breastfeeding source. Formula-fed babies can also want closer feeds at times; the red flags about intake, wet nappies, and unusual sleepiness still belong to your pediatrician, and we will not invent a separate official "formula cluster" protocol.

Do not try to "stretch" a day-old baby to teach a schedule. The newborn page already says they are not ready for one.

## What may help, and what is a warning

Offer the breast (or a planned bottle) when the baby is asking. Skin-to-skin and a quiet room make the stretch more tolerable. Look after yourself: a partner, a snack, a place to sit. Frequent feeding is work, not a failure of routine.

See a pediatrician or a lactation specialist if, after the first week, the "every hour" pattern never lets up, if wet or dirty nappies are fewer than you were told to expect, if the baby is hard to rouse or too sleepy to feed, or if feeding is painful and milk transfer seems poor. Those are intake questions, not a reason to wait for the cluster to "finish." The newborn page's red-flag section is the same idea.

If days later become light and nights become the only long feeds, switch pages: that is [reverse cycling](/reverse-cycling), and [night weaning](/night-weaning) is the wrong first move.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call if a baby older than one week is cluster feeding around the clock (Cleveland Clinic), if you are worried they are not getting enough, or if weight checks or nappies are off. Ask for lactation help if latch or supply is the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does cluster feeding last?**  
The around-the-clock newborn phase is usually a matter of days, easing toward the end of the first week (Cleveland Clinic). Evening or occasional clusters can return later. There is no official hour at which you "graduate."

**Is evening cluster feeding the same as the witching hour?**  
They can happen in the same part of the day. Cluster feeding is a feed pattern. The witching hour is a fuss window. Use both pages if you need both.

**Should I night-wean a cluster-feeding baby?**  
No, not as a first response, and not in the newborn days. Night weaning is a later decision on its own page.
