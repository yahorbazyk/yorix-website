# 10 Week Old Baby Sleep: Still Pieces, Even If Nights Look Better

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 10 snapshot**. It is not [9 week old baby sleep](/9-week-old-baby-sleep), not [11 week old baby sleep](/11-week-old-baby-sleep), and not [2 month old sleep schedule](/2-month-old-sleep-schedule). If you are searching for a "10 week old daily schedule," the honest answer is that official sources still do not hand you one.

## Short answer

KidsHealth still points to NSF's **14–17 hours** for the 0–3 month period. The other published newborn-month bands remain in force as disagreements, not as a blended figure: **11–19**, some NHS pages at about **8–18**, HealthyChildren.org's "about 16–17." Nothing official is labelled "week 10."

No new sourced landmark lands exactly on day 70. The 8–9 week body-clock shift (Sleep Foundation) may already have started. Period of PURPLE Crying often eases after the 6–8 week peak, though not on a calendar you can set a watch by. "Sleeping through the night" at 1–3 months still often means a **5–6 hour** stretch (KidsHealth), even when a parent is hoping for 9 hours.

### Nothing official changes on day 70

- **Hours:** the 0–3 month NSF 14–17 hour figure (via KidsHealth) is still the labelled range. The 11–19 and ~8–18 figures earlier on this page are not a week-10 official total.
- **No new landmark this week.** Sleep Foundation's 8–9 week rhythm may already be in play; it is not a fresh week-10 event.
- **A long night:** KidsHealth's 1–3 month use of "through the night" is often 5–6 hours, even if a parent is hoping for 9.
- **Training:** Sleep Foundation usually places formal training later, toward 4–6 months — not on a 10-week timetable.

## What is usually going on at ten weeks

Days can look a little more rhythmic — a fairly reliable first nap, then a pile of shorter ones — without being a timetable. Feeds in the day are often still every 2–3 hours, sometimes stretching toward 3. A baby who had settled to one night feed may suddenly want two again. That is a common community complaint; it is not an official "10 week growth spurt law." Feed on cue and keep an eye on wet diapers and weight.

Bedtimes in parent discussions at this age often still sit later than the 7 p.m. month-page examples, sometimes around 8:30 or 9:00 p.m. A later evening can be ordinary. Do not copy the 2-month sample day onto this week.

## The urge to write a daily timetable

r/sleeptrain and What to Expect threads at this age often ask for a full daily schedule, or describe a baby who had one night feed and now has two, plus 30–45 minute naps and maybe one longer one. Those recurring questions chose the examples. They do not set official timings.

- **Wanting a clock-hour plan too early.** Wake-window charts circulate widely. They are not official week-10 law. A flexible rhythm is fine; a plan you defend when the second nap is 28 minutes usually just adds stress.
- **Naps stuck at 30–45 minutes, with at most one longer nap.** That picture is still typical. See [short naps](/short-naps). A "10 week nap regression" is a popular name, not an official diagnosis.
- **An extra night feed after a quieter spell.** Hunger, a short last nap, or an ordinary wobble can all look the same at 2 a.m. If they seem unwell or weight gain has stalled, call. If they seem well, treat it as a week to watch, not a week to start formal training.

Safe sleep is unchanged.

## Five short pieces and a late evening (example only)

Not the 2-month sample day, and not week 9's "long stretch then 4 a.m." night. Suppose wakings and short bassinet naps scatter through the day — about 8:15 (30 minutes), 10:40 (40 minutes), 1:10 p.m. (25 minutes), a longer contact nap around 3:20, a brief 15-minute catnap after 5:30 — and a last feed that pushes lights-out near 9:00 p.m. Night stretches may then be 4 hours, then 2, then 2, or the opposite. The clock times are an illustration of a scrappy 10-week day, not a plan to copy.

## Night-feed questions at a routine visit

At a routine check, ask whether night feeds can follow their cue or still need a planned wake. Seek earlier advice if they are unusually sleepy at feeds, if wet-diaper counts are down, or if breathing sounds or looks wrong. If they cannot be woken or their colour changes suddenly, that is urgent care, not a wait for the next visit.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good 10 week old sleep schedule?**
There is no official one. This snapshot is not a timetable. For a month view, use [2 month old sleep schedule](/2-month-old-sleep-schedule) — and even that page is a range, not a contract.

**Is there a 10 week nap regression or growth spurt?**
No official sleep-hour change is published for this week. Short naps and a temporary extra night feed are common complaints. Wonder Weeks is not a clinical schedule.

**Can a 10 week old sleep 9 hours at night?**
Some do, some nights. KidsHealth's sourced meaning of "through the night" at 1–3 months is about 5–6 hours. Confirm with your pediatrician whether you still need to wake them.
