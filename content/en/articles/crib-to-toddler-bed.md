# Crib to Toddler Bed: When and How to Switch

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A crib that still works is not a late milestone. The move to a toddler bed or a low "big-kid" bed is a **safety and readiness** decision, not a birthday present. This page is the process guide. The [2-year sleep regression](/sleep-regression/2-years) mentions the switch in passing when sleep is already bumpy; it is not the how-to.

## Short answer

There is **no official age** when every child must leave the crib. Cleveland Clinic, quoting pediatrician Laura O'Connor, MD, says most toddlers switch between **18 months and 3 years**, and that there is no harm in staying in a safe crib. The same article, attributing the line to the American Academy of Pediatrics, says a toddler has outgrown the crib if they are **taller than 35 inches (89 cm)**, or if the rail is at mid-chest (nipple line) when they stand, or if they are **climbing out** even with the mattress at its lowest setting. Climbing out is the clearest "move now" signal, because a fall from a crib rail is a longer drop than a roll from a low bed. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission defines a toddler bed as a crib-mattress-sized bed for a child **not younger than 15 months** and weighing **no more than 50 lb (22.7 kg)**. If the crib is still safe, many families wait — and that is reasonable.

## When to wait, and when not to

Cleveland Clinic's advice is to weigh which arrangement is **safer tonight**. A bed is easy to get out of. A crib that a climber can tip over or fall from is no longer the safer option.

**Reasons to wait**, if the crib is still safe:

- They are content in it and are not climbing out.
- Sleep is already hard (illness, a [2-year sleep bump](/sleep-regression/2-years), a new sibling, [potty training or a move](/baby-sleep-and-travel)). Cleveland Clinic suggests one big change at a time when you can.
- They still need high sides to stay put, and they have not shown they can follow a "stay in bed" rule.

**Reasons to move now:**

- Repeated climbing, even with the mattress down and (if you use one) a [sleep sack](/sleep-sacks) that has not stopped it.
- They have reached the height or rail-line limits above.
- The crib is damaged, recalled, or missing parts.

Do not move a 15-month-old only because a toddler bed's box says that age is allowed. That is a product minimum, not a developmental green light. Do not move them only because a younger baby needs the crib, if a pack-and-play or a second safe crib would do for the infant instead.

## How to make the switch

HealthyChildren.org's "Big Kid Beds" article, by Rachel Y. Moon, MD, FAAP (updated 1 August 2022), and Cleveland Clinic describe the same unglamorous method:

1. **Talk about it first.** Cleveland Clinic suggests explaining that a bed is a "big-kid" step and that the rule is to stay in it after you leave.
2. **Childproof again, at their height.** Cords, unanchored dressers and TVs, unguarded stairs, unlocked windows, bathrooms, and anything climbable. A gate at the top of the stairs is essential; some families also gate the bedroom door. Moon also recommends childproof latches or taped-shut lower drawers so they cannot be used as steps, and keeping large toys that could injure a falling child out of the way.
3. **Keep the [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) identical.** The furniture changes; the order of the evening should not.
4. **Start with naps if you can**, so the first experiments happen while you are awake (Cleveland Clinic).
5. **Put them down drowsy, then walk them back.** Moon's steps: tell them to stay in bed until you come; if they get up, calmly and quietly lead them back; praise them briefly for being in the bed, then leave. She warns that "twenty farewell appearances in one evening is by no means an unusual number." Keep the return boring. Extra attention — even annoyed attention — can reward the exit.
6. **Do not make the adult bed the prize.** Both sources say to avoid rewarding breakouts with a family-room party or a regular place in your bed. Comfort them, then return them.
7. **Use a comfort item that is now allowed.** After 12 months, a small [blanket or lovey](/when-can-baby-sleep-with-a-blanket) can be reasonable. A pillow is still a soft object; wait until they are in a bed and your pediatrician is comfortable with it.

A convertible crib that drops a side into a daybed is one way to keep the same mattress. A floor mattress is a safe temporary option while you set up the room (Moon). We are not ranking brands.

## Bunk beds, bed rails, and why not to use a crib tent

This page is the furniture move out of a crib. What the child sleeps in *next* still has limits.

**Bunk beds.** HealthyChildren.org (last updated 5 September 2013): children love them, and they can be dangerous (a fall from the top, or the upper bunk collapsing onto the lower). If you use one anyway, **do not allow a child younger than 6 years to sleep on the top bunk** — they do not yet have the coordination to climb safely or to stay from falling. Other precautions on that page: place the bunk in a corner so two sides are walled; the top mattress must fit snugly and cannot ride over the edge; attach a ladder and use a night-light so the child can see it; install a top-bunk guardrail with a gap **no wider than 3½ inches**, and check the child cannot roll under it when the mattress compresses; mattress supports should be fastened slats or wires, not a loose frame; no jumping or roughhousing on either bunk. If you separate the bunks into twins, remove leftover connectors.

**Bed rails.** Use only a rail made for that bed, installed as the maker says, if a clinician or the bed’s age-and-design instructions call for one. Do not improvise with pillows or a gap that can trap a head or chest. This is not a shopping review; if the child is still in a crib, rails on an adult bed are the wrong product.

**Crib tents and “safety” nets over a crib.** Do not use them. They are not a recommended way to keep a climber in. Mesh or fabric over a crib can loosen, trap, or become a climbing-and-fall hazard, and they fight the bare-crib rule already on [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies). If a child is climbing out, that is a reason to plan the toddler-bed move on this page, not to zip the crib shut. HealthyChildren.org’s lists of sleep products to avoid include add-ons that make a crib less bare or less safe; a tent is in that family, not in the “helpful accessory” family.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if you are unsure whether a climb or a height measurement means the crib is unsafe, if the child has unusual muscle tone or a medical reason they cannot use a low bed yet, or if weeks of walking-back have not settled and you want a plan that fits their development.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I transition my toddler from a crib to a bed?**  
When the crib is no longer safe (climbing, 35 inches, or rail at mid-chest), or when they are clearly ready and you have childproofed. Many families wait toward the later end of the 18-month-to-3-year window.

**Is 18 months too early?**  
It can be, if they are not climbing and the crib still fits. It can be necessary if they are already getting out. Follow safety first, not the calendar.

**Should I use a toddler bed or a twin bed?**  
Either can work if it is low, stable, and used within its limits. CPSC's toddler-bed definition assumes a full-size crib mattress and a child 15 months to 50 lb. Guardrails, if you use them, should not create a gap a child can slide into.

**What if they keep getting up?**  
Walk them back the same way each time, stay calm, and keep the room safe. This is normal in the first nights. See also [sleep associations](/sleep-associations).

**Can I wait until after a vacation or a new baby?**  
If the crib is still safe, yes. Cleveland Clinic prefers not to stack big changes. If they are climbing, safety wins.

**Can a toddler sleep with a pillow, or on a mattress on the floor?**
Pillows stay out of infant cribs. For a toddler in a bed, follow the dentist- and pediatrician-advised, age-appropriate bedding already discussed with your clinician, and keep the space clear of hanging cords and top-heavy furniture. A mattress on the floor is a household setup, not an AAP "floor bed" protocol; if you use one, it still needs a clear, smoke-free, safe room (no adult pillows piled as bumpers). This is not a product review.
