# 4 Week Old Baby Sleep: A Week-4 Snapshot, Not the Month Plan

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This page is the **week 4 snapshot**. The month view — including that page's example day and wake-window comparison — lives on [1 month old sleep schedule](/1-month-old-sleep-schedule). Use that guide for the month view and this page for the week-four snapshot. We will not repeat that month page's timetable here.

## Short answer

At about 28 days you are at the end of the first month, and you still do not have an official clock day. KidsHealth cites NSF's **14–17 hours** for the 0–3 month period. HealthyChildren.org's "about 16–17, often in short pieces" is another way of describing the same era. Sleep Foundation and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby publish **11–19**; some NHS pages go as wide as about **8–18**. None of those is a special week-4 total.

The landmark that *starts to become relevant* this week is the body clock, not a new hour total. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby places the beginning of that rhythm around **4–6 weeks**. That is a start, not a finished 7 p.m. bedtime. KidsHealth's note that "sleeping through the night" at 1–3 months can mean a **5–6 hour** stretch is a possible meaning of the phrase, not a promise at day 28.

## What is usually going on at four weeks

You may have a one-month check this week or next. That visit is a good moment to ask whether you still need to wake for night feeds. Until that conversation, do not drop the wake-to-feed plan you were given.

Feeds are still frequent. A slightly longer stretch may have appeared in week 3 and then become less reliable. Evening fuss can be building toward the later 6–8 week peak described for Period of PURPLE Crying (NCSBS); do not treat this page as a second [witching hour](/witching-hour) essay.

Safe sleep is unchanged. If they fight a swaddle, that is a comfort experiment, not a reason to place them on the side or the tummy. Stop swaddling when they show signs of rolling — most babies are not rolling at four weeks.

## Bassinet transfers and later evenings

Parent communities talk about a "4-week nap regression" and about transfers that suddenly fail. We describe those situations in our words. They are not a clinical duration and not a diagnosis.

- **The bassinet transfer stops working.** Asleep in arms, awake within minutes in the bassinet, after days or weeks when the transfer was easier. Contact naps often return. That is a common week-4 complaint, not proof you missed a training window.
- **Evenings run later.** A baby who seemed to settle around 8 or 9 p.m. may fuss toward 11 or midnight. Late "bedtimes" are still ordinary at this age. Do not copy a 7 p.m. month-page bedtime just because the calendar says "1 month."
- **Hands-out wrestling in a swaddle.** Some babies start fighting the wrap. A safe-sleep space still has to stay on the back and clear. If you loosen or drop the swaddle, do it as a comfort change, not as a stomach-sleep experiment.
- **"Is there a 4 week sleep regression?"** There is no official one. More alert days, shorter bassinet naps, and later evenings are the usual week-4 picture. Link the month page for the broader 1-month view.

## A late evening, not a full day (example only)

The 1-month page already shows a sample day. This sketch is only the late stretch parents describe when transfers fail. Suppose a feed around 8:15 p.m., a settle in arms, a move to the bassinet at 8:35, eyes open at 8:45, another hold, a second try at 9:10 that lasts 20 minutes, then a long fussy spell with a feed at 10:20 and a first longer doze after 11:00. The next evening may be earlier. Do not copy the hours onto tomorrow.

## Use the month visit — and when not to wait

If a one-month check is on the calendar, take weight-gain and night-feed questions there. Do not wait for that appointment if they will not rouse, if milk intake or diapers have dropped, if they look yellow, or if breathing looks wrong. Emergency care is for a baby who will not wake, who cannot breathe comfortably, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a 4 week old old enough for a sleep schedule?**
No official one. This page is a snapshot. The month view is [1 month old sleep schedule](/1-month-old-sleep-schedule). A short wind-down is fine; a defended timetable is not the job of week 4.

**Why are bassinet naps only 30 minutes at 4 weeks?**
Short pieces are still typical. See [short naps](/short-naps) for the general pattern. Training to "lengthen" a nap is not a week-4 plan.

**Can I start sleep training at 4 weeks?**
Sleep Foundation discusses formal training later, generally around 4–6 months. Week 4 is not that window. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).
