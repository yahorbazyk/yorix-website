# 7 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Keeping Three Naps or Moving to Two

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

The question most parents of a 7-month-old are really asking is: *is it time for two naps?* At this age some babies are still happily on three, some are fighting the late-afternoon catnap, and some swing between the two from one day to the next. Below you'll find the sleep totals, how to tell which nap pattern fits, wake windows for 7 months, two example days, and a bedtime routine that holds up when the day has gone sideways.

## Short answer

Seven-month-olds need the same **12–16 hours a day** (naps counted) as every baby between 4 and 12 months, per the AASM, which the AAP endorses. Most are on **two or three naps**. Mayo Clinic expects a morning and an early-afternoon nap through the first year and only recommends phasing out an extra late-day nap at around **9 months**. Wake windows in the published charts are **2–4 hours** for 5–7 months and **2.5–4.5 hours** for 7–10 months (Cleveland Clinic). If the third nap keeps failing, try two naps with an **earlier bedtime** rather than pushing your baby to stay awake.

## How much sleep does a 7 month old need?

Nothing changes officially at 7 months: the AASM's 12-to-16-hour band covers every baby from 4 months up to the first birthday. For the split between night and day, Australia's Pregnancy, Birth and Baby site suggests nights of 10 to 14 hours for babies between 6 and 12 months, with naps adding up to 2 to 4 hours.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry's target for this month is roughly 14 hours a day, of which 2.5–3 hours are naps. Napper's range is 12–15 hours, and it pictures two naps lasting between one and two hours apiece.

## Three naps or two at 7 months?

There's no age at which a baby must drop the third nap. The two app examples even disagree: Huckleberry says most 7-month-olds still need three naps "for another month or so," while Napper describes most babies of this age on two. Mayo Clinic's 9-month suggestion for losing the extra nap is a reasonable outer marker.

**Signs two naps might suit your baby now** *(practical suggestion)*:

- The third nap takes 20+ minutes to start, or doesn't happen, most days for a week or more.
- The first two naps are each about an hour or longer.
- Your baby is still in a good mood by late afternoon on days without the third nap.
- Bedtime keeps getting pushed late because of the third nap.

**Signs to stay on three for now:** short first naps, a baby who melts down by 4 p.m., or night wakings that increase when you try two naps.

**Making the switch.** Move bedtime earlier on two-nap days. Losing daytime sleep without making it up at night tends to backfire: Nationwide Children's Hospital links fewer naps to an overtired baby and a rougher night. Expect some mixed weeks with two naps on some days and three on others. For the bigger picture, see how nap counts shift month by month in our [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age).

## 7 month wake windows

Seven months falls right where two chart bands meet, so it helps to see both:

| | Band ending at 7 months | Band starting at 7 months |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleveland Clinic | 2 to 4 h | 2.5 to 4.5 h |
| Sleep Foundation | 1.5 to 4.5 h | 1.5 to 6 h |

The apps give narrower targets for this exact month (app examples, not clinical guidance): Huckleberry 2.25 to 3.5 h, Napper 2.5 to 3.5 h.

On a three-nap day the windows sit at the lower end, often around 2.25–2.5 hours. On a two-nap day they need to stretch to about 3–3.5 hours, especially before bedtime *(our reading of the ranges above)*. No organization like the AAP or AASM publishes wake windows; treat every row as a starting point and adjust by your baby's signs.

## Bedtime routine for a 7 month old

The strongest evidence for any bedtime habit comes from a randomized trial of 405 mothers whose children were 7–36 months old. After a baseline week, families who followed a set nightly routine for two weeks saw children fall asleep faster and wake less often and for shorter periods than the control group (Mindell et al., 2009). Seven months was the youngest age in the infant arm (7–18 months).

To build a 7-month routine:

- **Keep it short.** Cleveland Clinic suggests about 20–30 minutes.
- **Finish where the night happens.** Nationwide Children's Hospital wants the final steps done in your baby's sleep room, with lighting that stays the same until morning.
- **Separate milk from sleep.** After six months, the same hospital suggests the bedtime feed shouldn't be a routine step. Offer milk first, then move on to the bath and the rest.
- **Brush the first tooth.** Once a tooth shows, the AAP's Brush, Book, Bed campaign recommends a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste, and it rules out bottles in bed.
- **Let your baby finish falling asleep.** KidsHealth's approach for this age is to put your baby down still a little awake; if there's crying, give it a few minutes, then comfort without lifting them out.

## Night wakings and feeds at 7 months

By this age, KidsHealth says, a feed in the middle of the night is usually no longer a need, although plenty of breastfed babies still ask for one. Brief rousing is universal: Nationwide Children's Hospital counts four to six short wake-ups a night for every baby and separates "self-soothers," who drift off again unaided, from "signalers," who call out. It also notes that somewhere between a quarter and half of babies keep waking past six months. If your baby still has night feeds and you want to reduce them, talk to your pediatrician first, and see our [dream feed](/dream-feed) page for one gentle option.

Separation worries can start to appear in this window too (KidsHealth). The same source points out that if each wake-up earns a cuddle, a feed, or a chat, a baby may learn to wake for it.

## Daytime development that affects sleep

- **Solids.** HealthyChildren.org suggests solids from about 6 months, alongside breast milk or formula. Plan solid meals during wake windows, not right before a nap *(practical suggestion)*.
- **Rolling and sitting.** A baby who rolls front-to-back and back-to-front reliably may sleep in whatever position they end up in, KidsHealth says, but should still be put down on their back. Anything hanging over the crib should already be gone; Nationwide Children's sets that at around 5 months.
- **Room-sharing.** The AAP's advice (via KidsHealth) is a separate sleep space in the parents' room for six months at minimum, and ideally until age one.

## What can make a 7 month schedule harder

- Dropping the third nap without moving bedtime earlier.
- A long, stimulating routine that keeps stretching *(practical suggestion)*.
- Leaving a bottle with your baby in the crib (HealthyChildren.org warns of tooth decay and ear infections).
- Big changes to the nap place or bedtime from day to day.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Talk to your doctor if your baby snores loudly or has pauses in breathing, isn't feeding well or gaining weight, seems unusually sleepy or hard to wake, has lost skills, or if night wakings are leaving you unable to cope.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps should a 7 month old take?**  
Two or three. Plenty of babies keep a short late-day nap until around 9 months.

**What are 7 month wake windows?**  
Usually about 2.25–3.5 hours. Cleveland Clinic's charts give 2–4 hours (5–7 months) and 2.5–4.5 hours (7–10 months).

**What time should a 7 month old go to bed?**  
Example schedules mostly put it between 6:30 and 8 p.m. On two-nap days, go early.

**What's a good bedtime routine for a 7 month old?**  
Something short and repeatable: milk before the routine, then washing, sleepwear, brushing any teeth, a song, and down awake. Around 20–30 minutes is plenty.

**Why is my 7 month old fighting the third nap?**  
Often because they're ready to stay awake longer. Try a two-nap day with an earlier bedtime and see how the night goes.
