# 6 Month Sleep Regression: Is It Real, and What Can You Do?

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Around six months, plenty of families expect sleep to get easier. For some it does. For others, a baby who had started to string longer nights together begins waking again, protesting in the crib, or snacking at night. Is this a "6 month sleep regression"? Sometimes it is a passing phase linked to development. Often it is something more ordinary, such as a schedule that needs a tweak.

## Short answer

The evidence for a distinct **6 month sleep regression** is weak. Sleep Foundation says **some babies have one, but many do not**, and some parents see their baby's sleep **improve** at this age. When sleep does get worse, Sleep Foundation links it to greater awareness of surroundings and sounds, new physical skills like rolling and sitting, and changing schedules. It says symptoms usually pass **within a few days or weeks**. A steady routine, putting your baby down sleepy but awake, and a brief pause before responding at night are the core strategies.

## Is the 6 month sleep regression real?

There is no study showing that babies, as a group, sleep worse at exactly six months. Sleep Foundation stresses how much infant sleep varies: a 6-month-old can regress, stay the same, or improve, and all three are normal. Huckleberry's guide (an app example) reaches a similar view and calls the idea of a fixed 6-month regression a myth, while acknowledging that sleep can get bumpy as new skills emerge.

It also helps to separate a **regression** (sleep that was good, then got worse) from sleep that **hasn't consolidated yet**. In a study of 388 infants, between 27.9% and 57.0% of babies at 6 and 12 months did not sleep through the night, depending on whether that meant 6 or 8 hours in a row. Not sleeping through was not linked to mental or psychomotor development, or to mothers' mood. Waking at night at six months is common, not a sign that something has gone wrong.

## Signs parents notice

Sleep Foundation lists these as typical:

- more night wakings, and more trouble getting back to sleep;
- difficulty falling asleep at bedtime;
- more crying at night;
- longer daytime naps, sometimes at the expense of night sleep.

Before calling it a regression, check for **illness, teething discomfort, hunger,** or a **change in routine** such as travel, starting childcare, or a parent returning to work.

## What may be behind it

Sleep Foundation describes several overlapping changes:

- **More awareness.** Six-month-olds respond more to sounds and their surroundings and can be easily overstimulated.
- **New skills.** Rolling over and sitting without support take practice, and some babies "rehearse" in the crib.
- **Separation anxiety.** Some babies start protesting when a parent walks away.
- **Schedule shifts.** As more sleep moves to the night, nap times and bedtime often need adjusting.

Tresillian adds that at around 6–7 months many babies start waking more because they are more aware as they move into light sleep phases.

## How long does it last?

Sleep Foundation says the symptoms are usually short-lived, often resolving within a few days or weeks, though a plateau in progress can last longer. If things are no better after several weeks, look at whether a new sleep association has formed (for example, being fed or rocked back to sleep every time) or whether the daytime schedule fits your baby's age.

## How to help a 6-month-old sleep better

### Keep the same bedtime steps

Sleep Foundation recommends the same sequence every night, with a feed and calm activities beforehand. Choose three or four steps you can repeat anywhere, such as bath, pajamas, feed, song.

### Put your baby down sleepy but awake

Sleep Foundation and Cleveland Clinic both say this helps your baby link the crib with falling asleep, which makes it easier to resettle during the night.

### Pause before going in

Sleep Foundation suggests waiting a minute or two at a night waking to see whether your baby settles alone. If you need to go in, keep the light low and the visit short.

### Comfort without lifting, if you can

For separation protests, Sleep Foundation suggests staying by the crib and comforting with a gentle touch or quiet voice rather than taking your baby out, then moving away once they are calm. Tresillian describes a similar sequence: cuddle until calm, place on the back drowsy, then soothe with "shh" sounds and rhythmic patting, and return if distress builds.

### Check the day

At this age, Cleveland Clinic gives 2–4 hours of awake time between sleeps for 5–7 months, and Mayo Clinic suggests at least two naps, often around 9 a.m. and 1 p.m., with a third short nap for some babies. Our [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) chart and [nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age) can help you adjust.

### Daylight and activity

Sleep Foundation notes that active, light-filled days help babies learn that daytime is for play and night is for sleep.

## Night feeds, solids, and sleep at 6 months

- **Night feeds.** Whether your 6-month-old still needs a night feed depends on growth and feeding. Ask your pediatrician before you drop one.
- **Solids.** The AAP recommends introducing solid foods at about 6 months, when your baby shows signs of readiness. We found no evidence in the sources we checked that starting solids improves sleep, so treat it as a nutrition milestone rather than a sleep fix. See our [baby feeding schedule](/baby-feeding-schedule).

## Safe sleep reminders at 6 months

- Always place your baby on their back. Once they roll both ways on their own, you can leave them in the position they choose (NICHD Safe to Sleep; HealthyChildren.org).
- Keep the crib free of pillows, blankets, bumpers, and soft toys.
- Swaddling should have stopped once your baby started trying to roll (KidsHealth).

## Should you sleep train during a 6 month regression?

Sleep Foundation suggests that around 6 months many babies can self-soothe, making it a common time to start methods such as the Ferber method. Choose a calm week without illness or travel. Our [sleep training methods guide](/sleep-training-methods-explained) compares the options.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Sleep Foundation says sleep problems at 6 months don't usually need medical attention, but you can raise them at any checkup. Call sooner if poor sleep comes with fever, pain, breathing pauses or loud snoring, poor feeding, or fewer wet diapers, or if you feel you can't cope.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the 6 month sleep regression real?**  
Some babies have a rough patch at around six months, but many don't (Sleep Foundation). The evidence for a fixed 6-month regression is weak.

**How long does the 6 month sleep regression last?**  
Usually a few days to a few weeks (Sleep Foundation).

**Why is my 6 month old suddenly waking at night?**  
Common reasons include more awareness of surroundings, practicing rolling or sitting, separation anxiety, illness, teething, or a schedule that needs updating.

**Should I feed my 6 month old every time they wake?**  
Not necessarily. Some wakings are for comfort. Ask your pediatrician whether your baby still needs a night feed.

**Can I sleep train at 6 months?**  
Many families do. Sleep Foundation describes 6 months as a common starting point.

**Is there a 24 week sleep regression?**
Twenty-four weeks is 6 months. Use this page, alongside the 6-month schedule. Whether the "6 month regression" is a distinct event is already discussed above.
