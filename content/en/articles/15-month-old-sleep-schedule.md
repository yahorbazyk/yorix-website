# 15 Month Old Sleep Schedule and the Switch From Two Naps to One

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Fifteen months is right in the middle of the most common window for the **2-to-1 nap transition**. Some toddlers have already settled into one long midday nap; others still need two; plenty bounce between the two patterns for weeks. This page explains how much sleep a 15-month-old needs, how to tell whether your toddler is ready for one nap, how to make the switch, and what the day can look like on either pattern.

## Short answer

The AASM recommends **11–14 hours of sleep a day, naps included, for children aged 1–2**, and the CDC repeats it on its 15-month milestone page. Boys Town Pediatrics says the move from two naps to one usually happens **between 15 and 18 months**, and KidsHealth says most toddlers have **one nap by about 18 months**, sometimes sooner. A meta-analysis found that **fewer than 2.5% of children stop napping altogether before age 2** (Staton et al., 2020), so this is a merge, not an end to naps. During the switch, plan on an **earlier bedtime** and some mixed days.

## How much sleep does a 15 month old need?

The AASM gives 11–14 hours for 1–2 years, and the CDC's 15-month page says the same. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby describes 10–12 hours at night after 12 months, plus one nap of 1–2 hours.

**App example (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry aims for about 13 hours, usually at least 11 at night and 2–3 hours of day sleep over 1 or 2 naps.

## Is my toddler ready for one nap?

Boys Town Pediatrics calls 15–18 months the average age for the change and notes it may not line up with a daycare's schedule. KidsHealth gives one clear sign: **a toddler who fights the morning nap is probably ready for just an afternoon nap**. Huckleberry (app example) also lists naps that become very brief, fighting either nap, and nights that regularly come in below 10 hours.

**Probably ready** *(practical suggestion)*:

- The morning nap is refused or very short on most days for 1–2 weeks.
- When the morning nap does happen, the afternoon nap gets skipped or bedtime drifts late.
- Your toddler copes well until lunch without the morning nap.

**Probably not yet:**

- Your toddler is visibly exhausted by 10 a.m.
- Skipped morning naps lead to more night waking or early rising.
- It's a rough week for another reason (illness, teething, a new caregiver).

## How to move from two naps to one

Boys Town describes the usual pattern: families gradually trim the afternoon nap and keep the child up a little longer each morning, until the old 9:30 and 2:30 naps become one nap around midday. Lunch usually moves earlier too. A step-by-step version *(practical suggestion, based on that description)*:

1. **Push the morning nap later** by 15–30 minutes every few days.
2. **Cap the afternoon nap** if needed so bedtime doesn't drift (Huckleberry, app example, suggests capping each of two naps at 90 minutes).
3. **Move lunch earlier,** to around 11–11:30 a.m., so your toddler isn't hungry at nap time.
4. **When the morning nap reaches about 11:30–12:00,** it has become the single midday nap.
5. **Bring bedtime earlier** for a few weeks. Mayo Clinic suggests moving the afternoon nap and bedtime up by about half an hour once the morning nap is gone, and Boys Town notes your child may need an earlier night or a longer morning sleep to make up for it.
6. **Expect mixed days.** Huckleberry notes that the new single nap can need at least a week before it stretches out to 2–3 hours.

## 15 month old wake windows

Neither Cleveland Clinic nor Sleep Foundation publishes wake windows past 12 months. The only figures we found are from apps:

| Source | Age band | Wake window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Napper (app example) | 14–24 months | 4–6 hours |
| Huckleberry (app example) | 15 months, one nap | 5–5.5 hours before the nap |

Treat these as loose guides. At this age, a steady nap time and bedtime usually matter more than counting minutes *(practical suggestion)*.

## Bedtime for a 15 month old

The CDC's 15-month tips describe exactly what a toddler routine needs: **a calm, quiet bedtime — put on pajamas, brush teeth, and read 1 or 2 books**. A few more pointers:

- **Keep it the same length each night.** KidsHealth warns that extras like a long back rub can turn into nightly demands.
- **Screens off** in the hour before bed, and no devices in the bedroom (KidsHealth).
- **Comfort items are fine now.** KidsHealth says a light blanket and a small lovey can go in the crib after the first birthday.
- **Expect protests when tired.** The CDC notes tantrums are more likely when toddlers are tired or hungry, which is one reason an earlier bedtime helps during the nap switch.

## Milk, bottles, and night sleep

HealthyChildren.org (AAP) recommends whole milk for 12–24 months, about 16–24 ounces a day, and suggests finishing the move from bottle to cup between 12 and 18 months, dropping the nap and bedtime bottles gradually. The CDC's 15-month tips also encourage letting your child drink from a cup without a lid. If your toddler still has a bottle to fall asleep, replace it step by step with cuddles and a story; HealthyChildren.org says giving in at bedtime can make a short problem last longer.

## Crib safety for climbers

KidsHealth suggests keeping the crib mattress at its lowest setting and not leaving toys that could be stacked to climb. If your toddler keeps climbing out even then, it may be time to think about a bed; our [2 year old sleep regression](/sleep-regression/2-years) page covers the crib-to-bed move.

## What can make the 15 month schedule harder

- Dropping the morning nap abruptly without an earlier bedtime.
- A late, long afternoon nap that pushes bedtime past 8:30 p.m. *(practical suggestion)*.
- A bottle as the last step of falling asleep (HealthyChildren.org).
- Screens close to bedtime (KidsHealth).

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Talk to your doctor if your toddler snores most nights, has pauses in breathing, seems very sleepy during the day despite enough sleep, isn't taking any steps alone, or has lost words or skills. Also mention it if night wakings have gone on for weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps should a 15 month old take?**  
One or two. Boys Town says the switch to one usually happens between 15 and 18 months.

**How do I know my toddler is ready for one nap?**  
KidsHealth: a toddler who fights the morning nap is probably ready for just an afternoon nap. Look for a pattern over 1–2 weeks.

**What time should a 15 month old go to bed?**  
Usually between about 6:30 and 8 p.m.; earlier during the nap transition.

**How long should a 15 month old's single nap be?**  
About 1–2 hours (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby), sometimes longer once the transition settles.

**How much sleep does a 15 month old need?**  
11–14 hours a day including naps (AASM; CDC).
