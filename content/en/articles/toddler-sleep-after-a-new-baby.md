# Toddler Sleep After a New Baby: Expect Disruption, Not a Named Regression

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

When a newborn arrives, the older child's bedtime often gets later, bumpier, or both. This page is that household, including the tight "two under two" version. It is not [siblings sharing a room](/siblings-sharing-a-room) (two sleep surfaces and safety). It is not [sleep after separation or divorce](/sleep-after-separation-or-divorce).

## Short answer

There is **no official "new baby sleep regression"** and no official hour of lost sleep that every toddler should lose when a sibling is born. What usually changes is the *context*: nights are noisier, one parent is in the hospital or on a night feed, daytime attention is split, and a well-rehearsed routine gets skipped. HealthyChildren.org's toilet-training advice also notes that a new baby (or an expected one) is a reason some families **put potty training on hold** — see [potty training and sleep](/potty-training-and-sleep) rather than treating night wetting as a sleep-training failure.

Keep the older child's **same wind-down and the same sleep space** when you can. If they must share a room with the newborn, use the siblings page for two firm, clear surfaces — not a sofa or a bed-sharing improvisation that breaks safe sleep for the baby.

Two-under-two logistics are real: you may need one adult on the toddler routine while the other feeds the baby, or a slightly earlier toddler bedtime so you are not running two settling jobs at once. That is household design, not a new official schedule.

## What often shows up in the older child's nights

Community threads often describe a toddler who had been sleeping in long stretches and then starts calling, climbing out, or appearing in the doorway after the baby comes home. That can be ordinary protest, a copied "someone comes when I make noise" lesson, illness in a tired household, or a bedtime that has drifted later.

- Protect a short, boring, predictable routine even if it is shorter than before.
- Avoid adding a new screen-in-the-crib habit to "keep them quiet" — see [screens and toddler sleep](/screens-and-toddler-sleep).
- If they were mid-potty-training, night dryness was already a separate skill; a new baby is a documented reason to pause. Do not punish wet nights.
- If they share a room, the newborn's cluster feeding is noisy. White noise and a consistent start, not a second essay, belong on the siblings and [white noise](/white-noise-for-baby-sleep) pages.

## Two bedtimes under one roof

A slightly earlier toddler bedtime can give you a window before the next newborn feed. It can also be too early if they still need that last bit of awake time — [what time should baby go to bed](/what-time-should-baby-go-to-bed) has no official clock, and the same is true for the older child. Move one variable. Tag-team when two adults are home. When you are alone, a safe place for the baby (bassinet, crib) while you finish the toddler's last book is safer than settling both on a sofa.

## When to get clinical advice

Call if the older child's nights changed *and* they seem unwell, if they were dry at night for months and wetting returns with pain or daytime accidents (the bedwetting page has the red flags), or if you cannot keep the baby in a safe sleep space because nights have become chaotic. If you are dangerously exhausted, ask for help for you as well. Urgent care is for a child who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a toddler sleep regression after a new baby official?**  
No. Use this page for the household change, and [toddler bedtime battles](/toddler-bedtime-battles) for stalling and climbing out.

**Should we move the toddler to a bed before the baby arrives?**  
Only if they are ready. The crib-to-bed decision stays on [crib to bed transition](/crib-to-toddler-bed). A newborn does not automatically require it.

**Can they share a room with the baby?**  
Yes, with two safe surfaces and the rules on [siblings sharing a room](/siblings-sharing-a-room). Do not put the newborn in the toddler's bed.

**Should we pause sleep training?**  
If a plan is mid-stream and the house is in upheaval, many families pause and restart later. Ask your pediatrician; this page is not a method.
