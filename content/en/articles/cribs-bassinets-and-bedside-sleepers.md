# Cribs, Bassinets, Bedside Sleepers, and Moses Baskets: How They Differ

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Choosing a first sleep space is a *types* question. This page compares cribs, mini-cribs, bassinets, bedside sleepers, and Moses baskets, and it shows where U.S. and UK advice is not worded the same way. **When** to leave a bassinet for a crib stays on [when to move baby to crib](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib). Play yards have their own types page: [pack-and-play sleep](/pack-and-play-sleep). The ABC rules stay on [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies).

## Short answer

In the United States, AAP and CPSC treat these as intended infant sleep products **when they meet their federal standard**: a **crib**, **bassinet or cradle**, **play yard**, or **bedside sleeper**. Each has its own CPSC rule. A product that is only a pretty basket, a compact travel sleeper, or a "mini-bassinet" may **not** meet those rules.

CPSC, in its bassinet and cradle standard (already cited on the move-to-crib page), says a bassinet or cradle is **not intended to be used beyond about 5 months, or when a child can push up on hands and knees**. Follow the stricter of that line and the manual (some models say to stop at the first rolls).

**U.S. and UK wording parts company on Moses baskets.** The Lullaby Trust and NHS describe a **cot or Moses basket** (or travel cot or carrycot) on a firm, flat, waterproof mattress as a recommended sleep space, and they cite British Standard **BS EN 1466** for Moses baskets and carrycots. HealthyChildren.org (29 May 2026) warns that **many mini-bassinets or baskets sold in the U.S. do not meet U.S. infant sleep safety standards**; soft sides can let a baby tip or fall, especially when a lightweight basket is placed on a counter, table, or appliance. We will not pretend those two messages are the same. If you are in the U.S., look for a product that meets the current federal **bassinet** (or crib, play yard, or bedside sleeper) standard — not a generic basket on a nightstand. If you are using UK advice, stick to a basket that meets the named British standard, on a stable stand, with a firm, flat, waterproof mattress that fits, and still never on a high, unstable surface.

Bedside sleepers that meet the federal bedside-sleeper standard are a **separate** surface attached next to the adult bed — that is still room-sharing, **not** bed-sharing. Lullaby Trust: since 2020, bedside cribs should meet **BS EN 1130:2019**, and should **not** have a side that fully drops down. Attach and use the unit exactly as the manual says.

### The types at a glance

| Sleep space | Whose standard (opened for this page) | Usual limit to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-size crib | CPSC crib standard (16 CFR part 1219) | Until the child reaches the manufacturer's height/weight line or can climb out; lower the mattress as they sit and stand. U.S. **drop-side cribs are banned**; do not use a vintage drop-side |
| Mini-crib | Non-full-size / mini-crib rules; still a rigid crib | Same clear, firm, flat idea in a smaller footprint |
| Convertible crib | Starts as a crib; later modes are in the manual | Do not skip ahead to a toddler-bed rail before the crib is outgrown — [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed) |
| Bassinet or cradle | CPSC 16 CFR part 1218 | About 5 months or pushing up on hands and knees, or the manual's earlier line |
| Bedside sleeper | CPSC bedside-sleeper standard (16 CFR part 1222); UK: BS EN 1130:2019 | A separate, approved surface beside the bed, used as designed; not a sofa gap, not an open adult bed |
| Moses basket / carrycot | UK: BS EN 1466, recommended by Lullaby Trust / NHS as a sleep space. U.S.: only if the product actually meets a federal sleep-product standard — many decorative baskets do not (HealthyChildren.org) | Stop when the baby is active enough to roll or push up, or at the manual's limit; never on a high, unstable surface |
| Play yard / pack-and-play | See [pack-and-play sleep](/pack-and-play-sleep) | Under 35 inches and cannot climb out; only the supplied mattress |

HealthyChildren.org also warns against **vintage cribs** that do not meet current U.S. standards, including the drop-side ban, and against broken cribs or missing hardware.

## How to choose without a shopping list

Pick an intended sleep product that meets the standard that applies where you live, that is intact, and that you will actually use in your room for the first months. A bassinet is for a short early window. A crib or play yard can last longer. A bedside sleeper is optional, not required, and it does not make bed-sharing "approved" — see [bed-sharing and co-sleeping](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping).

Do not use two stacked mattresses, a pillow in a basket, or a bassinet perched on a dresser "just for a nap."

## When to get advice

If a hand-me-down has no model number, no stand, or a side that drops fully, do not guess — check CPSC or, in the UK, the named British standard, and ask your pediatrician or health visitor if you are stuck. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a bedside sleeper the same as bed-sharing?**  
No. It is a separate, approved surface beside the bed, if it meets the standard and is used as designed.

**Is a Moses basket safe in the United States?**  
Only if *that* product meets a current U.S. sleep-product standard. Many baskets do not. UK advice is more welcoming of standard-meeting Moses baskets. We show both.

**Is a mini-crib enough, or do I need a full-size crib?**  
A mini-crib that meets its standard can be an intended sleep space. You may still want a larger crib later. That is a space decision, not a safety rule that "mini is always worse."

**Can I keep the bassinet for naps after we switch at night?**  
Only if the baby is still within the bassinet's developmental and printed limits. The move-to-crib page covers that timing.
