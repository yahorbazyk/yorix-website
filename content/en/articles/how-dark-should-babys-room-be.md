# How Dark Should a Baby's Room Be? Contrast Matters More Than Pitch Black

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

"Do they need blackout curtains?" is a room-setup question. This page is darkness: how dark is enough, types of coverings, and a safety issue that matters more than a perfect cave. It is not [night lights and baby sleep](/night-lights-and-baby-sleep) (whether and how to use a small light). It is not the full early-waking plan on [early morning waking](/early-morning-waking), which now points here for the room itself.

## Short answer

**There is no official AAP lux number, and no rule that every baby must sleep in pitch black.** What official sources do support is a **day–night difference**: more light and ordinary household noise by day, a dimmer, boring room at night — already the plan on [newborn day-night confusion](/newborn-day-night-confusion). A dark room can be a practical experiment when street light or an early sunrise is pulling mornings forward. It is not a medical device and not a substitute for a wind-down.

If you darken the room, **window-covering cords are the safety issue to get right first.** CPSC's cordless campaign is blunt: **cordless coverings are the option that eliminates the strangulation hazard.** Corded blinds and shades can still be dangerous even when cords are tied up. Keep cribs, play yards, and climbing furniture away from windows. This page is not a product review of curtain brands.

### Ways families darken a room (generic types)

| Approach | What it is | Safety / sleep note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cordless blackout shade or blind | A covering with no loop or pull cord | Preferable from a CPSC cord-safety point of view |
| Blackout curtain panel on a rod | Fabric that blocks much of the light | Watch long cords and tie-backs; keep the crib away from the window and from any loop |
| Temporary film or travel shade | A removable panel or film | Useful for a rental or a trip; still keep cords and loose fabric out of reach |
| A dim night light, if you use one | A low light for feeds or a toddler who fears the dark | See [night lights and baby sleep](/night-lights-and-baby-sleep) — dim, not a projector until morning |
| Doing nothing special | Ordinary curtains and a darker night | Also fine if 24-hour sleep is all right and mornings are not a light problem |

We will not invent a "must be 1% light" target. If you can no longer see the change from 4 a.m. street glow to 6 a.m. sunrise, that is the kind of practical check some sleep writers describe — it is a home experiment, not an AAP test.

## Day versus night, and naps

Newborns need the contrast plan more than they need a cave: daylight in the morning, ordinary day noise, a dimmer night. For later early waking, a darker room is Step 1 of a larger plan (schedule, consistent morning, noise) — stay on [early morning waking](/early-morning-waking) for those steps.

Daytime naps do not have an official "must be pitch black" rule either. Some families darken naps so the room matches night; some keep them slightly lighter so nights stay distinct. There is no AAP winner. If a dark nap is pushing bedtime later, you have a schedule question, not a curtain failure.

## Mistakes to skip

- Choosing a dramatic print or a long corded roman shade because it looks like a nursery catalog, then placing the crib under it.
- Adding loose blankets or a canopy over the crib "to make it darker." Darkness cannot come from something in the sleep space.
- Turning the whole house into a cave all day and wondering why days and nights have mixed (see the day-night page).

## When to get advice

Ask if a new, very dark room coincides with the baby being hard to rouse, or if you cannot tell day from night and they seem unwell. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do babies need blackout curtains?**  
No official requirement. They are a tool for light control, especially at dawn, not a must-have.

**Are blackout curtains bad for babies?**  
The covering itself is not a medical treatment. The hazard is cords, loose fabric, and a crib pushed against a window. Prefer cordless. There is no AAP warning that darkness itself is harmful.

**Should naps be as dark as night?**  
Your choice. Official sources do not specify a nap lux. Watch whether very dark day sleep is moving bedtime later.

**Can I use a regular curtain?**  
Yes. Many families do. If mornings are still a light problem, then try a darker cordless covering.
