# 5 Week Old Baby Sleep: When a Longer Stretch Slips

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 5 snapshot**. It is not [4 week old baby sleep](/4-week-old-baby-sleep), not [6 week old baby sleep](/6-week-old-baby-sleep), and not [1 month old sleep schedule](/1-month-old-sleep-schedule). If a 3- or 4-hour night stretch appeared last week and then fell apart, start here — not on the 6-week fuss page.

## Short answer

Day 35 has no unique official hour target and no clock timetable. KidsHealth still points to NSF's **14–17 hours** for the 0–3 month period. Beside that sit HealthyChildren.org's "about 16–17 hours in short pieces," the wider **11–19** band from Sleep Foundation and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby, and some NHS pages at about **8–18**. This snapshot leaves the disagreement in place.

Two landmarks sit *near* this week, not inside a special 5-week law. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby places the start of a body clock around **4–6 weeks**, so you are in that window. Period of PURPLE Crying often peaks later, around **6–8 weeks** (NCSBS). Week 5 can feel like a downhill slope toward that peak, especially if a slightly longer night you had last week has become 30- or 40-minute fragments again.

KidsHealth's phrase "sleeping through the night" at 1–3 months often means a **5–6 hour** stretch. That is a meaning of the phrase, not a guarantee at 5 weeks, and not a reason to ignore a wake-to-feed plan you were still given.

### Landmarks near day 35 — not a special total

- **Hours:** KidsHealth still points to NSF's 14–17 hours for the 0–3 month period. The 11–19 and ~8–18 figures above remain published disagreements, not a week-5 total.
- **Clock window:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's 4–6 week start means you are inside it, not finished with it.
- **Fuss:** NCSBS describes a Period of PURPLE Crying peak often around 6–8 weeks, so week 5 can already feel unsettled.
- **A "long" night:** KidsHealth's 1–3 month use of "through the night" is often a 5–6 hour stretch, not 12 hours, and not a promise this week.

## What is usually going on at five weeks

Days may be slightly more organised than week 2, or they may not. Feeding is still frequent. If weight gain is going well and your pediatrician has already said a longer stretch is all right, you may not be waking them on a 3–4 hour clock anymore. If nobody has said that, do not drop night feeds because a blog called this a growth week.

Popular sites and parent forums often mention a "5-week leap" or a first growth spurt. Wonder Weeks is not a medical schedule, and this page will not date your baby's development from it. Hungrier days can happen. Feed on cue. The 6-week snapshot covers the later evening-cluster picture; this page stays on the "the longer stretch vanished" problem.

## When last week's stretch disappears

Recurring parent-community threads describe a baby who seemed to "settle" at week 4 and then, quite suddenly, needed to be held almost around the clock. Those stories shape the headings below. They are anecdotes, not a measured length of a phase.

- **A 4-hour night stretch disappears.** After a slightly better week, they may wake every 30–60 minutes and not always want a full feed. Holding or rocking the bassinet often "works" in the moment. That is comfort, not a new official pattern, and not automatically a 5-week regression (there is no official one).
- **All-day fuss without an obvious illness.** They may look well between spells and still need almost constant contact. Rule out hunger, a dirty diaper, and the usual illness signs, then treat it as a hard week, not a training failure. If they seem unwell, call.
- **"We feel as if we have a new baby."** That phrase comes up a lot at this age. It is a feeling, not a clinical reset. The next week or two may still include the 6–8 week fuss peak — see [6 week old baby sleep](/6-week-old-baby-sleep) and [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression) when you get there.

Safe sleep does not loosen because they want to be held: back, firm, flat, clear, in your room when *you* sleep — [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies).

## A night that used to hold a longer stretch (example only)

Not a full-day timetable. Suppose they last week did a first stretch from about 10:30 p.m. to 2:30 a.m. Tonight the same 10:30 settle is followed by a waking at 11:10, another at 11:50, a feed at 12:20 a.m. that does not produce a long doze, and then 40-minute fragments until a slightly longer sleep after 4:00. The next night may look like last week again. The sketch is only to show how a "lost" stretch can look, not to set an alarm.

## If the change looks like illness, do not wait

A stretch that vanishes is often an ordinary hard week. It is *not* ordinary if they stay sleepy and hard to rouse, if feeding or wet-diaper counts fall, or if fever, odd breathing, or a colour change appears. A sudden change plus illness signs is a same-day clinical question, not a "wait for a leap to end" plan. For a baby who cannot be woken or is struggling to breathe, use emergency care.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a 5 week sleep regression?**
No official one. A better stretch can slip, and fuss can rise as you approach the later 6–8 week crying peak. See [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression) for that neighbouring name; do not start a training plan this week.

**My 5 week old was doing 4 hours and now wakes every 30 minutes. Is that normal?**
It is a common complaint at this age. It is not a medical statistic. Check feeding, diapers, and whether they seem unwell, and ask your pediatrician if the change is sudden and you are worried.

**Is week 5 a time to start sleep training?**
No. Sleep Foundation usually places formal training later, toward 4–6 months. The policy page is [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).
