# 10 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Two Naps, New Skills, and Night Waking

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A 10-month-old is a busy person. Crawling, cruising along the sofa, pulling up on anything with an edge: all of it is exciting, and all of it can spill over into sleep. Many babies this age have a dependable two-nap day, yet still wake at night, stand up in the crib and can't work out how to lie back down, or cry the moment a parent leaves the room. Here is what the sources say is normal, plus an example day and ways to handle the common snags.

## Short answer

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (endorsed by the AAP) recommends **12–16 hours of total sleep per 24 hours for babies 4–12 months**. KidsHealth describes babies of 8–12 months sleeping **9–12 hours at night** and usually taking **two naps**, each lasting anywhere from **30 minutes to 2 hours**. Most babies are past the third nap by now: Mayo Clinic puts its usual drop at about 9 months. Night waking can come back around this age. Two NHS services describe more frequent waking again at **8–9 months**, often linked with new motor skills and separation anxiety.

## How much should a 10 month old sleep?

Sources broadly agree on the total but frame it differently:

- **AASM:** 12–16 hours in 24 hours, naps included.
- **KidsHealth:** 12–16 hours, including a 9–12 hour night stretch.
- **Pregnancy, Birth and Baby:** 11–16 hours in total for 6–12 months, with 10–14 hours at night.
- **Sleep Foundation:** by about 9 months, babies are generally sleeping 8–10 hours at night in addition to naps.

The previous version of this page gave a total of "13–15 hours". We could not match that figure to a primary source, so it has been removed.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry suggests about 13.5 hours at 10 months, with 11–12 hours at night and 2–3 hours across two naps. Napper describes two naps of about 1–1.5 hours each.

## Example 2-nap day for a 10 month old

**Example only.** Your baby's best times depend on when they wake, how long the naps last, and how your family day runs.

| 10:00–11:15 a.m. | Morning nap | About 3 hours after waking |

This day gives about 2.5 hours of naps. The awake periods of 3–3.5 hours sit inside Cleveland Clinic's ranges for this age.

## How many naps at 10 months?

Two naps is the norm. Mayo Clinic suggests a morning and an early-afternoon nap for babies from 4 months to a year, says the third nap is usually dropped by about 9 months, and notes that some babies give up the morning nap at 10–12 months. Boys Town National Research Hospital places the move from two naps to one later, at **15–18 months**. Sources disagree, so if your 10-month-old resists the morning nap for a few days, it may be a short phase rather than a real transition.

Signs that one long midday nap might be worth a trial:

- The morning nap gets later and later, pushing the afternoon one toward evening.
- Your baby plays happily through the second nap opportunity on most days for two weeks or more.
- Bedtime becomes a long battle even though the day looks well spaced.

Before switching, try moving the first nap a bit later instead. For the full picture, see our [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age).

## 10 month old wake windows

Ten months is where two clinical bands meet. Cleveland Clinic lists **2.5–4.5 hours** for 7–10 months and **3–6 hours** for 10–12 months; Sleep Foundation's matching bands are **1.5–6 hours** and **3–7.5 hours**. The two app examples are much narrower: Huckleberry gives **3–3.75 hours** at 10 months and Napper about **3–4 hours**.

For a two-nap day, that usually means something like 3 hours before the morning nap, a little longer before the afternoon one, and the longest stretch before bed *(practical suggestion)*. If the morning nap starts getting refused, a slightly longer first window is often worth trying before you think about one nap. None of these numbers come from the AAP or AASM, so treat them as a starting point. The [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) page shows the full chart.

## Standing in the crib at night

Tresillian notes that when babies start moving around more or standing up in the cot, sleep can be disturbed and they may wake more. Many babies this age can pull up but not yet get down again.

What can help:

1. **Practice during the day.** Show your baby how to bend their knees and sit down from standing, using a low table or the crib rail.
2. **At night, guide rather than entertain.** Tresillian suggests using a calm tone with simple words like "time to lie down" and then showing or guiding them down.
3. **Keep the response short and the same each time.** Lay them down, say the same phrase, and leave.
4. **Check the crib.** KidsHealth advises removing hazards your baby could reach from a seated or standing position, such as mobiles, wall hangings, curtains, and blind cords.

If they have started climbing *out* of the crib, even with the mattress at its lowest setting, the safety question changes: see [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed). Lowering the mattress and clearing cords still comes first for a baby who can stand but cannot get out. A slightly richer "first time they pull up" version of this advice also sits on the [8 month old sleep schedule](/8-month-old-sleep-schedule).

## Separation anxiety and bedtime

KidsHealth explains that separation anxiety usually appears between 8 months and 1 year, as babies understand that a parent who leaves still exists somewhere else. A baby who slept well may begin calling out or crying in the night and calm down as soon as a parent appears.

Approaches KidsHealth suggests:

- **A short, warm exit ritual.** Say goodbye in a pleasant, loving, and firm way, and keep it brief.
- **A quiet night response.** Reassure your baby softly that you are there, give a soothing pat, then leave quickly. Long cuddles, feeds, or chatting may encourage more waking.
- **Avoid big changes if you can.** KidsHealth suggests not starting childcare with an unfamiliar person during this window, where possible.

## Night waking at 10 months

Newcastle Hospitals NHS says it is normal for some babies to start waking more often again at around 8–9 months, and Derbyshire NHS describes the same pattern. Sleep Foundation's wake-windows page notes that sleep disturbances may be more common around 9 months.

Before deciding what to change, rule out the basics: illness, teething discomfort, hunger, a room that is too warm or cold, or a nap that ended too close to bedtime. If the waking persists and your baby is otherwise well, a consistent settling plan usually helps. Our [sleep training methods guide](/sleep-training-methods-explained) compares the main options.

## What can make it worse

- **A morning nap dropped too early,** leading to an overtired afternoon and a hard bedtime.
- **An afternoon nap that ends late,** leaving too little awake time before bed.
- **Changing responses each night,** which makes it harder for your baby to know what to expect.
- **A long, playful night visit** after every cry.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Contact your pediatrician if your baby snores loudly, pauses in breathing, or breathes with effort during sleep; seems unwell or has a fever; is eating much less; or if night waking is leaving you exhausted and unable to cope.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps does a 10 month old need?**  
Usually two, each between 30 minutes and 2 hours (KidsHealth).

**What is a good bedtime for a 10 month old?**  
There is no single correct time. Choose one that leaves about as much awake time after the last nap as your baby manages earlier in the day.

**Why is my 10 month old suddenly waking at night?**  
Common reasons include new skills such as standing, separation anxiety, illness, and schedule changes. NHS services describe more waking at 8–9 months as normal for some babies.

**My 10 month old stands up in the crib and cries. What should I do?**  
Practice sitting down during the day, then at night calmly lay them down with the same short phrase (Tresillian).

**Is my 10 month old ready for one nap?**  
Most babies are not. Mayo Clinic says the morning nap is often dropped at 10–12 months, while Boys Town gives 15–18 months.

**My 8, 9, 10, or 12 month old won’t nap. Does that mean they should drop a nap?**
Not automatically. Use this page and the matching month schedule. At this age a two-nap day is still common; dropping to one nap too early can look like “they won’t nap.” See [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age) and, when the one-nap transition is actually close, [when do toddlers stop napping](/when-do-toddlers-stop-napping).

**Is it normal for a 10 month old to sleep 12 hours?**
Some babies at that age do have a long night; others do not. There is no official “must sleep 12 hours” target. Use the 24-hour bands on this page (they do not all agree — we do not average them) and the matching month schedule.
