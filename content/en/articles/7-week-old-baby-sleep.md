# 7 Week Old Baby Sleep: Almost Two Months, Still No Timetable

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 7 snapshot**. It is not [6 week old baby sleep](/6-week-old-baby-sleep), not [8 week old baby sleep](/8-week-old-baby-sleep), and not [2 month old sleep schedule](/2-month-old-sleep-schedule). If you are hoping "almost 2 months" means a pattern has arrived, read this page first.

## Short answer

Day 49 still has no unique official hour target. You remain in the 0–3 month period KidsHealth ties to NSF's **14–17 hours**. HealthyChildren.org's "about 16–17 in pieces," the **11–19** band, and some NHS pages at about **8–18** are still the other published options. This snapshot will not invent a week-7 number, and it will not borrow Sleep Foundation's 8–9 week melatonin/cortisol landmark a week early.

What is *usually in play* this week is the late side of the 6–8 week crying peak described by NCSBS, plus short daytime pieces that parents often label a "7 week nap regression." Sleep Foundation places a melatonin/cortisol rhythm shift around **8–9 weeks**, so this page will not claim that the clock has already finished its job.

Some babies offer a 5–7 hour night stretch; KidsHealth notes that at 1–3 months "sleeping through the night" often means **about 5–6 hours**. That can appear and disappear. It is not a 2-month schedule.

### What is in play at day 49

- **Hours:** the 0–3 month NSF 14–17 hour figure (KidsHealth) still applies. The other published newborn-month ranges earlier on this page are conflicts, not a week-7 official total.
- **Fuss peak:** NCSBS describes a crying peak often around 6–8 weeks, so week 7 can still be inside that window.
- **Clock landmark:** Sleep Foundation's melatonin/cortisol rhythm sits around 8–9 weeks — not claimed as a week-7 event.
- **A longer night:** KidsHealth's 1–3 month use of "through the night" is often 5–6 hours. A 7-hour stretch can appear; it is not a 2-month timetable.

## What is usually going on at seven weeks

You are close to the two-month visit in many calendars. That check-up is a good moment for feeding and development questions; it is not a signal that a timetable has arrived.

Feeds are often still every few hours in the day. If a longer night stretch has started, ask whether your pediatrician still wants a planned night feed. Do not drop calories because an app predicted a 7-hour stretch.

Evenings can still be the hardest part of the day. The longer cluster-feeding and witching-hour essays stay on those pages; this snapshot only notes that the 6–8 week crying window may not have finished.

## Almost two months, still scraps

Parent communities talk about a "7 week nap regression," about one hopeful long night, and about a partner already back at work while days are still scraps. We use those recurring situations to choose examples. Forum posts are not clinical measurements.

- **Naps stay at about 30 minutes, even if nights improved a little.** A slightly longer night does not automatically lengthen the bassinet nap. See [short naps](/short-naps) and, next week, [8 week old short naps](/8-week-old-short-naps). This is not a reason to start formal training.
- **One 6- or 7-hour night, then a broken night.** Protecting that one good night with a rigid new "schedule" usually backfires at this age. Treat it as a possible stretch, not a skill you must now defend.
- **"Almost 2 months — why is there still no pattern?"** Because official sources still describe pieces, not a clock day. The month view arrives on [2 month old sleep schedule](/2-month-old-sleep-schedule). Week 7 is not that page.
- **Evenings still peak.** If you are in the 6–8 week crying window, the 6-week pages remain the right place for that longer explanation.

Safe sleep is unchanged. Room-sharing, back, firm, flat, clear.

## A hopeful morning that slips at nap two (example only)

Not the 2-month sample day. Suppose a first waking around 6:45 a.m., a full feed, an easy 35-minute bassinet nap at 8:00, a content stretch, and then a second nap at 10:15 that lasts 12 minutes, followed by a fussy hold, a short carrier doze, and a lunchtime feed that does not produce a third settle until mid-afternoon. The "first nap worked, the second did not" shape is the point of this sketch. Tomorrow may invert it.

## The two-month visit, and when to call sooner

If a two-month check is coming, take night-feed questions and any smile or development questions there. Get advice before the visit if they stay drowsy and will not feed well, if wet-diaper counts fall, or if breathing looks laboured. A baby who cannot be woken, or who is fighting for air, needs emergency care rather than a booked appointment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a 7 week nap regression?**
No official one. Short day pieces are still typical. The neighbouring 6- and 8-week pages cover the fuss peak and the short-nap snapshot.

**Can a 7 week old sleep 7 hours at night?**
Some do, some nights. KidsHealth's 5–6 hour meaning of "through the night" at 1–3 months is the sourced phrase. Ask whether you still need to wake them for a feed.

**Is "almost 2 months" a training start date?**
No. Sleep Foundation usually places formal training later, toward 4–6 months. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).
