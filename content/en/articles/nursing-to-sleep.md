# Nursing to Sleep: When It Is Normal, and What to Change Later

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Falling asleep at the breast or bottle is one of the most common ways young babies drift off. This page is about **that specific habit**: when it is expected, how it differs from a [dream feed](/dream-feed) and from [night weaning](/night-weaning), the dental rule once teeth are present, and how to change it later if you want to. The general theory of habits — why the way a baby falls asleep is the way they want to fall back asleep — is already on [sleep associations](/sleep-associations). We will not retell that whole change plan here.

## Short answer

In the newborn months, feeding to sleep is normal. Newborns fall asleep at the breast or bottle because feeding is frequent, tiring, and comforting. You do not need to "break" this in the first weeks.

A **dream feed** is a different practice: you offer a feed to a baby who is already asleep, or barely roused, usually in the late evening. This page is about the baby who **falls asleep while feeding**, at the start of a nap or night or during a waking.

Once teeth are in, there is a dental issue as well as a sleep issue. HealthyChildren says baby-bottle tooth decay is most common when babies are put to bed with a bottle of formula or juice, and that **breastfeeding infants who fall asleep with unswallowed milk still in the mouth are also vulnerable**. The same page says: **remove your breast from your baby's mouth once they have fallen asleep**. After teeth appear, wipe or brush before the long night sleep when you can. A bedtime bottle, if you still use one, should not be milk, formula, or juice left in the mouth all night — water is the liquid that does not bathe teeth in sugar (HealthyChildren, early-childhood-caries advice).

If the baby falls asleep in your bed while nursing, move them to their own firm, flat surface on the back when you can do so safely. See [bed-sharing and co-sleeping](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping).

## Why it becomes a puzzle after the newborn period

HealthyChildren's "Getting Your Baby to Sleep" advice, aimed at babies about 4 months and older, is to put them down **drowsy**, not already asleep, if you want them to practice falling asleep in their own sleep space. If the last step is always a full feed to unconsciousness, many babies will look for that same step after a normal night waking. That is an association, not a moral failure, and it is very common.

You can keep nursing or offering a bottle at bedtime and still change *how far* into sleep the feed goes: finish the feed while the baby is sleepy but still aware, burp, then place them in the crib or bassinet. That is the same idea as the gentle change plan on the associations page, applied to this one habit.

Night calories are a separate question. A baby who still needs a feed for growth should get it. Nursing to sleep and needing a night feed can look the same at 3 a.m.; your pediatrician can help you tell comfort from nutrition. [Night weaning](/night-weaning) is optional and is a medical conversation first.

## The dental rule, once teeth appear

Cavities can form when teeth sit in milk, formula, or juice for a long time (HealthyChildren). The upper front teeth are often affected first; early signs can be white spots near the gum line. HealthyChildren notes that damage is often not obvious until after the first birthday.

Practical steps from that guidance and from "Breastfeeding After Your Baby Gets Teeth":

- Do not send a baby or toddler to bed with a bottle or sippy cup of milk, formula, or juice.
- If they fall asleep nursing, take them off the breast once they are asleep, rather than leaving milk pooled in the mouth.
- Wipe gums even before teeth; once teeth are through, clean them before the night sleep. For children under 3, HealthyChildren describes a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste.
- Do not share spoons or "clean" a pacifier in your own mouth — cavity-causing bacteria can be passed in saliva (HealthyChildren).

This is not an argument against breastfeeding, including at night, in the early months. It is a hygiene step once teeth are present, and a reason not to use a bottle of anything but water as a sleep prop.

## A slow change, if you decide you want one

Wait until feeding is going well and, if you are planning formal [gentle sleep training](/gentle-sleep-training), until the age and health checks on the readiness page make sense. Then, for a week or two, try:

- Moving the last full feed slightly earlier in the bedtime routine, so it is not the final event.
- Ending the feed while the baby is still awake enough to notice the crib.
- Offering other calm cues — a dim room, a short song, a hand on the chest — that are not a second secret feed.

If nights unravel, pause and check whether a feed is still needed, whether an illness or teething stretch is in the way, or whether the plan is simply too fast. There is no prize for rushing this in a baby who is still very young.

## When to talk to your pediatrician or dentist

Ask if you are unsure whether night feeds are still required, if weight gain is a concern, if you see white spots or brown areas on the teeth, or if feeding to sleep is the only way your baby will sleep *and* you are not coping. Early dental visits are something to raise at well-child checks (HealthyChildren).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it bad to nurse a newborn to sleep?**  
No. It is expected. The "drowsy but not asleep" advice is aimed at older babies (HealthyChildren discusses it from about 4 months).

**Is nursing to sleep the same as a dream feed?**  
No. A dream feed is you offering milk to a baby who is already asleep. This page is the baby falling asleep *during* a feed.

**Can breast milk still cause cavities?**  
HealthyChildren says breastfeeding infants who fall asleep with unswallowed milk in the mouth can be vulnerable once teeth are present, and it advises taking the baby off the breast after they fall asleep.

**What about a bedtime bottle?**  
Do not leave milk, formula, or juice in a bottle at the sleep space. If a bottle is part of bedtime, finish it, clean the teeth when they have come in, and put the baby down without the bottle in the crib.
