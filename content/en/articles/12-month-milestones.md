# 12 Month Milestones: What Most 1-Year-Olds Can Do

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your child's development, hearing, vision, or feeding, contact your pediatrician.*

The first birthday brings a flood of comparisons: one cousin is walking, another is saying five words, yours is cruising along the sofa and saying "da" for everything. This page sets out what CDC expects most children to do by 12 months, separates that from what often comes a few months later (independent walking, more words), and explains when it is worth asking your pediatrician. For naps and bedtime, see our [12 month old sleep schedule](/12-month-old-sleep-schedule).

## Short answer

By their first birthday, most babies **play games like pat-a-cake, wave "bye-bye", call a parent "mama", "dada", or another special name, understand "no" (pause or stop when you say it), put an object into a container, look for something they saw you hide, pull up to stand, walk while holding on to furniture, drink from an open cup you hold, and pick up small things between thumb and pointer finger.** This is CDC's 2022 checklist, which includes skills that **at least 75% of children** have by 12 months. **Walking alone isn't on the 1-year list**; taking a few steps without help appears on the **15-month** list.

| Area | Most children by 12 months… |
| --- | --- |
| Social and emotional | Join in simple games with you, such as pat-a-cake |
| Language and communication | Wave goodbye; use "mama", "dada", or another special name for a parent; understand "no" and briefly pause or stop |

## Walking: what's normal at 1 year

Most parents' first question at this age. On CDC's lists:

- **By 12 months:** pulls up to stand and walks holding on to furniture ("cruising").
- **By 15 months:** takes a few steps alone.

So a 1-year-old who cruises but doesn't yet walk alone is within the checklist. Reasonable ways to help, from CDC's tips: give your child safe furniture to pull up on, let them hold your hands to practice, and offer sturdy push toys or even an empty box to push. **Baby walkers aren't recommended** (CDC).

## Talking: first words and understanding

At 12 months the CDC list focuses on **communication**, not vocabulary:

- using "mama", "dada", or a special name for a specific parent;
- waving bye-bye;
- understanding "no".

One or two extra words (often half-words, like "ba" for ball) appear on the **15-month** list, along with pointing to ask for something and following a simple instruction given with a gesture. CDC's advice for building language: answer when your child points ("You want the cup? Here's your cup"), and expand what they say ("ta" → "Yes, a truck").

## Hands and eating

The neat pincer grasp (thumb and pointer finger) is a 1-year milestone, and it makes self-feeding much easier. CDC suggests:

- foods of different textures: smooth, mashed, or finely chopped, offered again and again, since many babies need several tries to accept a new food;
- letting your child feed themselves with their fingers while you sit with them, and practicing with a cup that has a little water in it;
- water, breast milk, or plain milk to drink; no juice is needed, and if you give it, no more than 4 ounces a day of 100% juice; no sodas, fruit drinks, or flavored milk.

Learn which foods are choking hazards before offering finger foods; see our [baby-led weaning](/baby-led-weaning) guide.

## Teeth and the first dental visit

Teeth aren't milestones, and the timing varies widely. The AAP says your child should see a dentist within 6 months of the first tooth appearing and **no later than 12 months**, whichever comes first, and that you should brush with a rice-grain-sized smear of fluoride toothpaste from the first tooth. If your child has no teeth yet at 1 year, the AAP says the timing can be hereditary and usually doesn't mean anything is wrong; ask at the checkup if you're unsure. More in our [teething symptoms](/teething-symptoms) guide.

## Play ideas for a 1-year-old

From CDC's tips for this age:

- **Narrate and sing** what you're doing: "We're washing your hands."
- **Point things out** (trucks, birds, the dog) so your child learns to follow your attention.
- **Books with flaps and textures**; "reading" can just mean talking about the pictures.
- **Blocks, cups, and containers** to fill, stack, and dump.
- **Pots, pans, and a drum** to make noise.
- **Teach wanted behaviors** with praise and hugs; redirect quickly instead of long explanations, and save a firm "no" for danger. CDC advises against spanking and yelling.
- **Comfort routines**: a familiar toy or blanket when meeting a new caregiver.

CDC continues to advise no screen time other than video calls before age 2.

## Babies born early

The AAP recommends using **corrected age** until age 2. A child who turns 1 but was born 6 weeks early is compared with what a baby about 10½ months old would do.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask about developmental screening, or call your state's early intervention program (free evaluation for children under 3 in the U.S.; cdc.gov/FindEI), if:

- by 12 months (corrected age) your child isn't doing one or more items on the checklist, for example **doesn't pull to stand, doesn't wave, doesn't use a name for a parent, or doesn't look for hidden objects**;
- by 15 months your child isn't taking a few steps alone (CDC 15-month checklist);
- your child **loses** words, gestures, or other skills they had;
- your child doesn't respond to their name or to sounds;
- you're worried for any reason. CDC's message is "don't wait".

The AAP recommends standardized developmental screening at 9, 18, and 30 months and autism screening at 18 and 24 months, but a screening can be done at any visit when there's a concern.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a 1 year old be doing?**  
Most can pull up to stand, cruise along furniture, pick up small bits of food with a pincer grasp, wave bye-bye, use "mama" or "dada" for a parent, understand "no", play pat-a-cake, and find a toy you hid.

**Is it normal if my 1 year old isn't walking?**  
Yes, if they pull to stand and cruise. Walking a few steps alone is on CDC's 15-month checklist. Talk to your pediatrician if your child isn't pulling to stand by 12 months or isn't taking steps alone by 15 months.

**How many words should a 12 month old say?**  
CDC's 1-year list includes a special name for a parent ("mama", "dada"). One or two other words are expected by 15 months.

**Should my 1 year old use a walker?**  
CDC says baby walkers are not recommended. Push toys and holding hands are safer ways to practice.

**When should a baby have the first dental visit?**  
Within 6 months of the first tooth, and no later than the first birthday (AAP).
