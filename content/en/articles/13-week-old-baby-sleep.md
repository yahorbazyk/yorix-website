# 13 Week Old Baby Sleep: Just Past 3 Months, Between Two Bands

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 13 snapshot**. It is not [12 week old baby sleep](/12-week-old-baby-sleep), not [14 week old baby sleep](/14-week-old-baby-sleep), not [3 month old sleep schedule](/3-month-old-sleep-schedule), and not [4 month old sleep schedule](/4-month-old-sleep-schedule). Week 13 is about seven days past the usual "3 months = 12 weeks" label. We will not copy either month timetable.

## Short answer

Official labels do not land neatly on day 91, and this snapshot will say so instead of inventing a 13-week total. KidsHealth's NSF figure of **14–17 hours** is tagged **0–3 months**. AASM's **12–16 hours** is tagged **from 4 months** (about 16 weeks). Week 13 sits in the gap between those labels. Both neighbouring ranges are listed. They are not blended, and AASM is not treated as already in force.

"Sleeping through the night" in the 1–3 month language (KidsHealth) often still means a **5–6 hour** stretch. Some babies have been offering that stretch and then, this week, lose the first half of the night. That is a common community complaint at 13–14 weeks, and it is also how some families first meet what later gets called a 4-month change. The full 4-month regression essay stays on [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months). The 3-month named discussion stays on [sleep regression at 3 months](/sleep-regression/3-months).

### Why week 13 has two neighbouring hour bands, not one official total

- **The older label:** NSF 14–17 hours at 0–3 months, as quoted by KidsHealth. Week 13 is just past that usual "3 months = 12 weeks" mark.
- **The next label:** AASM 12–16 hours from 4 months (about 16 weeks). It has not started yet.
- **A long night:** KidsHealth's 1–3 month use of "through the night" is often 5–6 hours — which is exactly the first stretch some families say has just broken.
- **Training:** still early relative to Sleep Foundation's usual 4–6 month window.
- **Fuss:** Period of PURPLE Crying often eases by 3–4 months (NCSBS), which may or may not match your evenings.

## What is usually going on at thirteen weeks

The three-month visit may just have happened. Naps can look worse than they did at week 10, even if nights were recently the "easy" part — or the opposite: days will not start, while the first night stretch that used to last until 3 or 4 a.m. now breaks around midnight. Rolling may be starting for a few babies. If they can roll, stop swaddling and keep the crib clear; the 4-month page covers rolling and swaddles in more detail.

Feeds may be stretching in the day. Do not drop a night feed only because the calendar says "past 3 months."

## When the first stretch breaks early

What to Expect threads at 13–14 weeks often describe a first stretch that used to last about 6 hours and now ends with a hard waking around 12:30 or 1:00 a.m., or a baby who "will not sleep during the day" after a calmer 3-month mark. Those recurring reports shaped the examples. They do not measure how long a wobble lasts.

- **The first stretch breaks earlier.** A 6-hour opening to the night becomes a 3-hour one, sometimes with a second-wind wide-awake spell. This can be an early 4-month-style change, a short last nap, or an ordinary wobble. Watch a week before you rebuild the whole day.
- **Days fall apart while nights were the strong part — or the reverse.** Community posts split both ways. Neither pattern has an official week-13 name. For daytime scraps, see [short naps](/short-naps) and [baby won't nap](/baby-wont-nap).
- **"Is this the 4-month regression at 13 weeks?"** Some babies show that kind of change before 16 weeks. Use the 4-month regression page for the explanation; stay on this snapshot for the week you are in. Do not start a formal training plan only because the word "regression" appeared in a search.

Safe sleep: back, firm, flat, clear. Room-sharing is still recommended in the first months (AAP).

## A broken first stretch (example only)

Not the 3-month or 4-month sample day. Suppose bedtime is around 8:40 p.m. after a last feed, a stretch that last week lasted until 3:00 a.m. now ends with a hard waking at 12:50, a feed does not bring a second long doze, and they are then up again at 2:40 and 5:10. Daytime may be a string of 20–35 minute naps that need a hold to continue. Tomorrow the first stretch may look like last week again. The sketch is the broken opening, not a timetable.

## Before you drop a feed, and if the change looks unwell

Ask at a routine visit before you drop a night feed. Get same-day advice if a sudden sleep change arrives with illness signs, poor feeding, fewer wet diapers, odd breathing, or a colour change. If they cannot be roused or cannot breathe comfortably, go to emergency care.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a 13 week sleep regression?**
No official week-13 one. The named discussions are [sleep regression at 3 months](/sleep-regression/3-months) and [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months). A broken first stretch at 13 weeks can be an early version of the later change, or a brief wobble.

**Why won't my 13 week old sleep during the day?**
Short or missing day sleep is a common complaint just past 3 months. See [baby won't nap](/baby-wont-nap). It is not a reason to copy the 4-month timetable onto this week.

**Is week 13 early for formal training?**
Yes, relative to Sleep Foundation's usual 4–6 month window. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).
