# Startle Reflex and Sleep: A Wake-Up, Not a Training Problem

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A baby who was heavy in your arms can fling their arms out and cry the moment their back meets the mattress. Parents name this the startle or Moro reflex. This page is that waking. It is not a second [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby) essay — that page already covers hip-healthy wrapping and **when to stop**. It is not [sleep sacks](/sleep-sacks).

## Short answer

The Moro reflex is a **normal newborn reflex**: a sudden change in head position, a sense of falling, or an abrupt sound can make the arms and legs fling out and then come back in, often with a cry (HealthyChildren.org; KidsHealth). It can interrupt a transfer into the bassinet or a light stretch of sleep.

**Sources do not agree on when it disappears, and we will not average them.** HealthyChildren.org's reflex table lists the Moro reflex as disappearing at **2 months**, and the text says it usually peaks in the first month and **starts to disappear after about two months**. Nemours KidsHealth says these early reflexes gradually disappear, **usually by 3–6 months**. MedlinePlus says the Moro reflex **normally goes away after 3 or 4 months**. Those figures sit next to each other.

If they are starting to roll, **stop swaddling now**, even if startles are still happening. Arms need to be free so they can change position. The swaddle page and AAP safe-sleep rules win over "but the startle is worse without it."

### Timing conflict at a glance

| Source | What it says about the Moro / startle reflex |
| --- | --- |
| HealthyChildren.org (AAP) | Table: disappears at 2 months. Text: peaks in the first month, starts to disappear after about two months |
| Nemours KidsHealth | Early reflexes, including Moro, usually gone by 3–6 months |
| MedlinePlus | Normally goes away after 3 or 4 months |

Your pediatrician, not a blended average, interprets an unusually strong, one-sided, or late reflex.

## Why a startle shows up at sleep times

Lowering them onto a flat surface can feel, to the reflex, like a sudden head drop. Their own arm fling in light sleep can set it off again. That is a reflex, not a proof that the crib is "wrong" or that you missed a training window.

A slower, more horizontal lower — body on the mattress before you take your hands away — is a common practical step. It is a transfer trick, not a medical protocol.

Swaddling is sometimes used in the early weeks to limit that arm fling. It has strict safety rules (hips, back sleeping, no weighted wraps, stop at rolling) on the swaddle page. A wearable blanket with **arms free** is the usual next step, not a tighter wrap.

## When the swaddle ends and the startle has not

Community threads often describe stopping the swaddle because rolling started — or because a 4- or 5-month-old "felt too old" — and then facing shorter naps and more night wakes with flying arms.

- **The startle can outlast the swaddle.** That can happen if you read HealthyChildren.org's earlier fade and KidsHealth's later window as a range, not a promise. Do not re-swaddle a rolling baby to "wait out" the reflex.
- **"They still startle at 5 months."** That may still be in KidsHealth's 3–6 month band, or it may be ordinary twitching in active sleep, or it may be worth mentioning at a visit — especially if it is one-sided, very late, or they seem otherwise delayed. MedlinePlus: a Moro reflex in an older infant is abnormal and should be discussed. We will not pick a single "too late" day from conflicting tables.
- **Scratching their face.** Nails, not a tighter swaddle, are the first practical step once arms are out.

## When the reflex itself is the question

Mention a startle that is much stronger on one side, a reflex that disappeared and then returned, movements that come in stiff clusters rather than a single fling, or a startle that is still dramatic well past the ages in the table above. The well-child exam already checks reflexes. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**When do babies outgrow the startle reflex?**  
Sources disagree (about 2 months on HealthyChildren.org's table; 3–4 months on MedlinePlus; usually by 3–6 months on KidsHealth). Do not treat any one of those as a deadline you failed.

**Can I keep swaddling until the startle stops?**  
Not if they can roll, or are close. Stop for safety and move to an arms-free sack. See [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby).

**Is a sleep sack enough to stop the startle?**  
A sack is not a swaddle. It can replace loose blankets. It will not erase the reflex. See [sleep sacks](/sleep-sacks).

**Is this the same as twitching in sleep?**  
Not always. Twitching in active sleep is a different, usually quieter picture — [baby twitching in sleep](/baby-twitching-in-sleep). A Moro is a sudden fling, often with a cry.
