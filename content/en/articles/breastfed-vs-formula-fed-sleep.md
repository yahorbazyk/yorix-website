# Breastfed vs Formula-Fed Sleep: A Comparison, Not a Reason to Switch

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Parents often hear that "formula babies sleep longer" and wonder whether a switch — or a bit of combo feeding — would buy a longer night. This page is that comparison. It is not a second [baby feeding schedule](/baby-feeding-schedule) (how often and how much), and it is not [night weaning](/night-weaning) (whether and how to drop a night feed). Those pages keep their charts and how-tos.

## Short answer

**Sources do not all say the same thing, and we will not average them.**

HealthyChildren.org (Rachel Y. Moon, MD, FAAP) addresses the "my sister's formula-fed baby slept through at three months" comparison directly: **in the first few months, breastfed babies wake more often at night to feed** (and also feed more often during the day). Human milk is easier to digest than formula, so breastfed babies **usually wake hungry every two to three hours**, relatively more often than bottle-fed babies. The same piece says that **by about six months, breastfed babies start sleeping longer.**

HealthyChildren.org's formula-feeding advice uses a different clock, about *night feeds* rather than a promise of unbroken sleep: **between 2 and 4 months of age, or when the baby weighs more than 12 lb (5.4 kg), most formula-fed babies no longer need a middle-of-the-night feed.** That is already cited on our night-weaning page. It is not the same as "they sleep through."

The Australian Breastfeeding Association, on baby sleep patterns, states there is **no evidence that breastfed and formula-fed babies differ in when they start sleeping through the night.** That claim sits next to the HealthyChildren.org sentences above. We report both.

HealthyChildren.org also says **feeding human milk for at least 2 months reduces the risk of SIDS by at least 50%**, and that this may be partly because infants who consume only human milk may **wake more easily and often** — frequent awakenings that can be protective. The AAP recommends exclusive human milk feeding for about the first 6 months when it is possible. That is not a reason to shame a family who uses formula. It is a reason **not to switch milk type solely "so they will sleep longer"** as if longer stretch were an automatic safety upgrade.

Combo or mixed feeding is a family and feeding decision, not an official AAP "sleep recipe." There is no official combo-feed timetable that this page will invent.

## Do not switch milk type just to buy a longer night

If you are considering formula, or more formula, because nights are hard, take that question to your pediatrician as a *feeding* question — weight gain, supply, your health, your preferences — not as a sleep-training method. Night length still has to fit [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby) in the early weeks, and "through the night" at 1–3 months often means about 5–6 hours (KidsHealth), already discussed on [when do babies sleep through the night](/when-do-babies-sleep-through-the-night).

Combo feeding can be a practical middle for some families. It does not come with an official "add one bottle at 10 p.m. and they will sleep until six" protocol. If a combo plan is changing supply or weight gain, that is a clinician conversation, not a blog schedule.

The Moon article also offers two sleep-habit tips (waiting a moment before you pick up, and trying to finish a feed before they are fully asleep). Those are suggestions on that page, not a second Yorix method. If the last feed is also the last wake, see [nursing to sleep](/nursing-to-sleep) and [sleep associations](/sleep-associations).

Safe sleep does not change with the type of milk: back, firm, flat, clear, in your room for at least the first 6 months. A bottle is never left in the crib to "help them sleep" — [night weaning](/night-weaning) already covers the dental rule.

## When the comparison is the wrong tool

Premature babies often wake more and feed more, regardless of milk type — see [preemie sleep](/preemie-sleep). A baby who is not gaining, who is very hard to rouse, or who seems unwell needs a call, not a formula experiment. If you have already dropped a feed and they still wake, that is the second half of the night-weaning page, not a proof that you "chose the wrong milk."

## When to get clinical advice

Ask before you change milk type if the only goal is longer sleep, if weight gain is a concern, or if you are mixing feeds and cannot tell whether nights are hunger. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do formula-fed babies sleep better or longer?**  
Some HealthyChildren.org pages describe fewer early-month night *feeds* on formula, and more frequent early-month waking on breast milk. The Australian Breastfeeding Association says there is no evidence the two groups differ in when they start *sleeping through*. We do not pick a winner.

**Should I switch to formula so my baby will sleep through the night?**  
Not as a sleep protocol. Talk with your pediatrician if you are considering a change for feeding or health reasons.

**Does combo feeding help them sleep?**  
It can change how nights are shared. It is not an official AAP sleep method, and it can affect supply. There is no combo timetable on this page.

**Does that mean breastfed nights will always be worse?**  
No. The Moon piece says that by about six months, breastfed babies start sleeping longer. Individual babies vary at every age. Use [how much sleep does a baby need](/how-much-sleep-does-a-baby-need) for 24-hour bands (they already conflict; we do not average them).

**If more waking can be protective, should I wake them on purpose?**  
Only when your pediatrician or the wake-to-feed advice says so. See [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby). This page is not a reason to interrupt a healthy stretch.
