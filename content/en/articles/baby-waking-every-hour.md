# Baby Waking Every Hour at Night: Why It Happens and What Helps

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Few things are as draining as a baby who wakes every hour. By the third night you're counting cycles, and by the fifth you're wondering whether something is wrong. Frequent waking is common in babies, and some of it is protective. Hourly waking *all night, every night*, though, usually has a cause worth looking for. Below we explain what's normal at each age, the most common reasons for hourly waking, when to call the doctor, and what helps.

## Short answer

Waking often is **normal for young babies**, especially under 6 months, and many still wake at night well beyond that. Waking **every hour for part of the night** is common during the newborn months, the 4-month sleep changes, illness, or teething. Waking **every hour all night with no longer stretches** isn't typical: Huckleberry (app example) suggests checking in with your pediatrician, because reflux, feeding problems, or another medical issue may be involved. When health checks out, the most common causes are **needing the same help to fall back asleep at each cycle**, **overtiredness**, **hunger**, and **discomfort**. A steady routine, the right daytime schedule, and (when your baby is old enough) practice falling asleep independently usually help.

## Why babies wake between sleep cycles

Everyone surfaces briefly at the end of each sleep cycle. Adults rarely remember it. Babies' cycles are shorter, so the surfacing happens more often, and if something is different from when they fell asleep, they call out. The Royal Children's Hospital (RCH) Melbourne explains that babies who fall asleep with help (being fed, patted, rocked, or held) may need that same help every time they wake between cycles. The UK charity NCT adds that night waking is a normal part of development and is thought to be protective against SIDS, so the goal isn't to stop all waking.

## What's normal at each age

| Age | What's typical | Is hourly waking expected? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 months | Frequent feeds day and night; days and nights may be mixed up | For part of the night, yes; but most newborns have some longer stretches |
| 3–4 months | Sleep becomes more adult-like; the [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months) | Common for a few weeks |
| 5–6 months | Many still wake 1–3 times; some begin linking cycles | Not usually all night |
| 6–12 months | Object permanence; sleep associations matter more | Possible in phases; usually a cause to find |
| 12 months + | Most babies have long night stretches, but some still wake | Not typical; look for causes |

Sources for this table: Huckleberry (app example), RCH Melbourne, Texas Children's, and Henderson et al. (2010).

### Newborns

Hunger is the main reason newborns wake often, especially during growth spurts and cluster feeding. Huckleberry (app example) notes that even newborns usually manage some stretches longer than an hour. If yours seems to wake hourly around the clock, mixed-up days and nights may be part of it; capping daytime naps at about 2 hours and keeping nights dark can help.

### 4 month old waking every hour

Around 3–4 months, sleep matures and babies spend more time in lighter sleep. Babies who were fed or rocked all the way to sleep may now need that help at each cycle. Huckleberry (app example) calls this the most common reason for hourly waking in babies 3–4 months and older.

### 6 month old waking every hour

RCH Melbourne explains that from about 6 months, babies reach object permanence: they know you still exist when they can't see you, and they may cry at the end of each cycle to bring you back. Sleep associations can start causing more problems at this age.

### 8–12 months and beyond

The same causes apply, and by now most babies have at least one long night stretch. If hourly waking continues for weeks, look at the daytime schedule and at how your baby falls asleep at bedtime.

## Common causes of waking every hour

1. **Sleep associations.** Your baby needs the same help to fall back asleep at every cycle change (RCH Melbourne; Huckleberry, app example).
2. **Overtiredness.** Short naps or long wake windows make it harder to stay asleep. Napper (app example) points out that a newborn's wake window can be as short as 30–50 minutes, so overtiredness can creep up quickly.
3. **Hunger.** Especially in young babies, during growth spurts, or if daytime intake has dropped.
4. **Day/night confusion.** Long daytime naps and stimulating nights.
5. **Illness and discomfort.** A cold, teething, a wet diaper, or feeling too hot or cold (NCT; Huckleberry, app example).
6. **Reflux or feeding problems.** Frequent spit-up, arching, or coughing during feeds (Huckleberry, app example).
7. **Breathing problems during sleep.** Snoring, pauses in breathing, or struggling to breathe (RCH Melbourne).
8. **Changes in routine.** Travel, a new caregiver, a move.

## When to call your pediatrician

Talk to your doctor or child health nurse if your baby:

- wakes every hour **all night** with no longer stretches;
- snores, seems to pause when breathing, or has trouble breathing at any age (RCH Melbourne);
- seems to be in pain, or shows reflux signs such as frequent spit-up, arching, or coughing during feeds;
- isn't gaining weight well;
- is feeding fewer than 8–12 times a day if breastfed or 5–8 times if bottle-fed, or has fewer than 4 wet diapers a day (HealthyChildren.org);
- has a fever, or you have any other worry. Trust your instincts.

RCH Melbourne also recommends speaking to your doctor or nurse if you're struggling to cope. Sleep deprivation is hard, and asking for support is part of looking after your baby.

## What helps: a practical plan

### 1. Rule out health problems first

Go through the list above. If something fits, start with your pediatrician.

### 2. Get the daytime schedule right

Overtired babies tend to sleep worse, not better. Check [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) and make sure naps add up to a reasonable total. If naps are very short, see [short naps](/short-naps).

### 3. Build a predictable bedtime routine

RCH Melbourne notes that by about 6 months, babies can predict and respond to positive routines. Keep the same few calm steps every evening.

### 4. Feed fully in the day and early evening

For babies who still need night feeds, keep them. For older babies, a full bedtime feed (not one where your baby falls asleep halfway) can reduce hunger wakings. Ask your pediatrician before dropping night feeds.

### 5. Separate feeding from falling asleep (when your baby is ready)

Moving the feed earlier in the bedtime routine helps break a feed-to-sleep link. RCH Melbourne says there's no evidence behavioral sleep interventions help babies **under 6 months**, so for young babies, respond to cues as best you can. From about 6 months, gentle approaches or structured sleep training are options. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).

### 6. Respond consistently at night

Keep night interactions quiet, dim, and the same each time. Whatever approach you choose, consistency helps more than switching tactics every night.

### 7. Share the load

NCT suggests sharing night-time care where possible. If your baby is formula-fed, another caregiver can give some feeds; NCT also discusses how a breastfed baby's feeds can be shared once breastfeeding is established.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it normal for my baby to wake every hour?**  
For part of the night, especially in young babies or during a regression, illness, or teething, yes. Every hour all night isn't typical and is worth discussing with your pediatrician.

**Why does my 4 month old wake every hour?**  
Usually because sleep has just matured and your baby needs help to link cycles, often the same help they had at bedtime.

**Why does my 6 month old wake every hour?**  
Common reasons are sleep associations, new skills, object permanence, and overtiredness. Hunger and teething can also play a part.

**Will my baby grow out of waking every hour?**  
Many babies' sleep consolidates over time; Henderson et al. found the fastest changes in the first 4 months. If the pattern lasts weeks, look for causes rather than waiting.

**Does feeding to sleep cause night waking?**  
It can contribute from about 3–4 months, because your baby may need a feed to fall back asleep at each cycle. It's not harmful, and you don't have to change it if it works for your family.

**What if my baby wakes every 30–45 minutes at night?**  
Huckleberry (app example) says waking every 30 minutes without longer stretches isn't typical and may point to extreme overtiredness or discomfort such as reflux or illness. Talk to your pediatrician.
