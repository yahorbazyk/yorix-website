# 3 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Naps, Night Stretches, and an Example Day

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Around 12 weeks, many parents notice the first glimmers of order: a longer stretch after the late-evening feed, a baby who stays bright and chatty a little longer in the morning, naps that start to land at roughly similar times. It is a halfway stage. Your 3-month-old is no longer a newborn in sleep terms, but not yet ready for the more clock-based days that tend to come later.

## Short answer

A **3 month old sleep schedule** is still flexible. The National Sleep Foundation's expert panel recommends **14–17 hours of sleep per 24 hours for babies 0–3 months**; the American Academy of Sleep Medicine's recommendations only begin at 4 months. KidsHealth says many babies this age take **2–3 daytime naps** followed by a longer night stretch, and that "sleeping through the night" at this stage means about **5 or 6 hours**. Formal sleep training is generally considered too early: Sleep Foundation places readiness at 4–6 months.

### Key numbers at a glance

| Question | What the sources say | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total sleep, 0–3 months | 14–17 hours per 24 hours | National Sleep Foundation (Hirshkowitz et al., 2015); KidsHealth |
| Naps | 2–3 for many babies at 1–3 months | KidsHealth |
| "Sleeping through" at this age | A stretch of about 5–6 hours | KidsHealth |

## What changes in sleep around 3 months

Several developments come together at about this age:

- **The body clock is switching on.** Sleep Foundation explains that from about 8 to 9 weeks, melatonin and cortisol begin following a daily rhythm. The Sydney Children's Hospitals Network says many babies around 3 months start staying awake longer in the day and sleeping longer at night.
- **Night stretches lengthen for some.** Newcastle Hospitals NHS says some babies skip a night feed by 3 months and sleep around 5 hours at a time. Tresillian describes night sleep at 3 months lengthening to up to 5–6 hours, often twice a night, with one to three brief rousings inside those stretches.
- **But not for everyone.** KidsHealth stresses that the range of "normal" at 1–3 months is still very wide. Plenty of healthy 12-week-olds still wake every few hours.

## How much should a 3 month old sleep?

Because the AASM does not set a figure for babies under 4 months, the most commonly used number comes from the National Sleep Foundation: **14 to 17 hours** for newborns up to 3 months. KidsHealth and Sleep Foundation both quote this range. Other services describe wider spreads. Tresillian says most babies of 3–6 months sleep about 14 or 15 hours, while Pregnancy, Birth and Baby lists 11–19 hours for birth to 3 months.

The panel behind the NSF figures added a caveat worth knowing: durations outside the range can still be appropriate for some individuals, but being far outside it is rare. In practical terms, a content, feeding, growing baby who sleeps 13 hours is not a cause for alarm. A baby who seems unusually hard to wake or is sleeping far more than before is worth a call.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry suggests about 15 hours with 4–5 naps at 3 months; Napper describes 14–17 hours with 3–4 naps. Both sit near the NSF range but suggest more naps than KidsHealth.

## A 3 month old schedule: example day

This is **an example only**. At this age, follow your baby's cues first and the clock second. The times assume a 7:00 a.m. start.

| Time | Activity | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7:00 a.m. | Wake, feed, curtains open | Morning light helps the developing body clock |
| 8:15–9:30 a.m. | Nap | Yawns, glazed look, turning away |
| 9:30 a.m. | Feed, then floor time and tummy time | Short bursts are enough |
| 11:00 a.m.–12:30 p.m. | Nap | A walk in the pram can count |
| 12:30 p.m. | Feed, cuddles, play | |
| 2:00–3:15 p.m. | Nap | |
| 3:15 p.m. | Feed | |
| 4:45–5:20 p.m. | Short late-afternoon nap | Helps the baby reach bedtime calmly |
| 6:30 p.m. | Bath or wash, feed, song | Keep the same order every evening |
| About 7:30 p.m. | Down for the night | Expect one or more night feeds |
| Late evening or night | Feed(s), kept dim and brief | |

Add it up and you get roughly 4 hours 25 minutes of daytime sleep, with night sleep broken by feeds. Some 3-month-olds take more, shorter naps; some take three longer ones. Both patterns are fine.

## Naps at 3 months

Naps are often still irregular in length. One nap might last 35 minutes and the next almost 2 hours. Rather than chasing a fixed number, look at the whole day: is your baby getting enough sleep across 24 hours to be mostly content when awake?

Helpful habits at this stage:

- Offer a nap when you see early tiredness signs, before crying starts.
- Use a short, repeatable wind-down: dim the room, change the diaper, a song, into the sleep sack.
- Let some naps happen on the go. Newcastle Hospitals NHS suggests daytime sleep in a pram or a lighter room can help babies learn that long, dark, quiet sleep is for night.

For a wider view of how nap counts fall across the first two years, see our [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age).

## 3 month old wake windows

Around 12 weeks a baby can usually manage a little more time awake than in the newborn weeks, but not much. What the charts say:

| Who says it | Age it covers | Time awake between sleeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleveland Clinic | 1–3 months, then 3–4 months | 1–2 hours, then 1.25–2.5 hours |
| Sleep Foundation | 1–4 months | 1–3 hours |
| Huckleberry (app example) | 3 months | 60–120 minutes |
| Napper (app example) | 3 months | 1–2 hours |

A sensible place to begin is about **75 to 90 minutes**, feeds included, and to go shorter after a poor nap *(practical suggestion)*. Huckleberry's list of sleepy cues at this age includes going quiet and still, staring into space, and resting the head against you. Wake windows are not an official AAP or AASM measure, so use them only as a rough guide; our [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) chart shows how they change month to month.

## Night feeds at 3 months

Night feeds are still normal for most 3-month-olds, especially breastfed babies. HealthyChildren.org (AAP) notes that most formula-fed babies no longer need a middle-of-the-night feed somewhere **between 2 and 4 months**, or once they weigh more than about 12 pounds, though this varies a lot between babies. Breastfed babies often keep at least one night feed for longer; there is no single cut-off that suits every baby.

A few ways to keep night feeds sleepy:

- Low light only, no screens.
- Change the diaper only if it is dirty or very wet.
- Put your baby back down soon after the feed, rather than waiting for deep sleep in arms.

## Is there a 3 month sleep regression?

Many parents notice changes between 3 and 4 months. Sleep Foundation describes newborn sleep as falling into one- to three-hour segments and says this begins to change by around 3 to 4 months, as sleep starts to mature. Some babies wake more or nap more briefly during that shift. If your 3-month-old's sleep has suddenly worsened, our [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months) guide explains the process. Rule out illness, hunger, and discomfort first.

## Day-night contrast and the bedtime routine

KidsHealth suggests starting a bedtime routine now if you haven't already, using familiar, relaxing steps in the same order. It doesn't have to be long. Many families use something like a bath or wash, a feed, a short song, and into bed.

During the day, the NHS suggests opening the curtains, playing, and not worrying much about everyday noise while your baby naps. Derbyshire NHS adds that getting up at about the same time each morning helps line up your body clock and your baby's. At night, keep everything quiet, dim, and dull.

## Why sleep training is usually too early at 3 months

Sleep Foundation says babies generally aren't ready for sleep training until **4 to 6 months**, because the rhythms that support longer independent sleep are still developing. HealthyChildren.org adds that regular sleep cycles don't really appear until about 4 months.

What you *can* practice now is gentle: putting your baby down drowsy but not fully asleep some of the time, and giving them a moment to settle before you step in. If you are curious about later options, our [sleep training methods guide](/sleep-training-methods-explained) compares them.

## Safe sleep at 3 months

Rolling often starts around now. KidsHealth says swaddling is fine until babies begin to roll, which typically happens at about 3 to 4 months, and that the first attempts to roll over are the signal to stop. HealthyChildren.org (AAP) warns against weighted swaddles, sleepers, and blankets at any age.

The rest of the AAP basics still apply: back to sleep every time, a firm, flat crib or bassinet with only a fitted sheet, and room-sharing without bed-sharing.

## What can make a 3-month-old's sleep harder

- **Keeping your baby awake in the day "so they sleep at night."** KidsHealth notes that overtired infants often have more trouble sleeping at night.
- **Long daytime naps in a pitch-dark room**, which can blur day and night for some babies (Newcastle Hospitals NHS).
- **Lively night feeds:** lights, chatting, and play.
- **Comparing with other babies.** Ranges at this age are genuinely wide.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call your pediatrician if your baby is feeding poorly or having fewer wet diapers, seems much sleepier or harder to wake than usual, has a fever, breathes with effort or makes unusual noises during sleep, or if you are simply concerned. A fever in a baby under 12 weeks old warrants a prompt call (HealthyChildren.org symptom checker).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should a 3 month old sleep?**  
The National Sleep Foundation recommends 14–17 hours per 24 hours for babies up to 3 months.

**How many naps does a 3 month old take?**  
KidsHealth says many take 2–3 naps; app guidance suggests 4–5 shorter naps for some babies. Nap length decides how many your baby needs.

**Should my 3 month old sleep through the night?**  
Not necessarily. At this age, "through the night" usually means a 5–6 hour stretch (KidsHealth), and many babies still wake for feeds.

**Can I sleep train a 3 month old?**  
Sleep Foundation places readiness at 4–6 months. For now, focus on a calm routine and drowsy-but-awake practice.

**When should I stop swaddling my 3 month old?**  
Once your baby starts trying to roll over (KidsHealth).
