# 11 Week Old Baby Sleep: One Week Short of the 3-Month Mark

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 11 snapshot**. It is not [10 week old baby sleep](/10-week-old-baby-sleep), not [12 week old baby sleep](/12-week-old-baby-sleep), and not [3 month old sleep schedule](/3-month-old-sleep-schedule). If you are already bracing for a "3-month regression," read this page for the week you are actually in, then use the 12-week and 3-month guides when you get there.

## Short answer

You are still in the 0–3 month period KidsHealth ties to NSF's **14–17 hours**. The wider published bands (**11–19**, some NHS pages at about **8–18**, HealthyChildren.org's "about 16–17") have not been withdrawn. Nothing official is labelled "week 11." The AASM **12–16 hour** recommendation begins at **4 months**, not at day 77, and this snapshot will not back-fit it.

No sourced landmark lands uniquely on this week. The 8–9 week body-clock shift may already be in play. "Sleeping through the night" at 1–3 months still often means **about 5–6 hours** (KidsHealth). Formal sleep training is still generally discussed later, around 4–6 months (Sleep Foundation).

### One week short of 3 months — still the early band

- **Hours:** KidsHealth still ties the 0–3 month period to NSF's 14–17 hours. There is no official week-11 total, and the 4-month AASM figure is not used here.
- **Not yet AASM.** The 12–16 hour 4–12 month recommendation starts at 4 months. Do not apply it at week 11.
- **A long night:** KidsHealth's 1–3 month use of "through the night" is often 5–6 hours.
- **Training:** still generally a later conversation (Sleep Foundation, toward 4–6 months), even if a 3-month visit is next week.

## What is usually going on at eleven weeks

A three-month visit may be on the calendar in a week or two. Days can look almost like a loose rhythm — several short naps, a slightly earlier evening than week 6 — without being the 3-month sample day. Feeds may be stretching a little in the day. Nights may include one longer stretch and one or two more wakings, or they may not.

Parents sometimes try a slightly earlier bedtime around now, hoping the first stretch will lengthen. An earlier, boring wind-down can be a reasonable experiment. A sudden 6:30 p.m. lights-out copied from a later month page can also produce a very early morning. See [early morning waking](/early-morning-waking) if 5 a.m. becomes the new "morning."

## Earlier bedtime trials and first false starts

Community threads at 10–11 weeks often use the word "regression," describe a first false start (asleep at bedtime, up 30–45 minutes later), or talk about moving bedtime from 9:00 toward 7:30. Those recurring scenes chose the examples below. They are not official timings.

- **"10–11 week sleep regression."** There is no official one. Short naps plus a wobblier night as you approach 12 weeks is a common complaint. The named 3-month discussion lives on [sleep regression at 3 months](/sleep-regression/3-months) and the 12-week snapshot.
- **A first false start.** They go down after the last feed and are up again in half an hour, as if bedtime was a nap. The full false-start essay is on [false starts](/false-starts); the 12-week-specific page is [12 week old false starts](/12-week-old-false-starts). This week, treat a one-off as a one-off.
- **An earlier bedtime experiment.** Trying 7:30 p.m. after weeks of 9:00 p.m. can help some babies and can make others wake at 4 or 5 a.m. Change one thing and watch a few nights; do not also drop a nap on the same day.

Safe sleep is unchanged. If early rolling starts — unusual but not impossible — stop swaddling and keep the space clear.

## An earlier-evening trial (example only)

Not the 12-week "slightly earlier evening" table and not the 3-month sample day. Suppose a last short nap ends around 4:50 p.m., a slightly earlier wind-down starts at 7:10, lights are down by 7:35, a first stretch lasts until about 11:50 p.m., a feed follows, and then they are up again at 4:10 a.m. wondering if morning has started. The next night you might keep 8:15 p.m. instead. The sketch exists to show the experiment, not to prescribe 7:35.

## The three-month visit is next — some questions cannot wait

If a three-month check is coming, take night-feed and development questions there. Get help before the appointment if they stay hard to wake for a feed, if wet-diaper counts fall, or if breathing is a worry. A baby who will not wake at all, or who is struggling to breathe, needs emergency care now.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a 10–11 week sleep regression?**
No official one. If the wobble continues into week 12, use [12 week old baby sleep](/12-week-old-baby-sleep) and [sleep regression at 3 months](/sleep-regression/3-months).

**What is a good bedtime for an 11 week old?**
There is no official clock time. A slightly earlier, boring wind-down can be a trial. The 12-week FAQ on bedtime lives on that week's page, not here.

**Is 11 or 12 weeks a training start date?**
Usually not. Sleep Foundation places formal training later, toward 4–6 months. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).
