# Baby Nap Schedule by Age: Nap Chart and Nap Transitions

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Naps shrink in number long before they shrink in importance. A newborn may sleep six or more times between dawn and dusk; a toddler usually settles on one long afternoon nap. This guide covers **how many naps your baby needs at each age**, how long those naps tend to last, and the two transitions that cause the most confusion: **3 naps to 2** and **2 naps to 1**.

Looking for how long your baby should stay awake between naps? That's a separate question, covered in our [wake windows by age chart](/wake-windows-by-age).

## Short answer

Nap count drops in steps across the first two years. Sleep Foundation says newborns may take **six or more naps** a day. KidsHealth describes **2–3 daytime naps** for most babies from around 4 months, and **two naps** as typical at 8–12 months. Most toddlers move to **one nap by about 18 months** (KidsHealth); Boys Town National Research Hospital puts the usual 2-to-1 switch at **15–18 months**. Very few children stop napping before age 2.

### Key numbers at a glance

| Question | What the sources say | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newborn naps per day | 6 or more | Sleep Foundation |
| Naps from about 4 months | 2–3 (some babies still take a late-afternoon catnap) | KidsHealth; Mayo Clinic |
| Dropping the third nap | Around 9 months | Mayo Clinic |
| Two naps to one | Usually 15–18 months; most by 18 months | Boys Town; KidsHealth |

## How long should baby naps be?

There is no single "right" nap length, and the sources describe a broad spread:

- **3–6 months:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says each nap lasts up to about 2 hours. Tresillian notes that at 4–5 months a sleep cycle is around 30–50 minutes, so waking after 30–40 minutes is very common.
- **8–12 months:** KidsHealth says some babies nap for 30 minutes while others nap for up to 2 hours.
- **Toddlers:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby describes one nap of 1 to 2 hours after 12 months, and Mayo Clinic says most children still sleep 1–2 hours in the afternoon until about age 3.

Mayo Clinic suggests letting babies from 4 months to 1 year nap as long as they want, *unless* they then have trouble falling asleep at night. Late-day naps are usually the ones to trim.

## The 4 to 3 nap transition

**When:** There is no official AAP week for this drop. It usually happens in the 4- to 5-month stretch, as wake periods get longer and a fourth nap is squeezed out. Our [4 month old sleep schedule](/4-month-old-sleep-schedule) already shows 3-nap and 4-nap example days; our [5 month old sleep schedule](/5-month-old-sleep-schedule) has a signs table for this same switch. This section is the short process, not a third chart.

**Signs your baby may be ready** (practical signs, aligned with the 5-month page; no clinical body publishes a checklist):

- The last nap of the day is fought or skipped on most days.
- Bedtime keeps drifting later because that last nap landed too close to night.
- The first naps of the day are getting a little longer, and your baby is often calm after two hours or more awake.

**How to make the switch:**

1. Let the first three naps be the day's sleep, instead of waking the baby to "fit in" a fourth.
2. On days without a fourth nap, bring bedtime a little earlier if the late afternoon is long.
3. Expect mixed 3- and 4-nap days for a week or so. That is the transition, not a failure.

This is not the later [3-to-2 nap transition](/3-to-2-nap-transition). If your 5-month-old is still happy on four short naps, there is no prize for dropping early.

## The 3 to 2 nap transition

**When:** Mayo Clinic suggests cutting out a late-afternoon third nap at about **9 months**, which helps bring bedtime earlier. As an app example, Huckleberry describes most babies finishing the 3-to-2 switch by 8 months.

**Signs your baby may be ready** (practical signs; no clinical body publishes a checklist):

- The third nap is refused or takes ages to start on most days.
- When it does happen, the third nap pushes bedtime uncomfortably late.
- Your baby copes well with the longer gap before bed on days the nap is skipped.

**How to make the switch:**

1. Let the first two naps run a little longer if your baby will take it.
2. On days without a third nap, bring bedtime earlier, even by 30–60 minutes, so the afternoon isn't too long.
3. Expect a messy week or two, with some three-nap days mixed in. That's part of the process.

## The 2 to 1 nap transition

**When:** Boys Town National Research Hospital describes **15–18 months** as the most common window, and KidsHealth says most toddlers are down to one nap by 18 months. Mayo Clinic gives an earlier picture, noting that babies 10–12 months old often drop the morning nap. Sources clearly differ here, so think of roughly 12–18 months as the range, with mid-toddlerhood being typical.

**What the switch usually looks like:** Boys Town describes gradually keeping a toddler awake longer in the morning, so that two naps (for example around 9:30 and 2:30) turn into a single midday nap closer to noon, often with lunch moving earlier. Toddlers who weren't quite ready may need an earlier bedtime or a slightly later morning wake-up for a while.

**Daycare tip:** Boys Town also points out that daycare nap times may not match your child's readiness exactly. Ask how the room handles toddlers who are mid-transition.

## Short naps and nap strikes: what to try

- **Under 6 months, short is common.** A 30–40 minute nap may simply be one sleep cycle (Tresillian). Offering an extra nap later in the day often works better than trying to stretch every nap.
- **Start the nap drowsy but awake.** KidsHealth recommends putting infants down sleepy but not yet asleep, so they learn to fall asleep on their own.
- **Keep a short, steady nap routine.** KidsHealth suggests soft music, dim lights, and a quiet story. Mayo Clinic says naps at about the same time each day tend to work best.
- **Light or dark room?** Advice varies. Mayo Clinic says a dark, quiet room can help babies sleep. Newcastle Hospitals NHS suggests avoiding long daytime naps in dark rooms, so babies learn that dark, quiet rooms are for night. A reasonable middle path is a dim (not blacked-out) room for naps, with the darkest setting saved for night.
- **A sudden nap strike** around a transition age often signals the next step, but check for illness, teething discomfort, or hunger first.

## What can make naps harder

- **Dropping a nap too early** because of one bad week. Short-lived refusals are common.
- **A very late final nap** that leaves your baby wide awake at bedtime.
- **Inconsistent places** for every nap, every day. Some flexibility is fine, but a predictable main nap spot helps.
- **Unsafe nap spots.** Naps count too: back to sleep, on a firm, flat surface with nothing loose in the crib. HealthyChildren.org (AAP) advises moving a baby who falls asleep in a car seat, swing, or carrier to a safe flat surface as soon as possible.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Bring it up if your baby is regularly very sleepy during the day despite good night sleep, snores or struggles to breathe when asleep, is suddenly much sleepier or harder to wake than usual, or isn't feeding or growing well. KidsHealth also suggests looking at bedtime and the nap schedule if a child seems tired, rubs their eyes, or acts out during the day.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps does a baby need?**  
It depends on age: many in the newborn weeks, usually 2–3 from about 4 months, 2 in the second half of the first year, and 1 for most toddlers by 18 months (Sleep Foundation; KidsHealth).

**When do babies go from 3 naps to 2?**  
Mayo Clinic suggests dropping the late-afternoon third nap at about 9 months.

**When do babies go from 2 naps to 1?**  
Most commonly at 15–18 months (Boys Town), with most toddlers on one nap by 18 months (KidsHealth).

**How long should a baby nap?**  
Anywhere from about 30 minutes to 2 hours is described as normal for infants. Toddlers typically take one nap of 1–2 hours.

**When do toddlers stop napping?**  
Fewer than 2.5% stop before age 2, and 94% have stopped by age 5, according to a 2020 meta-analysis.

**Should naps be at the same time every day?**  
Mayo Clinic says babies get the most out of naps taken at about the same time and length each day, though real days will vary.
