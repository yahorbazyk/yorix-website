# 6 Week Old Baby Sleep: The Fussy, Hungry Middle of the Early Weeks

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is the **week 6 snapshot**. It is not [1 month old sleep schedule](/1-month-old-sleep-schedule) (weeks 4–7 as a month view) and not [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression) (the popular name for this rough patch). If evenings are the whole problem, also use [witching hour](/witching-hour) and [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding) rather than copying those pages here.

## Short answer

The official 24-hour band has **not** changed: for 0–3 months, KidsHealth quotes the National Sleep Foundation **14–17 hours**. Wider ranges still apply (11–19; NHS about 8–18). There is no separate AAP or NSF "week 6 quota."

What *has* often changed is the texture of the day. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby: the body clock **starts to develop around 4–6 weeks**, so light and dark begin to matter a little more — not as a finished timetable. Many families also hit a harder evening: more fuss, more frequent feeds, shorter daytime naps. Parent communities often call this a "6 week regression" or a "growth spurt." There is **no official sleep-regression event** at 6 weeks (see the regression page). The Period of PURPLE Crying, a well-described fussy period, often **peaks around 6–8 weeks** and then eases by 3–4 months (National Center on Shaken Baby Syndrome and clinical explainers). That is a crying pattern, not a sleep-hour chart.

Do not start formal sleep training this week. Sleep Foundation generally places readiness later, around 4–6 months.

## What is usually going on this week

Babies are often a little more awake and interested, which is lovely at 10 a.m. and miserable at 6 p.m. Feeds may pack together in the late afternoon and evening — that is [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding), and after the first week it should not be *around the clock* without a feeding check. A short, same-order wind-down (dim light, feed, a song) can start to mean "night," because the clock is just beginning to listen. It will not switch off an evening peak by itself.

Social smiling is often appearing around this stretch; the usual pediatric milestone language is a social smile **by 2 months**. Do not treat a missed smile at exactly 42 days as a sleep problem. If development worries you, ask the pediatrician, not a schedule.

## What parents often struggle with at 6 weeks

Paraphrased from recurring community threads, not from a survey:

- A loose daytime pattern seemed to start last week, and this week it fell apart.
- From late afternoon, the baby will not settle in the bassinet and wants to feed again and again.
- Naps in the crib are 20–40 minutes; longer sleep happens only in arms.
- Nights that had one slightly longer stretch go back to hourly waking for a while.

None of that, by itself, means you caused a "bad habit." It also does not mean you should run a cry-it-out plan. Get through the evening with help if you can, keep nights boring, and use the regression page if you want the popular name unpacked.

## A late-day sketch (example only)

This is not the 7:00 a.m. table on the 1-month page. It is one **evening**, labelled as a sketch.

From about 5:00 p.m. the baby is tired and wired: a short feed, a brief doze on your chest, then another feed. Between 6:30 and 8:30 there may be almost no bassinet success — walking, a carrier, or a partner taking a shift is the work, not a failed routine. Sometime after 9:00, a proper feed and a dim room may finally start a longer stretch. Night feeds still come. If tomorrow's evening is quieter, that does not mean you "fixed" week 6 with a trick.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if the baby is not waking to feed, has too few wet diapers, seems in pain, is hard to rouse, or if you are not coping. Evening fuss that you can soothe at least some of the time is common; a cry you cannot make sense of with poor feeding or a fever is not a "wait for week 8" problem. If you feel angry or unsafe with a crying baby, put them down in a safe crib and take a break — the PURPLE Crying materials exist in part for that moment.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should a 6 week old sleep in 24 hours?**  
The 0–3 month band, usually quoted as 14–17 hours. There is no official week-6 total.

**How long should a 6 week old sleep at night without eating?**  
That is a [wake-to-feed](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby) question, and it depends on weight gain and what your pediatrician already told you — not on a new 6-week rule.

**Is this a sleep regression?**  
It is a popular name. Read [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression) rather than starting a training book.
