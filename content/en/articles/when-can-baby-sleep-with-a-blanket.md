# When Can a Baby Sleep With a Blanket or Lovey?

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A small blanket or a favourite stuffed toy is one of the first things many families want to add to the crib. In the first year, loose bedding and soft objects are still a suffocation and SIDS risk, which is why safe-sleep advice is so blunt: a bare crib. This page is about **when a blanket or lovey becomes a reasonable idea**, not a second article on [how to dress a baby for sleep](/how-to-dress-baby-for-sleep) or [sleep sacks](/sleep-sacks). Those remain the first-year warmth tools.

## Short answer

**Do not put a loose blanket, quilt, pillow, or stuffed toy in the sleep space for the first 12 months.** That is the AAP, KidsHealth, NHS, and Sleep Foundation position. After 12 months, the AAP's parent materials say most experts agree these objects pose little risk to **healthy** babies, while also stating that research has not shown a moment when they are 100% safe. A wearable blanket or sleep sack is not the same thing as a loose blanket and can be used earlier. A lovey or stuffed animal belongs in awake, supervised play until that first birthday, then can be introduced cautiously if you and your pediatrician are comfortable.

## Why a first-year crib stays empty

Soft objects and loose bedding can cover a baby's face or trap them against the side of a crib or bassinet. The AAP's list is familiar: pillows, quilts, comforters, sheepskins, bumper pads, and stuffed toys. A thin receiving blanket used as a [swaddle](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby) is a separate case — and even that comes off when the baby starts to roll.

Sleep Foundation's blanket article (updated 30 September 2026) is aligned: no loose blankets under 12 months; wearable blankets are the usual substitute; after 12 months, still be cautious with heavy or very loose bedding. NHS and Lullaby Trust describe a clear cot, with any covers tucked in firmly using a [feet-to-foot](/how-to-dress-baby-for-sleep) method in UK guidance, rather than a duvet over a young baby.

A muslin "for comfort" draped over part of the mattress, a pillow "just under the head," or a quilt along the rail are still loose bedding. So is a stuffed animal left in the corner "where they cannot reach it yet." Babies move more than we expect.

## So what keeps them warm?

Use clothing and a sleep sack, not a thrown-on blanket. See [how to dress a baby for sleep](/how-to-dress-baby-for-sleep) for layers and the room-temperature disagreement between sources, and [sleep sacks](/sleep-sacks) for wearable blankets. The AAP's practical rule of thumb is about one more layer than an adult would wear in the same room, and no hats indoors.

## After 12 months: a cautious yes

Once your child is past their first birthday, a light blanket or a small soft toy is something many families add. The AAP's patient-education wording is careful: it has not been proven when it is completely safe, but most experts agree the objects pose little risk to healthy babies after 12 months. That is not a promise, and it does not apply to a child with unusual muscle tone, a breathing problem, or a sleep space that is already cluttered.

Practical cautions after 12 months, compiled from Sleep Foundation and AAP materials rather than invented as a new standard:

- Start with one small, lightweight blanket — not a heavy quilt, weighted blanket, or adult duvet.
- Skip pillows until your child is in a bed, unless your pediatrician says otherwise; a crib pillow is still a soft object near the face.
- Choose a lovey that is not large enough to cover the face, does not have loose ribbons, buttons, or batteries, and is not a weighted toy.
- Keep the crib or toddler bed otherwise clear. Adding a lovey is not a reason to add bumpers, pillows, and a quilt at the same time.

If your 13-month-old still sleeps well with only a sleep sack, you do not have to introduce a blanket.

## When a lovey is useful — and when it is not

A small comfort object is still a soft object: keep it out of the crib in the first year, and introduce it cautiously after 12 months for a healthy toddler. Types, how to choose one, and a wording conflict between U.S. advice and Red Nose Australia are on [when can a baby have a lovey](/when-can-baby-have-a-lovey). This page stays on blankets, pillows, and wearable sacks.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask before adding bedding if your child was born preterm, has a neuromuscular or breathing condition, or still has very restless or unusual sleep. If a blanket or toy is in the crib and you find your child face-down in it, remove it and mention the episode at the next visit.

## Frequently asked questions

**When can babies sleep with a blanket?**  
After 12 months, with a light blanket and a still-fairly-clear crib. Until then, use a sleep sack or extra layer of clothing.

**When can a baby have a lovey or stuffed animal at night?**
Not in the first year. For types, choosing a small comfort object later, and the differences between U.S. and Australian wording, see [when can a baby have a lovey](/when-can-baby-have-a-lovey).

**Is a sleep sack a blanket?**  
No. It is a wearable layer. It should still be unweighted and the right size so it cannot ride up over the face.

**Can I tuck a blanket into the sides of a bassinet?**  
No. Bassinets are for younger babies, who should have no loose bedding at all.

**Are weighted blankets okay after the first birthday?**  
The AAP recommends against weighted blankets, sleepers, and swaddles. We do not treat the first birthday as a green light for a weighted product.
