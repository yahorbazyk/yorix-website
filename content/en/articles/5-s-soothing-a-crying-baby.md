# The 5 S's for Soothing a Crying Baby (Product-Neutral)

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

The "5 S's" are a popular soothing framework named by pediatrician Harvey Karp in *The Happiest Baby on the Block* and explained on the Happiest Baby site: **swaddle, side or stomach position, shush, swing, and suck**. This page is a **product-neutral** guide to that framework and, just as important, to the safety limits that official bodies already set. It is not a sleep-training method, not a [colic](/colic) treatment list, and not a review of any bassinet or noise machine.

We attribute the *name* and the five-item list to Karp / Happiest Baby. The safety rules — back for every sleep, hip-healthy swaddling, never shaking, no overnight sleep in a swing — come from the AAP, the International Hip Dysplasia Institute's swaddle guidance we already use on [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby), and related pages. Where Karp's commercial site promotes branded products, we leave those out.

## Short answer

The 5 S's are a way to comfort a **fussy, awake** baby, often in the first months. Used carefully, they borrow sensations many babies found familiar before birth: snug holding, rhythmic sound, gentle motion, and sucking. The one rule you cannot loosen is this: **side or stomach is for calming in your arms, never for sleep**. Karp's own explanation says you may hold a baby on the side or stomach, but it is not safe to place them that way to sleep; the back is the only safe sleep position. After the baby settles, put them down on their back in a clear crib or bassinet, the same way [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies) describes.

Swaddling is the first S. It must leave the hips free to bend, and it must stop when rolling starts — including in any device. Gentle rocking is not shaking. A baby swing is not an overnight bed. Sucking can mean a feed or, once breastfeeding is going well if you are nursing, a [pacifier](/pacifier-and-sleep).

### The five S's at a glance

| The S | What it means here | The safety limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swaddle | A snug wrap that can reduce startle and help some babies settle | Hips loose and flexed; stop as soon as rolling starts; not tight on the chest. See [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby) |
| Side or stomach | Hold the baby on the side, tummy, or over your shoulder while you soothe | **In arms only, while you are awake.** Then place on the back to sleep (Karp; AAP) |
| Shush | Steady, fairly strong white noise or a shushing voice | Keep volume reasonable; do not rest a speaker against the baby's ear (see official noise advice on our safe-sleep hub if you use sound) |
| Swing | Small, rhythmic motion — a rock, a walk, a gentle bounce — while you support the head | Never shake. Do not leave a baby to sleep in a swing or sitting device ([car-seat sleep](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat)) |
| Suck | Feeding, or a pacifier | If breastfeeding, wait until nursing is well established before a dummy (AAP / our pacifier page; Karp agrees) |

## What the 5 S's are — and are not

Karp frames the first three months after birth as a "fourth trimester," and he describes a "calming reflex" that responding to womb-like rhythm can trigger. That framing is his, from his book and site. It is a popular way to think about newborn fussiness; it is **not a formal medical diagnosis** and not a claim we can independently verify from AAP or NHS pages. We mention it only so you know where the method's story comes from.

The 5 S's will not fix hunger, a dirty diaper, illness, or pain. They are a soothing sequence, not a reason to delay a feed or a call to the doctor. They also will not replace a [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) later in the first year, and they are not a substitute for [gentle sleep training](/gentle-sleep-training) or any other method once a family is ready for that conversation.

Evening fuss that is not colic has its own page: [the witching hour](/witching-hour). If crying meets the clinical picture of colic, or you are worried something is medically wrong, use [colic](/colic) and call your pediatrician.

## How to use them without fighting safe sleep

There is no official AAP protocol that says "do the five S's in this exact order." Parents who use the framework often layer more than one S at a time — for example, a swaddled baby, held on their side in your arms, with a shush and a gentle rock. A practical, safety-first way to think about it:

1. Check the basics first: hunger, diaper, temperature, a burp, illness.
2. If you swaddle, follow hip-healthy wrapping and stop when signs of rolling appear. Our swaddle page owns the how-to and the stop-date.
3. Hold, don't place, on the side or stomach. The moment the baby is going down for a nap or the night, turn them onto their back in their own firm, flat space.
4. Use a steady shush or white noise if it helps. You do not need a branded track.
5. Rock or walk with the head and neck supported. If you feel angry or at the end of your rope, put the baby down on their back in the crib and step away for a few minutes. **Never shake a baby.**
6. Offer a feed if they are hungry, or a pacifier if that is already part of your plan.

If the baby falls asleep in your arms or in a sling, move them to the crib or bassinet on their back as soon as you can do so safely. Motion and holding are for settling, not for all-night sleep in a device.

## When the 5 S's are not the right tool

See a clinician if crying is sudden and severe, the baby has a fever (100.4°F or 38°C or higher in a baby 3 months or younger is an immediate call), is not feeding, is vomiting forcefully, is hard to wake, or has trouble breathing. Karp's site estimates that a small share of colicky crying has a medical cause such as a food intolerance, infection, or reflux; we will not treat that percentage as a diagnosis threshold. Trust your instincts and get help.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I let my baby sleep on their side after the 5 S's settle them?**  
No. Side and stomach are for holding while you soothe. Sleep is on the back (Karp's own caution; AAP policy).

**Is this sleep training?**  
No. It is a soothing pattern for fussy periods, mainly in young infants. Sleep training, including [gentle methods](/gentle-sleep-training), is a later, separate decision. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).

**Do I need a special bassinet or swaddle to do the 5 S's?**  
No. The framework is older than any one product. This article does not recommend a brand.

**What if my baby hates the swaddle?**  
Some do. You can still use holding, sound, motion, and sucking. See "What if my baby hates the swaddle?" on [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby).
