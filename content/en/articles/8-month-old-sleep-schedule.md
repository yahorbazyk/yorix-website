# 8 Month Old Sleep Schedule: A Typical Two-Nap Day

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This page is about the **ordinary day** of an 8-month-old: when naps usually fall, how long your baby can stay awake, where meals and milk fit, and what time bedtime tends to land. If your baby's sleep has suddenly fallen apart (new night wakings, nap strikes, standing up in the crib at 2 a.m.), that's a different question, answered on our [8 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/8-months) page. Many families need both: a sensible plan for the day, and patience for the weeks when a new skill disrupts it.

## Short answer

KidsHealth says babies of 8–12 months need **12–16 hours of sleep a day, including 9–12 hours at night**, and will likely still take **two naps** lasting anywhere from 30 minutes to 2 hours. Some 8-month-olds still have a short third nap; Mayo Clinic suggests phasing it out at about **9 months**. Published wake windows for 7–10 months are **2.5–4.5 hours** (Cleveland Clinic). Most example schedules put bedtime somewhere between **6:30 and 8 p.m.**, earlier after short naps.

Newcastle Hospitals NHS says it's normal for some babies to begin waking more often at night again at 8–9 months, so a steady schedule and a rough patch can overlap.

## How much sleep at 8 months?

The 12–16 hour range (KidsHealth; AASM) holds all the way to the first birthday. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby gives a similar picture for 6–12 months: 10–14 hours at night and 2–4 hours of naps.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry aims for about 14 hours, with 11–12 at night and 2–3 hours over 2–3 naps. Napper describes 12–15 hours, with 10–12 at night and 2–3 hours in two naps of 1–1.5 hours each.

## Two naps or three at 8 months?

Most 8-month-olds are on two naps or moving there. KidsHealth says babies of this age will likely still take two naps, and Mayo Clinic recommends a morning and early-afternoon nap (it suggests trying 9 a.m. and 1 p.m.), with any late-afternoon third nap cut at about 9 months.

Huckleberry (app example) describes 8 months as a common time for the switch and says it's normal to have a mix of two-nap and three-nap days while it happens. If the morning and afternoon naps are each an hour or more, two naps usually cover the day; if one is short, a 20–30 minute catnap in late afternoon can still rescue bedtime *(practical suggestion)*.

## 8 month wake windows

| Source | Age band | Wake window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleveland Clinic | 7–10 months | 2.5–4.5 hours |
| Sleep Foundation | 7–10 months | 1.5–6 hours |
| Huckleberry (app example) | 8 months | 2.25–3.5 hours |
| Napper (app example) | 8 months | 3–4 hours between naps |

On a two-nap day, a common shape is a shorter morning window (about 2.5–3 hours), a longer midday one, and the longest before bed *(our reading of the app examples)*. Sleep Foundation suggests watching for your baby's own "tell": the sign that, when you act on it, lets your baby fall asleep within about 15 minutes of being put down.

## Meals, milk, and sleep

HealthyChildren.org (AAP) says babies of 8–12 months still need about 400–500 calories a day from breast milk or formula, roughly 24 ounces, and that around 8 months you can offer slightly coarser foods. Its sample menu includes milk with each meal and a last breast milk or formula feed before bed, followed by water or toothbrushing.

Two sleep-related points from the same source and its sister pages:

- **No bottle in the crib.** HealthyChildren.org warns this can lead to tooth decay and ear infections.
- **Cup practice** can start now. The AAP suggests offering a cup around 6 months and finishing the move off bottles between 12 and 18 months.

We found no evidence that more solids at dinner make a baby sleep longer.

## Bedtime at 8 months

KidsHealth suggests a soothing routine, such as a warm bath followed by stories or singing, and says the same activities can carry on for years. Then put your baby in the crib drowsy but still awake. If your baby cries, KidsHealth suggests staying away for a few minutes, then soothing briefly without picking them up, as long as you know they're safe, fed, dry, and well.

Keep the routine short (Cleveland Clinic suggests 20–30 minutes), and keep the last steps in the bedroom (Nationwide Children's Hospital).

## Standing in the crib, and a safer space for a mover

Many 8-month-olds crawl, sit up by themselves, and start pulling up (the CDC's 9-month milestones include getting to a sitting position without help). KidsHealth suggests checking what your baby can reach from a sitting or standing position in the crib, such as cords, curtains, or wall hangings. Nationwide Children's Hospital says to take down mobiles by about 5 months, when babies begin pulling up.

If they have started pulling to stand at night:

1. **Practice sitting back down during the day**, at a low table or the crib rail, so they are less stuck at 2 a.m.
2. **Keep the mattress at the safe height** the crib manual specifies for a baby who can sit or pull up — usually the lowest setting once they can get up.
3. **At night, guide rather than entertain.** Tresillian suggests a calm, short phrase such as "time to lie down," then helping them down and leaving. The fuller night-by-night version is on our [10 month old sleep schedule](/10-month-old-sleep-schedule).
4. **Do not add pillows, bumpers, or extra bedding** to cushion a fall. That creates a suffocation risk. If they are climbing *out*, the crib may no longer be the safe option — see [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed) (most 8-month-olds are not there yet).

Sleep can wobble when this skill arrives; that bump is also discussed on [8 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/8-months).

## What can make an 8 month schedule harder

- Holding on to three naps once the third one pushes bedtime past 8 p.m.
- Dropping the third nap without an earlier bedtime (Nationwide Children's: cutting naps leads to overtiredness).
- A bottle or nursing session as the very last step every night, if your baby then needs it at every waking (KidsHealth; Cleveland Clinic).
- Big weekend swings in nap times *(practical suggestion)*.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Talk to your doctor about loud snoring, pauses in breathing, poor feeding or weight gain, a baby who is hard to wake, loss of skills they used to have, or broken nights that leave you unable to cope.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps does an 8 month old need?**  
Usually two, sometimes a third short one while transitioning. KidsHealth says babies of 8–12 months likely take two.

**What are 8 month wake windows?**  
About 2.5–4.5 hours in Cleveland Clinic's 7–10 month range; app examples cluster around 2.25–4 hours.

**What time should an 8 month old go to bed?**  
Often 6:30–8 p.m. Aim for a full wake window after the last nap.

**Is my 8 month old's bad sleep a regression or the wrong schedule?**  
If the day is steady and bedtime is late, adjust the schedule. If sleep changed suddenly with new skills or separation worries, read our [8 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/8-months) page.

**How much milk does an 8 month old need?**  
About 24 ounces a day of breast milk or formula, according to HealthyChildren.org.

**My 8, 9, 10, or 12 month old won’t nap. Does that mean they should drop a nap?**
Not automatically. Use this page and the matching month schedule. At this age a two-nap day is still common; dropping to one nap too early can look like “they won’t nap.” See [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age) and, when the one-nap transition is actually close, [when do toddlers stop napping](/when-do-toddlers-stop-napping).
