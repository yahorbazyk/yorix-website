# 14 Week Old Baby Sleep: A Long Stretch That May Not Repeat

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 14 snapshot**. It is not [13 week old baby sleep](/13-week-old-baby-sleep), not [15 week old baby sleep](/15-week-old-baby-sleep), and not a second copy of [3 month old sleep schedule](/3-month-old-sleep-schedule) or [4 month old sleep schedule](/4-month-old-sleep-schedule). Searches for "14 week old sleep through the night" belong here as a week question, not as a 12-hour promise.

## Short answer

No guideline publishes a 14-week hour target. Two labelled ranges still sit on either side of you: KidsHealth's NSF figure of **14–17 hours** for **0–3 months**, and AASM's **12–16 hours** from **4 months** (about 16 weeks). Both are listed. They are not averaged.

When a search result, or a relative, implies 10–12 hours "through the night" at 14 weeks, the sourced 1–3 month meaning on KidsHealth is still often a **5–6 hour** stretch. Some babies do offer a long opening to the night; many still wake to feed. The 4-month AASM band is close. It is not in force yet.

### "Through the night" at 14 weeks is not a 12-hour target

- **Hours:** no official week-14 total. The NSF 14–17 hour 0–3 month figure (KidsHealth) and the AASM 12–16 hour figure from 4 months are the neighbouring labels.
- **The phrase:** KidsHealth's 1–3 month use of "through the night" is often 5–6 hours, not 10–12.
- **Training:** still early relative to Sleep Foundation's usual 4–6 month window.
- **Rolling:** if it has started, stop swaddling (HealthyChildren.org / AAP) and keep a clear, firm, flat space, on the back to start.

## What is usually going on at fourteen weeks

You are about two weeks past the usual 3-month mark and about two weeks short of 4 months. Sleep cycles are already maturing in some babies, which is the mechanism the 4-month regression page explains — we will not rebuild that essay. Days may still include three or four short naps. Nights may show a 5–6 hour opening and then a pile of shorter wakings, and the next night may not match.

If they are rolling, or close, the swaddle should already be on the way out. Room-sharing and a clear, firm, flat sleep space still apply.

## "Through the night," and the urge to drop a feed

Community threads at 13–14 weeks often ask whether this is the moment to drop a night feed, or why a 6-hour stretch is followed by frequent wakings that "do not repeat" the next night. Those questions chose the FAQs below.

- **"Will they sleep through at 14 weeks?"** Not as an official target. A 5–6 hour stretch is already a sourced meaning of the phrase at 1–3 months. Ten or twelve hours is a later hope, not a week-14 standard. Do not drop a feed only because a neighbour's baby did.
- **A 6-hour opening, then a messy second half — and no repeat the next night.** That inconsistency is the week-14 picture more often than a stable new "skill." Watch a week of nights before you change two things at once.
- **Temptation to start formal training early.** Sleep Foundation generally discusses that around 4–6 months. If you want the policy discussion, use [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training), not a 14-week experiment copied from a forum.

## One night with a 6-hour opening, one without (example only)

Not a month timetable. Night A: down around 8:20 p.m., first waking around 2:15 a.m. for a feed, then shorter wakings at 4:40 and 6:10. Night B: down at a similar time, first hard waking at 11:40 p.m., then fragments until a 5:50 a.m. "morning." The contrast is the point. Neither night is a schedule to copy, and averaging the two clocks would invent a third night that did not happen.

## Dropping a night feed is a clinical question

Ask your pediatrician before you drop a night feed, especially if weight gain has been slow or they were premature. Do not wait for a scheduled visit if they are unusually difficult to wake, if feeds or wet diapers have fallen off, or if breathing is a concern. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken or who is in respiratory distress.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a 14 week old sleep through the night?**
Some have a long first stretch. "Through the night" in 1–3 month language often means 5–6 hours (KidsHealth), not 12 hours. Your pediatrician, not a search result, decides whether a night feed can go.

**Is there a 13–14 week sleep regression?**
No official one by that name. See [13 week old baby sleep](/13-week-old-baby-sleep), [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months), and [sleep regression at 3 months](/sleep-regression/3-months).

**Should I drop a night feed at 14 weeks?**
Only with your pediatrician's advice. A long stretch on one night is not the same as a feeding plan.
