# 12 Month Old Sleep Schedule: A Day in the Life of a One-Year-Old

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Happy first birthday. This page is for planning an **ordinary day** for a one-year-old: total sleep, nap times, wake windows, bedtime, and the practical changes that arrive at 12 months, like whole milk, cups, and a few things that are now allowed in the crib. If your child's sleep has recently gone off the rails, with new night wakings or a sudden refusal to nap, start with our [12 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/12-months) page instead; it also walks through the "two naps or one?" decision in detail.

## Short answer

Sleep guidance switches age bands at the first birthday. The **AASM** recommends **12–16 hours** for 4–12 months and **11–14 hours** for 1–2 years, naps included; the CDC uses the same ranges. Most 12-month-olds still take **two naps**: KidsHealth says most toddlers move to one nap **around 18 months or sometimes sooner**, and Boys Town Pediatrics puts the usual switch at **15–18 months**. Published wake windows for 10–12 months are **3–6 hours** (Cleveland Clinic). Bedtime in most examples falls between about **6:30 and 8 p.m.**

## How much should a 1 year old sleep?

On the first birthday the official range shifts from 12–16 hours (AASM, 4–12 months) to 11–14 hours (AASM, 1–2 years). Your child doesn't need an hour less overnight; the bands just overlap. KidsHealth describes 9–12 hours at night for 8–12 months, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby gives 10–12 hours at night after 12 months.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry aims for at least 13.25 hours, with 11–12 hours at night and 2–3 hours over 2 naps.

## 12 month old nap schedule

Plan on **two naps**. Huckleberry (app example) says 12 months is typically too early to drop to one nap, and both KidsHealth (about 18 months, sometimes sooner) and Boys Town (15–18 months) put the usual switch later. Mayo Clinic is the outlier: it says babies of 10–12 months often drop the morning nap.

What a two-nap day usually looks like at this age:

- A morning nap starting roughly 3–3.5 hours after waking *(our reading of the app examples)*.
- An early-afternoon nap after lunch.
- Each nap somewhere between 40 minutes and 2 hours. Boys Town describes two-nap days with naps of about 40 minutes to 2 hours each.

If your one-year-old has started fighting one nap, the full comparison of two-nap and one-nap days is on the [12 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/12-months) page. The [15 month old sleep schedule](/15-month-old-sleep-schedule) explains how the switch to one nap usually goes.

| Huckleberry (app example) | 12 months | 3.25–4 hours |

Most one-year-olds on two naps sit around **3.25–4 hours**, with the longest stretch before bed *(our reading of the app examples)*. These are not clinical figures; there is no AAP or AASM wake-window standard.

## Milk, cups, and bottles at one year

These changes don't decide sleep, but they shape the evening routine:

- **Cow's milk.** HealthyChildren.org says children of 12–24 months can drink whole milk, about 16–24 ounces (2–3 cups) a day, plus 8–32 ounces of water. Breastfeeding can continue for as long as you both want.
- **Juice.** Not needed; the CDC says no more than 4 ounces of 100% juice a day if you give it.
- **Bottles.** The AAP suggests finishing the move from bottle to cup between 12 and 18 months and dropping the nap-time and bedtime bottles gradually. HealthyChildren.org warns that giving in to a bedtime bottle can turn a short protest into a longer struggle; offer extra cuddles, songs, or stories instead.
- **Teeth.** Brush, Book, Bed (AAP): the toothbrush should be the last thing to touch the teeth before bed.

## The crib after the first birthday

- **Blanket and lovey.** KidsHealth says that after the first birthday a light blanket and a small "lovey" are OK in the crib, but not extra-large soft toys.
- **Comfort object.** Nationwide Children's Hospital suggests introducing a security object by 12 months and including it in the bedtime routine. The CDC's 1-year tips also suggest bringing a favorite toy or blanket when your child meets a new caregiver.
- **Bumpers.** Nationwide Children's advises removing crib bumpers by about 12 months, when babies can begin to climb.
- **Climbing.** KidsHealth suggests keeping the mattress on the lowest setting and not leaving toys that can be stacked to climb on.

## Bedtime routine for a one-year-old

The CDC's 1-year tips stress routines for sleeping and feeding. A one-year-old routine can be the AAP's Brush, Book, Bed in its simplest form: milk earlier in the evening, bath if it's a bath night, brush teeth, one or two books, the same goodnight phrase, lights out. KidsHealth suggests deciding how long the routine should be and keeping it from growing night by night. In a randomized trial of children aged 7–36 months, a consistent routine helped them fall asleep faster and wake less (Mindell et al., 2009).

## What can make a one-year-old's schedule harder

- Dropping to one nap before your child is ready, which can lead to an overtired evening.
- A bottle or cup of milk in the crib (HealthyChildren.org).
- Screens in the hour before bed (KidsHealth).
- A routine that keeps getting longer (KidsHealth).

## When to talk to your pediatrician

See your doctor if your child snores loudly or seems to pause breathing, is very hard to wake, isn't pulling up to stand, or has lost skills they had before. Mention ongoing night wakings or early waking if they're wearing you down.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps does a 12 month old need?**  
Usually two. Most children move to one nap between about 15 and 18 months (Boys Town; KidsHealth).

**How much sleep does a 1 year old need?**  
The AASM recommends 11–14 hours a day, naps included, for ages 1–2.

**What are 12 month wake windows?**  
Around 3.25–4 hours in app examples; Cleveland Clinic gives 3–6 hours for 10–12 months.

**What time should a 1 year old go to bed?**  
Most example schedules land between 6:30 and 8 p.m. Earlier after a short afternoon nap.

**Can my 1 year old have a blanket in the crib?**  
KidsHealth says a light blanket and a small lovey are OK after the first birthday.
