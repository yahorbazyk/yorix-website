# Can a Newborn Sleep Too Much? Long Sleep vs Hard to Wake

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Newborns can look as if they do little except sleep, and still be well. This page answers the fear behind **can a 2 week old sleep too much** and **can a newborn sleep too much**. It is not [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby) (the feeding-clock rules) and not [2 week old baby sleep](/2-week-old-baby-sleep) (the week snapshot), though you will need both. If sleep has *suddenly* increased in an older young baby, also see [why is my baby suddenly sleeping more](/why-is-my-baby-suddenly-sleeping-more).

## Short answer

**Yes, a newborn can sleep a lot and be fine — and yes, sometimes "too much" is a warning.** The useful test is not a stopwatch total by itself. Healthy newborns often sit in a **wide** 24-hour range: KidsHealth quotes the National Sleep Foundation **14–17 hours** for 0–3 months; Sleep Foundation and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby give **11–19**; NHS pages include totals from about **8 to 18**. HealthyChildren.org describes **about 16–17 hours**, often in short pieces. A baby who seems to "sleep all day" can still be inside those bands.

What matters more:

- They **wake to feed**, or you can wake them for the feeds your pediatrician still wants (KidsHealth: often every 3–4 hours in the first weeks until weight gain is good; HealthyChildren.org formula Q&A: wake in the first month if a formula-fed baby sleeps more than 4–5 hours).
- Wet diapers are in the range you were given (HealthyChildren.org: at least 5–6 a day after days 4–5).
- When they are awake, they have some alert, responsive moments.
- They are not **hard to rouse**, floppy, poorly feeding, or jaundiced in a way that worries you.

If the last line is true, this is not a "lucky good sleeper" story. It is a same-day call.

### A simple triage

| What you are seeing | What it often is | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sleeps many hours, but wakes or can be woken to feed, has enough wet diapers, has some alert time | Often ordinary newborn sleep | Follow your wake-to-feed plan; see the week or newborn page |
| A one-off 5-hour stretch at 2 weeks | Can be normal; may still need a planned wake | [Should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby) |
| You cannot wake them, they go limp at the breast or bottle, fewer wet diapers, yellow skin, or very weak cry | A clinical warning, not a schedule tweak | Call the pediatrician now; emergency care if you cannot rouse them or breathing is wrong |

## What parents often mean by "too much"

In What to Expect and Reddit threads, the same picture repeats: a 2-week-old who seems to sleep 18–22 hours, or "all the time," and a parent who is scared to enjoy it. We will not treat those hour counts as data. The questions those parents actually use as a test are the ones in the table above — wake, feed, diapers, alertness — not a forum total.

Day-night confusion can add to the illusion: long daytime sleeps and a wide-awake evening look like "they slept too much today." That pattern has its own page: [newborn day-night confusion](/newborn-day-night-confusion).

Jaundice deserves a special mention in the first two weeks. Sleepy, hard-to-feed babies with yellow skin need the plan their clinician already gave, or a call if they have not been seen. This page will not diagnose jaundice.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call if you cannot wake them for a feed, if they are feeding poorly, if wet diapers drop, if they look yellow, grey, or blue, if they are floppy, or if something feels wrong even if you cannot name it. In the U.S., 911 if a baby cannot be woken or is struggling to breathe. Do not wait for the next scheduled visit to ask about a baby you cannot rouse.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a 2 month old sleep too much during the day?**  
The same triage applies, with a slightly more mature baby. Long day sleep that wrecks nights is a timing issue ([should you wake](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby) has a nap section). Hard to rouse is still a call.

**Is 22 hours of sleep normal?**  
There is no official 22-hour target. Some newborns sleep a very large share of the day and are well. The ranges above already disagree with each other, which is why behaviour, feeding, and diapers matter more than beating a number.

**Should I keep waking them at night after the first two weeks?**  
Only if your pediatrician still wants that. The rules live on the wake-to-feed page.
