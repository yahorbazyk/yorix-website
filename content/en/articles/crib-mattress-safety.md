# Crib Mattress Safety: Firm, Flat, and Made for That Crib

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

"Is this mattress firm enough — and does it fit?" is a different question from the full safe-sleep checklist. This page is the **mattress**. The short definition on [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies) now points here. Play-yard pads have an extra rule — only the pad that came with that yard — on [pack-and-play sleep](/pack-and-play-sleep). We do not rank brands.

## Short answer

AAP 2022: use a **firm, flat, noninclined** surface. **Firm** means the mattress **maintains its shape and does not indent or conform to the infant's head**. The surface should not change shape when the fitted sheet for that model is on, **so that there are no gaps between the mattress and the wall** of the crib, bassinet, portable crib, or play yard. **Use only a mattress designed for that specific product.** Pillows and cushions are not mattresses. **Mattress toppers** meant to make the surface softer are not appropriate under 1 year. A waterproof cover, if used, should be **tightly fitting and thin**. Nothing should go under or over the mattress to create an angle.

The same recommendations warn that **soft mattresses, including those with adjustable firmness or memory foam**, can make a pocket and raise the risk of rebreathing or suffocation if the baby is prone. Many mattresses meant for older children or adults contain memory foam — do not move one of those into a crib.

CPSC has a mandatory **crib-mattress** standard, 16 CFR part 1241 (currently incorporating ASTM F2933, with CPSC modifications). For a **full-size** crib mattress, the standard includes minimum length and width and a *laboratory* **corner-gap** test. As of the incorporation opened for this page, that lab limit is **not more than 3.15 inches (80 mm)** in the specified test fixture, with a test sheet on the mattress. **That number is a manufacturer test, not a parent instruction to leave a 3-inch gap.** Do not "aim for" the lab figure at home.

Some public-health explainers (for example Michigan DHHS, in infant-sleep-product advice) describe a home check: with the sheet on, there should not be a gap greater than **two fingers** between the slats or mesh and the mattress, and the surface should bounce back when you press. We report that as *state public-health wording*, not as the federal lab method, and we do not average it with 3.15 inches. If a mattress moves easily or you can see a gap a baby's face or body could slip into, it does not meet the AAP "no gaps" description — replace it with one made for that crib.

### Types, and what "type" cannot fix

| Type you will see in a store | What matters | What does not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foam, innerspring, or a dual-firmness ("infant / toddler") design | For the infant side: firm, flat, no indent, correct size for *this* crib, meets 16 CFR 1241 if it is a crib mattress | A "two-sided" marketing line does not make memory foam safe in the first year |
| Waterproof cover or thin mattress protector | Tight and thin, if you use one | A quilted pad or thick protector that changes firmness or fit |
| Aftermarket play-yard or bassinet mattress | CPSC: for a play yard, use only the mattress supplied with it (or the model's own replacement). Aftermarket thicker pads can leave a gap | "Extra comfort" is the hazard |
| A toddler or adult mattress moved early | Not appropriate as an infant crib mattress if it is soft, adjustable, or memory foam | Waiting until a toddler bed is a separate decision — [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed) |

## Fit, height, and used mattresses

Use the mattress **at the height the crib manual specifies**, and lower it when the baby can push up or stand. A high mattress in a mobile baby is a fall, not a firmness problem.

Do not use a stained, sagging, or cut-to-fit pad. Do not add pool noodles or blankets around a too-small mattress to "fill the gap." Check CPSC / SaferProducts.gov for recalls.

## When to get help

If you are unsure whether a secondhand crib and mattress belong together, it is safer to stop and check the model numbers than to "make it work." Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**How firm should a crib mattress be?**  
It should not indent around the baby's head. If a handprint stays, it is too soft.

**Is a little give all right?**  
AAP's wording is "does not indent or conform." We will not invent a millimetre of acceptable sag.

**Can I use the two-finger test?**  
Some public-health pages describe it. It is not the federal lab test. If you can see or feel a gap, or the mattress is loose in the frame, do not use that combination.

**Is a firmer mattress bad for their head shape?**  
A firm flat mattress is the safe-sleep surface. Supervised [tummy time](/tummy-time) is the usual strategy for positional flattening, not a softer bed.
