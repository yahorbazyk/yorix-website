# What Time Should a Baby Go to Bed? There Is No Official Clock

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

"What time should my baby go to bed?" is a clock question. This page is that question. It will not invent a 7:00 p.m. rule, and it will not paste a second sample day — those already live on the month and week pages and on [baby bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby).

## Short answer

**There is no official AAP clock time** that every baby of a given age should be in bed. What official sources do publish are **24-hour sleep ranges**, and even those do not all agree: National Sleep Foundation 14–17 hours at 0–3 months versus Sleep Foundation and Pediatric Sleep Council 11–19, versus NHS-style ~8–18, versus Health Canada ~16–17. We do not average them. From **4 months**, the AASM 12–16 hour recommendation (including naps) is the later-infant band we already use on [how much sleep does a baby need](/how-much-sleep-does-a-baby-need). Do not back-fit AASM 12–16 onto weeks 1–15.

A useful bedtime is one that, together with naps, lands in a band that works for *this* baby, and that sits after a predictable wind-down. Many families land somewhere in the early evening as nights lengthen, but a household that works later — or earlier on a short-nap day — is not breaking an official rule.

## Why "7 p.m." became a folklore answer

Parent blogs often repeat a narrow early-evening window because it is easy to remember and because some toddlers do better with a slightly earlier night than adults keep. That is folklore, not an AAP protocol. The body clock does begin to organize in the early months (Pediatric Sleep Council often describes a start around 4–6 weeks; Sleep Foundation discusses melatonin and cortisol around 8–9 weeks — already reported on week pages, and not a bedtime clock). Light in the morning and a dimmer evening still help, with or without a 7:00 p.m. target.

If last nap ended late, an earlier official-looking bedtime can collide with "not tired yet." If last nap was very short, a slightly earlier night can be the overtired experiment — see [overtired baby](/overtired-baby). Move one variable at a time.

## When the clock is not the first problem

Hunger, illness, pain, and a bedroom that is not a safe, boring place to sleep will not be fixed by moving bedtime 30 minutes. Safe sleep stays: back, firm, flat, clear. Newborns still need night feeds; do not pick a bedtime that pretends otherwise. See [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby).

## When to get clinical advice

Ask if a very late bedtime is new *and* they seem unwell, if 24-hour sleep is far outside the published bands and they look exhausted, or if you cannot find any consistent window after a few weeks of a calm routine. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**What time should a 6 month old go to bed?**  
There is no official hour. Use the [6 month sleep schedule](/6-month-old-sleep-schedule) for the 24-hour picture and a sample day, not this page as a second timetable.

**Is 8 or 9 p.m. too late?**  
Not as an official rule. If they are short on 24-hour sleep, or mornings are 11 a.m., you may try a slightly earlier wind-down. If they are well-rested and the house runs later, that can still be fine.

**Should bedtime be the same every night?**  
A consistent *routine* is more clearly supported than a rigid clock. Travel, illness, and a crashed nap will move the hour. See [baby bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby).

**Do toddlers need an earlier bedtime than babies?**  
Sometimes the one-nap day means an earlier night than a three-nap baby had. Sometimes it does not. Use the toddler and 18-month pages, not a single clock.
