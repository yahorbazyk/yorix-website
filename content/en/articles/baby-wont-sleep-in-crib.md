# Baby Won't Sleep in the Bassinet or Crib

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Plenty of babies settle in arms, in a wrap, or on a chest and then protest the moment they meet the mattress. This page is about **night sleep in the bassinet or crib** — the "put them down and they wake" problem. [Contact naps](/contact-naps) already covers held daytime sleep and a nap-time move to the crib; we will not repeat that safety essay. The furniture switch from a bassinet that has been outgrown is on [when to move baby to the crib](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib).

## Short answer

A firm, flat bassinet or crib is the safe place for unattended sleep. It is also a less interesting, less womb-like place than a parent. Young babies often startle (the Moro reflex), miss motion and warmth, or have already linked sleep with feeding or rocking ([sleep associations](/sleep-associations), [nursing to sleep](/nursing-to-sleep)). None of that makes the couch, an inclined sleeper, a cushioned nest, or a car seat a better overnight plan. See [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies) and [baby sleeping in a car seat](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat).

What usually helps, once hunger, a dirty diaper, and illness are checked, is a boringly consistent put-down: the same short routine, the baby placed down **drowsy but not fully gone** when they are old enough for that step (HealthyChildren discusses this from about 4 months), and a slow transfer from arms if they are younger. Swaddling can reduce startle in babies who are not yet rolling — see [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby). If reflux is in the picture, they still go down flat on the back; a wedge is not the fix ([reflux and baby sleep](/reflux-and-baby-sleep)).

## Why the crib loses and your arms win

A parent's body provides heat, smell, sound, and a slight sway. A mattress provides none of those. Add a startle as the arms flail, a room that is darker or quieter than the living room, or a baby who is already past a reasonable wake stretch ([overtired baby](/overtired-baby)), and the crib can look like a sudden demotion.

For older infants, the protest is more often a learned sequence: they know how sleep starts, and the crib is not it. That is an association problem more than a "wrong mattress" problem. A new bassinet insert, a nest, or a wedge will not teach a different start, and several of those products work against safe sleep.

Check the simple list first anyway: last feed, gas after a feed (without turning this into a [colic](/colic) workup unless the crying fits), room temperature, a swaddle that has come loose, or a bassinet that is no longer the right size.

## A night-by-night way to practice the mattress

There is no official AAP "bassinet training" protocol. A cautious approach that stays inside sourced advice:

1. **Keep the sleep space legal.** Firm, flat, on the back, empty except for a fitted sheet. If you have been finishing nights on the sofa or in a reclined product, move those sleeps to the bassinet or crib even if the first stretch is short.
2. **Use a short, repeatable wind-down.** NHS and HealthyChildren both lean on a calm, predictable sequence more than on a gadget.
3. **For a very young baby,** many families feed, hold for a few minutes, then transfer once the baby is heavy and relaxed, keeping them on their back as they go down. If they startle, a hand on the chest for a moment can help; if they fully wake, you can resettle and try a slightly earlier transfer next time rather than giving up on the bassinet for the night.
4. **From about 4 months,** HealthyChildren's drowsy-but-awake suggestion starts to apply if you want crib sleep to last through the first waking. That does not require a formal method on night one.
5. **Try the first stretch of the night in the bassinet**, when sleep pressure is highest, even if later stretches still involve more help while you practice.
6. **Nights and naps can move at different speeds.** A contact nap can still be a useful tool (on the terms in that article) while nights happen in the crib.

If you are using a wrap or sling, the baby should be moved to the bassinet or crib for unattended sleep. Do not unbuckle a car seat and leave them in it in the house.

## What not to do

- Do not add pillows, rolled towels, positioners, or an incline "so they feel you."
- Do not leave a sleeping baby on a sofa, armchair, or adult bed unattended.
- Do not keep swaddling after rolling starts.
- Do not start [cry it out](/cry-it-out-method) with a newborn because the bassinet is going badly.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if the baby never gets any stretch in the bassinet despite a consistent try, if breathing, color, or weight gain worries you, if pain or reflux seems to be in the way, or if you are so exhausted that you are falling asleep in a chair with the baby. That last situation is a safety issue, not a discipline issue — make a plan with your clinician and any support you can get.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my newborn only sleep on me?**  
Because you are warm, slightly moving, and familiar. It is common. The job is to make the bassinet the place they finish that sleep, not to make your body unsafe by accident.

**Should I switch to a crib sooner?**  
Only when the bassinet's limits say so, or when you are ready and the crib is set up safely. A bigger mattress does not automatically solve a put-down problem. See [when to move baby to the crib](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib).

**Is a second bassinet in the living room a good idea?**  
A second *safe* sleep space can help you put the baby down where you actually are, as long as it meets the same firm, flat, empty-surface rules. It is not a loophole for sitting devices.

**Can I swaddle to get them to accept the bassinet?**  
If they are not rolling and the wrap is hip-healthy, swaddling can reduce startle. It is a tool, not a requirement. See [how to swaddle a baby](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby).
