# The 3-to-2 Nap Transition: A Practical Plan

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **process** page: what to do when a third nap starts to fail. It is not a second nap chart. How many naps are typical at each age already lives on [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age), and the 6- to 9-month schedule pages already show example days. Use those for the map; use this page for the messy week of the switch.

## Short answer

There is no official AAP week when every baby must drop from three naps to two. Mayo Clinic suggests cutting a **late-afternoon third nap at about 9 months**, so bedtime can come earlier. Huckleberry, which we treat as an app example rather than a clinical source, describes many babies finishing the switch by about 8 months. Ireland's HSE says children usually reduce to two naps a day at **6 to 9 months**. Those windows overlap but are not identical, so think "sometime in the second half of the first year," driven by your baby's day, not a birthday. When the third nap is refused, or it pushes bedtime too late, let the first two naps be the day's sleep, and **bring bedtime earlier** on no-third-nap days.

## Signs the third nap is done — or not

Practical signs, not a clinical checklist:

**Probably ready**

- The third nap is fought or skipped on most days for a week or more.
- When it happens, bedtime slides too late, or the night gets worse.
- On days you skip it and offer an earlier bedtime, your baby copes.

**Probably not yet**

- The first two naps are still very short, and late afternoon is a wreck without a third rest.
- Your baby is younger than the usual window and is still happily taking three naps.
- Illness, vaccines, or [travel](/baby-sleep-and-travel) is temporarily scrambling days. Wait if you can.

This drop is not the same as the later [2-to-1 transition](/2-to-1-nap-transition). Doing both in the same month is usually too much.

## A practical plan (not an official 7-day protocol)

No medical body publishes a day-by-day 3-to-2 script, so treat the steps below as a working plan, not a prescription.

1. **Watch a week, don't panic after one skip.** One refused third nap is information, not a verdict.
2. **Protect the first two naps.** If your baby will take a slightly longer morning or midday nap, that makes the late-afternoon gap easier. Do not cap those naps just to "save" a third one that is already failing.
3. **On days without a third nap, move bedtime earlier.** The goal is to avoid a long, [overtired](/overtired-baby) evening. A 30- to 60-minute shift is a common practical move; Mayo Clinic uses a half-hour shift when discussing a later nap drop.
4. **Keep the [routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) the same**, just earlier.
5. **Expect mixed days** for a week or two: some three-nap days, some two. That is the transition, not a failure.
6. **If two-nap days look worse after a week or two** — much more night waking, very early mornings, or a baby who falls apart by 4 p.m. even with an early bedtime — put the third nap back for a while and try again later.

If you want to see a three-nap day and a two-nap day side by side, use the [7-month sleep schedule](/7-month-old-sleep-schedule) and the [8-month](/8-month-old-sleep-schedule) page, not a new chart here.

## What can go wrong

- Dropping the third nap because a schedule you saw online said "8 months," while your baby still needs it.
- Keeping a late third nap that lands at 5 p.m. and then wondering why bedtime is 9:30.
- Treating every short mid-transition nap as a new problem. See [short naps](/short-naps) if 30-minute naps were already the pattern.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if your baby is well past 9 months, still seems to need three naps, and nights or growth worry you, or if sleep fell apart and is not recovering after you put a nap back.

## Frequently asked questions

**When do babies go from 3 naps to 2?**  
Often in the 6- to 9-month range, with Mayo Clinic highlighting about 9 months for cutting a late-afternoon nap. Individual babies differ.

**Should I wake them from the second nap to "fit in" a third?**  
Usually no, if the third nap is the one that is failing. Let the two better naps stand and use an earlier bedtime.

**How long does the transition last?**  
Often a messy week or two of mixed days. There is no official duration.

**Is this the 8-month regression?**  
Not necessarily. Sleep can wobble when naps change, and [8-month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/8-months) has its own page. Fix the day first; don't assume a named regression.
