# The 2-to-1 Nap Transition: How to Make the Switch

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

The move from two naps to one is one of the noisier toddler projects. This page is the **method**: when to start, how to slide the morning nap later, and what to do on mixed days. It is not a second [15-month sleep schedule](/15-month-old-sleep-schedule) — that page already has sample days and a shorter step list. It is not the [18-month](/18-month-old-sleep-schedule) "life on one nap" timetable, and it is not a copy of the brief 2-to-1 section on [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age).

## Short answer

Sources do **not** agree on a single month. Boys Town National Research Hospital describes **15 to 18 months** as the most common window. KidsHealth says most toddlers are down to one nap **by 18 months**. Sleep Foundation uses **18 to 24 months**. Mayo Clinic gives an earlier picture: babies **10 to 12 months** often drop the morning nap, and you can try moving the afternoon nap and bedtime up by about half an hour. We treat **about 12 to 18 months** as the honest range, with mid-toddlerhood as typical, and we do not average Mayo with Boys Town into a fake official week. KidsHealth's clearest sign: a toddler who **fights the morning nap** is probably ready for just an afternoon nap. Slide the morning nap later until it becomes a midday nap, move lunch earlier, and use an earlier bedtime while the new nap stretches.

## Are they ready?

**Probably ready** (practical signs, aligned with KidsHealth and with the readiness list on our 15-month page, not a clinical checklist):

- The morning nap is refused or very short on most days for a week or two.
- When the morning nap does happen, the afternoon nap disappears or bedtime becomes very late.
- They cope reasonably until lunch without that morning sleep.

**Probably not yet:**

- They are a wreck by mid-morning on a skipped morning nap.
- Nights or early mornings get clearly worse when you try.
- They are ill, teething hard, in a new daycare, or otherwise in a bad week. Wait.

If they just finished the [3-to-2 transition](/3-to-2-nap-transition) a few weeks ago, give the two-nap day time to work before you start this one.

## A step-by-step plan

Boys Town describes the usual pattern: you gradually keep the child up longer in the morning so two naps (for example around 9:30 a.m. and 2:30 p.m.) become one midday nap closer to noon, and lunch moves earlier. A practical sequence based on that description — not an official medical protocol:

1. **Push the morning nap later** by a small step every few days (15–30 minutes is a workable increment, the same kind of step we already use on the 15-month page).
2. **Watch the afternoon nap.** If it is crowding bedtime, shorten it slightly rather than letting the whole evening drift. (Sleep Foundation, in a related nap article, prefers shortening a nap to pushing bedtime later when the nap is the problem.)
3. **Move lunch earlier** so they are not starting the new midday nap hungry.
4. **When the "morning" nap lands around late morning to noon, it has become the one nap.** Protect it. The [18-month](/18-month-old-sleep-schedule) page is the picture of a settled version of this day.
5. **Bring bedtime earlier for a stretch.** Mayo Clinic's half-hour shift is a reasonable starting move; some toddlers need more than that on hard days.
6. **Allow mixed days.** Some days will still need two shorter naps. Sleep Foundation notes that a child may go days with one nap and other days with two during a transition. Follow the day's cues instead of ripping up the plan after one odd afternoon.

**Daycare.** Boys Town points out that the room's nap time may not match your child's readiness. Ask how they handle toddlers who are mid-switch, and use weekends to offer the nap your child still needs.

There is no second sample-day timetable on this page on purpose. If you want clock-by-clock examples, use [15 month old sleep schedule](/15-month-old-sleep-schedule).

## If it is not working

Put the morning nap back for a week or two if your toddler is falling apart every late morning, nights have become much worse, or the new "one nap" is a 30-minute collapse followed by a dreadful evening. Try again later. A failed attempt is information about timing, not a character test.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if you are well past 18 to 24 months and two naps still seem required, if total sleep is short of the 11–14 hour AASM toddler range, or if snoring or very restless sleep is part of the picture.

## Frequently asked questions

**When do babies go from 2 naps to 1?**  
Often between 12 and 18 months, sometimes later. Mayo Clinic describes an earlier morning-nap drop than Boys Town or KidsHealth. Follow your child and the signs above.

**How long does the 2-to-1 transition take?**  
Often a few weeks of mixed days. There is no official duration.

**Should I drop to one nap at 12 months?**  
Only if the signs are there. A first birthday is not a deadline.

**What if daycare already has one nap?**  
You may need an earlier bedtime, and some weekend two-nap days, until home and care align better. See also [baby won't nap](/baby-wont-nap).
