# Why Won't My Baby Sleep? Start Here, Then Follow One Link

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **routing page**, not a second guide to nights, naps, or training. If your baby is fighting sleep or simply not sleeping, use the questions below and open the matching article. We will not retell [baby waking at night](/survive-night-wakings-new-parents), [overtired baby](/overtired-baby), or the schedule pages here.

## Short answer

"Won't sleep" is usually one of a few patterns, not one mystery. Name the pattern first — *when* it happens, *how old* the baby is, and *what else* is going on — then go to the page that already owns that problem. If you are worried about breathing, color, a fever, poor feeding, or a baby who is hard to wake, skip the tree and contact a clinician or emergency services.

## What this hub will not do

- It will not give a new sample day. Use the age schedule pages and [how much sleep a baby needs](/how-much-sleep-does-a-baby-need).
- It will not repeat safe-sleep rules. If the current workaround is a sofa, a seat, or a wedge, go to [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies) first.
- It will not diagnose teething, hunger, or a medical cause. See [teething](/teething-symptoms) only as one possible thread, and call the pediatrician if you are unsure.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call promptly for breathing trouble, color change, fever in a young infant, poor feeding, far fewer wet diapers, or a baby who is unusually sleepy or difficult to rouse. Ask at a visit if sleep has been a struggle for weeks and none of the usual patterns above seem to fit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is my baby fighting sleep?**  
Often they are overtired, undertired, hungry, or used to falling asleep in a different place. Use the table: cues and overtired for "wired" protests, associations and the crib page for "only in arms," and the newborn pages if they are still very young.

**Why won't my newborn sleep unless I hold them?**  
That is extremely common. Start at [newborn sleep schedule](/newborn-sleep-schedule) and [baby won't sleep in the crib](/baby-wont-sleep-in-crib), not at a formal training method.

**Is this a regression?**  
Only if the change is a time-limited bump around a typical age or skill. Use the regression hub and the age-specific page. An ongoing pattern (crib protest, short naps, night feeds) usually has its own article.
