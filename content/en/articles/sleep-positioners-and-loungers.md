# Sleep Positioners, Loungers, and Inclined Sleepers Are Not a Crib

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Wedges, docks, "nests," and inclined sleepers are often marketed as if they were a safer or cozier crib. This page is that category. It is not a brand review, and it is not [baby swings and bouncers](/baby-swings-and-bouncers) (motion seats meant for play). The everyday ABC rules stay on [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies).

## Short answer

AAP 2022: to be considered for infant sleep, a product should meet federal safety standards for a **crib, bassinet, play yard, or bedside sleeper**. Products that do not — including many **inclined sleep products, hammocks, in-bed sleepers, nests and pods, compact bassinets without a stand, travel bassinets, and baby tents** — are **not recommended**. Sleep surfaces with inclines of **more than 10 degrees** are unsafe.

In 2022 the U.S. **Safe Sleep for Babies Act** banned the sale of **inclined infant sleepers** that lift the head more than 10 degrees, and also banned certain **crib bumpers**. HealthyChildren.org (Dina DiMaggio, MD, FAAP, and Rachel Y. Moon, MD, FAAP, updated 29 May 2026) explains that these sleepers can let the head slump and cut off air, and that banned items still show up secondhand.

CPSC has recalled inclined sleepers after infant deaths. The Fisher-Price Rock 'n Play Sleeper was recalled on **12 April 2019** (about **4.7 million** units) and the recall was **reannounced on 9 January 2023** after further deaths. CPSC's reannouncement describes **approximately 100 deaths** reported in connection with that product, including **at least eight** after the original recall; Fisher-Price has said that in some reports it could not confirm the circumstances or the exact product. A second inclined sleeper (Kids2 Rocking Sleeper) was reannounced the same day; the CPSC chair's statement cites **15** reported deaths in that product, including **four** after its recall. Stop using recalled inclined sleepers; it is illegal to sell them. Check SaferProducts.gov for the model you have.

### What sits in this "not a sleep space" category

| Product type | Why it is in this article | What to do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sleep positioners, bolsters, anti-roll wedges | HealthyChildren.org: linked with deaths; babies can become trapped; no evidence they prevent SIDS or flat head | Back, flat, clear — [tummy time](/tummy-time) is for *awake* time |
| Infant loungers and docks (soft padded nests) | HC: infants have suffocated or rolled off; limit to waking hours, or use a flat play mat | A firm, flat, approved sleep surface |
| Nursing pillows | HC: helpful for feeding, never for sleep; deaths have occurred when they were used as a bed | Move the baby after the feed |
| Inclined sleepers, "rocking sleepers," some pods and wedges sold for sleep | Incline over 10° is unsafe; U.S. law bans inclined infant sleepers; major CPSC recalls | Do not use for sleep, even if you still own one |
| Hammocks, in-bed sleepers, nests, and similar, if they do not meet an approved standard | AAP 2022: not recommended unless they meet the federal crib/bassinet/play-yard/bedside-sleeper rule | An approved crib, bassinet, play yard, or bedside sleeper |

We name Dock-A-Tot-style loungers and Boppy-style nursing pillows only because HealthyChildren.org uses them as category examples, not as a review.

## What marketing gets wrong

"Helps with reflux," "prevents rolling," "prevents SIDS," or "physician designed" does not override the standard. AAP already advises against raising the mattress or using positioners for reflux or a cold — see [reflux and baby sleep](/reflux-and-baby-sleep) and [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep). Flat-head prevention is supervised awake [tummy time](/tummy-time), not a bolster in the crib.

Secondhand and handmade versions count. A garage-sale sleeper can still be a recalled or banned product.

## When to get help

If you have been using a recalled inclined sleeper, stop now and ask your pediatrician if you have questions about what to use instead. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a lounger if I am sitting next to it?**  
HealthyChildren.org limits loungers to waking hours and still warns of rolloff and suffocation. A nap that starts there should move to an approved flat space.

**Are all "nests" illegal?**  
The Safe Sleep for Babies Act bans inclined infant sleepers (and certain bumpers). Other nests and in-bed sleepers are not recommended by AAP unless they meet an approved federal sleep-product standard. When in doubt, do not use them for sleep.

**Can a positioner stop a flat head?**  
HealthyChildren.org: there is no evidence that they prevent flat head syndrome. Use supervised tummy time.

**What if I already own a recalled Rock 'n Play or similar sleeper?**  
Stop using it. Follow the current CPSC recall notice for the remedy, and do not sell or give it away.
