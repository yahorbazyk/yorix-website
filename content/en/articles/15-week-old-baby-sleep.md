# 15 Week Old Baby Sleep: The Week Before 4 Months, Not a Second 4-Month Page

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 15 snapshot**, the last week page in this series. It is not [14 week old baby sleep](/14-week-old-baby-sleep), and it is not a second [4 month old sleep schedule](/4-month-old-sleep-schedule) or a second [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months). From week 16, use those month guides. Use this page only for the week you are in.

## Short answer

You are about seven days short of the usual "4 months = 16 weeks" label. No guideline publishes a 15-week hour target. On one side is KidsHealth's NSF figure of **14–17 hours** for 0–3 months; on the other is AASM's **12–16 hours** from 4 months. Both remain listed. They are not blended, and AASM is not treated as already in force at day 105.

This is the week when many families first feel the pattern the 4-month regression page describes: naps that need rescuing at 30 minutes, nights that fragment into 45–90 minute pieces after a previously longer stretch, more startles. That mechanism — maturing sleep cycles — is explained there. This snapshot only names the week-15 version of the complaint and points to the relevant month guides.

Rolling can be starting. If it is, stop swaddling now. Formal sleep training is generally discussed around 4–6 months (Sleep Foundation); week 15 is the early edge of that conversation, not an automatic start date.

### The last week page, one week short of the 4-month band

- **Hours:** no official week-15 total. The 0–3 month 14–17 hour NSF range (KidsHealth) and the 4-month 12–16 hour AASM range (from about 16 weeks) sit on either side of this week.
- **The phrase:** a 5–6 hour stretch is still the usual sourced meaning of "through the night" in KidsHealth's 1–3 month language.
- **Training:** you are at the early edge of the window Sleep Foundation usually discusses (toward 4–6 months). That is not an automatic start date.
- **Rolling:** drop the swaddle once rolling starts (HealthyChildren.org / AAP).

## What is usually going on at fifteen weeks

You are on the doorstep of the 4-month month view. Wake windows on that month page are not copied here. Days may still have three or four naps, often short unless rescued with a hold or a contact second half. Nights may look like the 4-month change a week early, or they may still look like week 12.

If a swaddle is still in use and they are rolling to the side or towards the tummy, drop the swaddle and keep them on the back to start sleep, in a clear crib.

## Rescued naps, fragmented nights, and rolling

r/sleeptrain threads at 15 weeks often describe three weeks of "rescued" naps and nights that went from two feeds to many 45–90 minute wakings, plus the question of whether to start training now or wait until 4.5 months. Those recurring dilemmas shaped this week's examples. They do not set a required length for a "regression."

- **Naps that only continue if you rescue them.** At about 30 minutes they stir, and a hand, a bounce, or a hold may buy another stretch. Treating every 30-minute nap as a failure is exhausting. See [short naps](/short-naps). The 4-month page covers short naps at 4 months in more depth.
- **Nights every 45–90 minutes after a previously calmer month.** This is the community picture that often gets the 4-month name a little early. Use [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months) for the explanation and the "how long" discussion. This page will not invent a week-15 duration.
- **Rolling plus a swaddle, or startles that returned.** Safe sleep comes first. Arms-out or a sleep bag, back to start, clear crib.
- **"Do we start training this week?"** Read [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training). Sleep Foundation's usual window is 4–6 months. Your pediatrician and your own threshold matter more than a forum timeline.

## A swaddle-out night (example only)

Not the 4-month sample schedule and not week 14's "Night A / Night B" contrast. Suppose the last swaddle night is already behind you. Tonight they start on the back in a sleep bag, stir at 9:05 p.m. after an 8:20 bedtime (a false-start shape), resettle with a brief hold, then wake at 10:40, 12:15, 1:50, and 3:40, sometimes having turned toward the side. A feed still happens once. The point of the sketch is a rolling, arms-out night, not a clock plan for tomorrow, and not a copy of the 4-month timetable.

## Rolling, feeds, and training — what to ask first

Ask before you drop a night feed or start a formal training plan. Get help promptly if they are very hard to wake, if feeding has dropped, if rolling has left them in a position that worries you, or if breathing is a concern. A baby who cannot be woken, or who cannot breathe comfortably, needs emergency care.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the 15 week wobble the 4-month regression?**
It can be an early version of the same change. The essay lives on [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months). From next week, use [4 month old sleep schedule](/4-month-old-sleep-schedule) for the month view.

**Can a 15 week old sleep through the night?**
Some have a long first stretch. The sourced 1–3 month meaning of the phrase is still often 5–6 hours (KidsHealth). There is no official 12-hour target this week.

**What happens after week 15?**
This is the last weekly snapshot in the series. For weeks 16–24, use the 4-, 5-, and 6-month schedules (and the 4- and 6-month regression pages where those names are used).
