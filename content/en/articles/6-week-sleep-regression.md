# 6 Week Sleep Regression: A Popular Name, Not a Documented Event

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Parents search **6 week sleep regression** — and **8 week sleep regression** — when a baby who had a slightly easier stretch suddenly wants to feed all evening, naps go to pieces, or nights get shorter again. This page is for that popular name. It is not [sleep regression](/sleep-regression) (the ages hub), not [3 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/3-months), and not a second [6 week old baby sleep](/6-week-old-baby-sleep) timetable.

## Short answer

There is **no official 6-week or 8-week sleep regression** in the clinical sources we use. Our regression hub already says sleep in the first 8 weeks is irregular from the start, not a "regression" from a stable baseline. Cleveland Clinic and Sleep Foundation describe regressions as popular names for rough patches, not clockwork events every baby must have.

What *is* often going on in this stretch, with sources:

- The body clock is only **starting** to develop around 4–6 weeks (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby). Days can feel more organized for a few days and then look messy again. That is not a skill being lost.
- Evening [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding) and [witching hour](/witching-hour) fuss are common. After the first week, cluster feeding should not be *all day, every day* without a feeding check (Cleveland Clinic, on the cluster page).
- The Period of PURPLE Crying, a well-described pattern of fussiness, often **peaks around 6–8 weeks** and eases by 3–4 months (National Center on Shaken Baby Syndrome and clinical explainers). That is about crying, not a sleep-hour quota.
- Parent and health-visitor language sometimes calls this a "growth spurt." There is no official AAP calendar of spurts we will treat as law.

So the honest label is: a hard 6–8 week patch with a popular name. Formal sleep training is still usually too early (Sleep Foundation: often 4–6 months).

## What actually helps this week

Keep the rules you already have: feed when the baby is asking (unless your clinician has given a different plan), put them down on the back in a clear, firm space, use morning light and a boring night, and accept that some naps will be in arms. A short, same-order evening cue is enough "routine."

Do not start melatonin, diphenhydramine, or a crib-training program because a forum thread used the word regression. If you want a later methods overview, use [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training) and wait.

If you feel yourself getting angry with a crying baby, put them in a safe crib and take a break. The PURPLE Crying materials exist in part for that moment.

## How long does it last?

There is **no official duration**. Clinical pages on PURPLE Crying describe a pattern that often eases by 3–4 months, with a peak around 6–8 weeks — that is the crying curve, not a promise that sleep will "return to normal" on day 10. Parent communities often talk about a stretch of days to a couple of weeks. We will not turn those anecdotes into a number.

If the rough patch came with a fever, poor feeding, or a baby who is hard to rouse, do not wait for a calendar.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call if feeding or wet diapers are off, if the baby is unusually hard to wake, if breathing or colour worries you, or if you are not coping. A noisy evening in an otherwise growing, dirty-diapered baby is a common story. A quiet, floppy, or painfully inconsolable baby is not "just week 6."

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the 6 week sleep regression real?**  
The *name* is popular. A documented, clockwork sleep-regression event at 6 weeks is not what the clinical sources describe.

**Is there an 8 week sleep regression?**
There is no official one. The popular "6 week" name, and the 6–8 week fuss peak described for Period of PURPLE Crying (NCSBS), are already discussed on this page. For the week-8 sleep snapshot — including short bassinet naps — use [8 week old baby sleep](/8-week-old-baby-sleep) and [8 week old short naps](/8-week-old-short-naps). 

**Should I sleep-train to get through it?**  
Usually not at this age. See the readiness page.
