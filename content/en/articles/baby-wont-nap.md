# Baby Won't Nap: What to Try When Naps Fail

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A baby who **will not start** a nap is a different problem from one who falls asleep and pops up 30 minutes later. That second pattern is [short naps](/short-naps) (sometimes called catnapping). A preschooler who is truly outgrowing daytime sleep belongs on [when toddlers stop napping](/when-do-toddlers-stop-napping). This page is for infants and toddlers who still need a nap and will not take it.

## Short answer

You cannot force a child to sleep. KidsHealth is blunt about that, and recommends a consistent nap routine — dim light, a quiet story, the same time each day — and **quiet time** in the room if they will not drop off. Put infants down **sleepy but not already asleep** when you can, so they practice falling asleep in the crib (KidsHealth; HealthyChildren.org often mentions "drowsy but awake" from around 4 months). Check whether the nap is too close to bedtime, whether they are [overtired or undertired](/overtired-baby), and whether the only way they nap is in a motion device that is not a safe sleep space — see [sleep associations](/sleep-associations) and [car seats and swings](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat). A skipped nap is usually a reason to **bring bedtime earlier**, not to keep them up to "make them tired."

## First, name the problem

- **Will not fall asleep at all** at nap time (this page).
- **Falls asleep and wakes after one cycle** — [short naps](/short-naps).
- **One nap is disappearing on purpose** at preschool age — [stop napping](/when-do-toddlers-stop-napping).
- **The third or morning nap is fading** as a scheduled drop — [3-to-2](/3-to-2-nap-transition) or [2-to-1](/2-to-1-nap-transition).

Getting the label wrong leads to the wrong fix: dropping a nap a 9-month-old still needs, or "sleep training" a 4-year-old who is done with daytime sleep.

## Why naps fail to start

Common, sourced or already-discussed on our pages, not a complete medical list:

- **Timing.** The gap since the last sleep is too short (not sleepy yet) or too long ([overtired](/overtired-baby) and wired). Use [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) as a starting range, then watch cues.
- **The nap is too close to bedtime.** KidsHealth specifically flags this for toddlers and preschoolers.
- **The routine is missing or changing every day.** KidsHealth: a consistent nap routine, set up early, is the main tool.
- **They only fall asleep in arms, a swing, or a moving car.** That is an association plus, often, an unsafe place if you leave them there. Move sleep to a crib when you can.
- **The room is busy, bright, or full of play invitations** at the moment you want sleep.
- **Discomfort:** a cold (see [sick-baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep)), hunger, or a wet diaper. Do not skip a needed feed to "protect" a nap in a young baby.

## What to try

1. **Keep a short, boring pre-nap routine** — the same few steps, in the same order, most days (KidsHealth).
2. **Offer the nap in a safe crib or play yard**, on the back, not in a seat after you have arrived home.
3. **If they will not sleep, switch to quiet time** rather than a 45-minute argument (KidsHealth). Stay consistent about the place and the length of the rest.
4. **Protect bedtime.** A lost nap is an earlier night, not a later one.
5. **Look at the whole day**, not one failed afternoon. Two hard days after vaccines or a bad night are not a new personality.

If you are mid [3-to-2](/3-to-2-nap-transition) or [2-to-1](/2-to-1-nap-transition), a refused nap may be the transition itself. Follow that plan instead of stacking extra "won't nap" tricks.

If you already have a night-time plan from [sleep training methods explained](/sleep-training-methods-explained) and want to know why the same plan is stalling at nap time, see **Why naps are often harder than nights** on that page. This page stays the place for a nap that will not start. Do not add a second, different method only for afternoons.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if a baby who used to nap reasonably has stopped for more than a short stretch and is also feeding poorly, very hard to wake, or breathing noisily, or if you are not sure they still need the nap at their age.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why won't my baby nap?**  
Timing, routine, associations, discomfort, or a nap that is already supposed to be disappearing. Start with timing and a consistent offer, not a new gadget.

**Should I cap night sleep to force a day nap?**  
We do not have an AAP rule that says to wake a well-rested baby at dawn to rescue a nap. For young babies, night calories and [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies) come first; see [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby).

**My toddler won't nap at home but naps at daycare.**  
Common. Keep the home rest period anyway, and keep bedtime steady.

**Is a stroller nap a failure?**  
An occasional on-the-go nap happens. It is not a replacement for a safe, flat sleep space at home, and a long seat nap is still a [car-seat safety](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat) issue once you have stopped traveling.
