# Developmental Leaps and Baby Sleep: There Is No Official Calendar

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Apps and books that promise a fussy “leap” on a dated week can make a rough patch feel predicted. This page is that claim. It is not a second [sleep regression](/sleep-regression) hub, and it does not add new official regression ages. Named stretches — [4 months](/sleep-regression/4-months), [separation anxiety](/separation-anxiety-and-sleep), new motor skills — already have their own homes.

## Short answer

**There is no official AAP, CDC, or NHS calendar of ten “developmental leaps” that will reliably disrupt sleep at set weeks.** The popular Wonder Weeks book and app popularise a timetable drawn from research on timed “regression” or “transition” periods in early infancy. In the AAP, CDC, and NHS milestone and crying pages opened for this article, we did **not** find that 10-leap timetable, and we did not find those organisations telling families to treat an app alert as a medical explanation for a bad night.

What we *did* find in the research trail:

- van de Rijt-Plooij and Plooij (1992), in the *Journal of Reproductive and Infant Psychology*, described infantile regressions and the onset of transition periods. A later paper that set out to test the idea summarises that 1992 work as **maternal reports of 10 strictly timed periods of emotional instability**.
- de Weerth and van Geert (1998), in the *British Journal of Developmental Psychology*, tried to validate that 10-period pattern with **ethological observations**. They followed **four** mother–infant pairs **weekly for 15 months**. The University of Groningen research-portal abstract of that study, which we opened, is plain: **“The results failed to support the 10-period pattern.”**

A study of four babies cannot “disprove” every later claim, and a study based on maternal reports from a small group cannot prove a universal week-by-week calendar either. The honest position is narrower: **the fixed 10-leap schedule has not been confirmed by the direct-observation study that set out to test it, and it is not an official pediatric protocol.** Fussy spells still happen. They just do not have to arrive on a shared due-date clock.

CDC’s “Learn the Signs. Act Early.” milestones, developed with an AAP-convened working group, are **skill checklists** (what at least about three-quarters of children can do by a given age) used for developmental *surveillance*. They are not a fussiness or sleep-regression timetable.

### What actually can make sleep wobble

These are ordinary, sourced reasons a baby’s nights can get bumpier — without a leap app:

- Sleep cycles maturing, often discussed around the [4-month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months).
- New motor skills (rolling, sitting, crawling, pulling to stand) and the urge to practice in the crib.
- [Separation anxiety](/separation-anxiety-and-sleep).
- Illness, a cold, or an earache — see [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep). Extra sleep after a rough spell is covered on [why is my baby suddenly sleeping more](/why-is-my-baby-suddenly-sleeping-more).
- [Sleep associations](/sleep-associations) that used to work and now need more help at each cycle.
- A change in [naps](/short-naps) or bedtime.

**Growth spurts** are often blamed on the same calendar. Official feeding pages talk more about extra appetite and [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding) than about a dated sleep regression. We will not invent an official “week 3, 6, 12” sleep-spurt chart.

NHS and AAP material on early crying still matters here: crying often rises in the first weeks and eases later in the first months (already the picture on our [colic](/colic) and [witching hour](/witching-hour) pages). That curve is not the same thing as ten labelled leaps.

## How to use a leap calendar, if you already have one

If a week label reduces your anxiety, it does not have to be thrown out — but treat it as a **story some families find comforting**, not as a diagnosis and not as a reason to ignore red flags. A baby who is hard to rouse, is feeding poorly, has fewer wet diapers, or is struggling to breathe is not “just in a leap.” Call.

Do not start a formal sleep-training method, drop needed feeds, or add unsafe sleep products because an app said a leap had begun. If the week is simply noisy and the baby is well, the usual supports are enough: a boring [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby), safe sleep, and patience.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Use well-child visits to ask about skills on the CDC or your clinic’s checklist if something is lagging, not to confirm a leap number. Get help sooner if sleep has changed *and* the baby seems unwell, is not gaining, or you cannot wake them for feeds. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are Wonder Weeks real?**  
The book describes a 10-period idea that began in small studies, including maternal reports. A 1998 observational study of four infants failed to support that 10-period pattern. Official AAP, CDC, and NHS pages we opened do not use the calendar. “Real” is the wrong test; “officially scheduled” is not supported.

**Is a leap the same as a sleep regression?**  
People use the words interchangeably online. Our [sleep regression](/sleep-regression) pages discuss named ages and what research actually shows. This page is the leap-calendar claim. Neither term is a medical diagnosis.

**Should I expect worse sleep every time the app pings?**  
No. Some weeks will be hard for other reasons, and some labelled “leap” weeks will be fine.

**Do growth spurts follow the same calendar?**  
Not as an official sleep timetable. See [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding) and [why is my baby suddenly sleeping more](/why-is-my-baby-suddenly-sleeping-more).
