# Why Is My Baby Suddenly Sleeping More? Catch-Up vs a Warning

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A baby who was fussy or frequent-waking can, a few days later, seem to **sleep much more**. Parents are often relieved and then uneasy. This page is that triage. It is not [can a newborn sleep too much](/can-a-newborn-sleep-too-much) (the baseline "is this much sleep safe?" question) and not [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression) (a popular name for a harder stretch). If you need the feeding-clock rules, use [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby).

## Short answer

A sudden increase in sleep is sometimes **ordinary catch-up**: after a growth-and-fuss spell, after immunizations, or after a few short-nap days, a baby may take longer stretches and look "so sleepy." Community threads describe this often around 6–8 weeks and after vaccine visits. Those stories are not evidence. The clinical test is the same one used on the too-much-sleep page.

**Usually reassuring** if the baby still wakes or can be woken to feed, has the wet diapers you were told to expect, has some alert, normal moments, and does not have a fever or other illness signs.

**Not reassuring** if they are **hard to rouse**, feeding has dropped, diapers have dropped, they are floppy or unusually limp, breathing is wrong, they look jaundiced or a colour you do not recognise, or a fever is present — especially under 3 months. Then you call, you do not wait to see if it is "just catch-up."

There is no official "sleeping more than usual" threshold in hours. The 0–3 month band is already wide (14–17 hours in the NSF / KidsHealth figure; other ranges wider still). A jump inside a wide band, with a well baby, is a weak signal on its own.

### Catch-up or a call?

| Clue | Leans ordinary | Leans "call now" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waking | Stirs for feeds, or wakes with a little help | You cannot wake them, or they fall straight back without feeding |
| Body | Normal colour, some alert eye contact or movement when awake | Floppy, weak cry, yellow / grey / blue, or a rash you were told to worry about |
| Intake | Feeds and wet diapers are still in range | Feeds refused; diapers suddenly fewer |
| Timing | After a known fussy spell or, sometimes, after vaccines (still watch them) | With fever, cough, vomiting, or a baby under 3 months who is just "not right" |

After vaccines, some sleepiness and fuss are expected enough that our sick-sleep page already says: dress for the room, do not add extra blankets, and call if you are worried. Extra sleep after a jab is not a free pass to ignore a baby you cannot rouse.

## What not to do

Do not skip feeds a clinician still wants, just because the baby is "finally sleeping." Do not wrap extra layers "to help them stay asleep." Do not start a supplement or a sleep medicine. If you are in the newborn weeks, follow the wake-to-feed advice you were already given.

If the extra sleep is only in the day and nights have become a party, that is day-night confusion or a timing issue, not a mystery illness — see [newborn day-night confusion](/newborn-day-night-confusion).

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call today if the "not reassuring" column fits. Call urgently or use emergency services if you cannot wake the baby or breathing is a struggle. If the baby is well, feeding, and just quieter after a hard week, you can mention it at the next visit, and still call sooner if your gut says something has changed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is sleeping more a sign of a growth spurt?**  
It can be part of a popular "growth spurt" story. There is no official AAP hour chart for that. Judge the baby, not the label.

**My 6 or 8 week old is suddenly sleeping all day.**  
Use this page's table, plus [6 week old baby sleep](/6-week-old-baby-sleep) or [8 week old baby sleep](/8-week-old-baby-sleep). If they are hard to wake, do not wait for the week to end.

**Can teething or a cold do this?**  
Illness can increase sleep *and* can be dangerous if the baby is not waking to feed. Teething is over-used as a catch-all; if you are unsure, call.

**Can vaccines affect my baby’s sleep?**
They can, for a short time. NHS inform (babies and children up to 3 years 4 months) lists being **sleepy**, irritable, upset, or less hungry than usual as common side effects after a vaccine; these usually pass within a couple of days. The Lullaby Trust: after vaccinations some babies may feel irritable and unwell, and may develop a fever; **dress them for the environment and do not wrap them in extra clothes and blankets**. The Lullaby Trust also states that vaccinations do **not** increase the risk of SIDS, and that babies who are up to date with vaccinations have a **lower** risk of SIDS.

Call your pediatrician or local urgent line if you cannot rouse them, if they seem significantly unwell, or if you are worried about a fever — especially under 3 months. Do not add loose blankets to the crib. See also [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep) and [baby sweating in sleep](/baby-sweating-in-sleep).
