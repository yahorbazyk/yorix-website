# Can a Baby Sleep With Hiccups? Usually Yes — You Don't Need a Cure

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A baby who hiccups on the way to sleep — or already looks asleep between hiccups — is one of the most common "is this OK?" searches. This page is that question. It is not a second [reflux and baby sleep](/reflux-and-baby-sleep) essay, and it is not [newborn grunting in sleep](/newborn-grunting-in-sleep).

## Short answer

HealthyChildren.org, in "Baby Burping, Hiccups & Spit-Up" (adapted from *Caring for Your Baby and Young Child*, updated 31 December 2024): **most babies hiccup from time to time, and this usually bothers parents more than the infant.** If hiccups start **during a feeding**, change position, try to burp them, or help them relax. **Wait until the hiccups are gone to resume feeding.** If they have not gone in **5 to 10 minutes**, try feeding again for a few minutes — that usually stops them. If hiccups happen often, try to feed when they are calm and before they are extremely hungry.

The same page says spit-up **almost never involves choking, coughing, discomfort, or danger, even if it occurs during sleep**, and that the best sleep position is still **on the back, mattress flat, not elevated** — even with frequent spit-ups.

If they are already asleep, look comfortable, and are breathing normally, the opened sources do **not** ask you to wake them in order to "cure" the hiccups. Do not prop the mattress, add a wedge, or turn them onto the side.

**Do not give water to a baby under 6 months** to stop hiccups. HealthyChildren.org's recommended-drinks advice: in the first 6 months, babies do not need water or other liquids besides breast milk or formula unless a pediatrician specifically says otherwise. A general symptom-checker note about "a few swallows of water" is not a reason to override that feeding rule. No adult folk remedies (startling them, holding their breath, paper bags).

NHS advice on reflux lists **hiccupping when feeding** among possible reflux *symptoms*, alongside bringing up milk and unsettled feeds. That does not mean every ordinary hiccup is reflux. Use the reflux page if there are feeding-pain signs, poor weight gain, or frequent vomiting — not this page as a diagnosis.

### A quick check, not a diagnosis

| What you notice | What the opened sources support | What not to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary hiccups, baby looks comfortable | Usually bother parents more than the baby (HealthyChildren.org) | Do not startle them or use adult "cures" |
| Hiccups during a feed | Pause, change position, burp, wait; if not gone in 5–10 minutes, try feeding again | Do not give water under 6 months |
| Already asleep and comfortable | Sources do not require you to wake them to stop hiccups | Do not prop the mattress or use a wedge |
| Spit-up during sleep as well | Almost never involves choking; still back, flat (HealthyChildren.org) | Do not place them on the side or tummy "so they won't choke" |
| Hiccups plus feeding distress, poor gain, or breathing trouble | Talk to the pediatrician; NHS lists hiccupping-when-feeding among reflux symptoms | Do not self-diagnose reflux from hiccups alone |

## When hiccups are not the first problem

Call if hiccups come with trouble feeding, forceful or green/yellow vomit, coughing or arching during feeds, fewer wet diapers than expected, unusual sleepiness, or breathing that looks hard. Those are clinical questions. A baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly needs urgent care — that is not a hiccup problem.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a baby sleep with hiccups?**  
If they look comfortable and are breathing normally, the opened sources do not ask you to wake them just to stop the spell. Keep them on their back, on a firm, flat, clear surface.

**Is it normal for a newborn to hiccup every night?**  
Hiccups are common. HealthyChildren.org does not give a "too many times a night" official number. If they seem distressed, or hiccups come with the feeding or breathing worries above, call.

**Should I give water, gripe water, or sugar water?**  
Not under 6 months, unless your pediatrician has specifically told you to offer water. Milk is the drink in that period. Do not start a product to "cure" hiccups without asking.

**Are hiccups a sign of reflux?**  
They can appear in a reflux picture (NHS lists hiccupping when feeding among symptoms). Ordinary hiccups in a comfortable, growing baby are also common. Do not change the sleep position to treat suspected reflux.

**Can I let them finish the feed with hiccups?**  
HealthyChildren.org says wait until they are gone, then resume; if they have not gone in 5 to 10 minutes, try feeding again for a few minutes, which usually stops them.
