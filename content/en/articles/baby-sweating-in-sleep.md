# Baby Sweating in Sleep: Check the Chest, Not Only the Hair

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A damp scalp at 2 a.m. is one of the most common "is this dangerous?" searches. This page is that question. It is not a second [how to dress baby for sleep](/how-to-dress-baby-for-sleep) (layers, TOG, and the room-temperature conflict live there). It is not [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep).

## Short answer

Sweating can mean the room or the outfit is too warm — and **overheating is a SIDS risk factor** the AAP asks you to avoid. HealthyChildren.org's swaddling and safe-sleep advice lists sweating, damp hair, flushed cheeks, heat rash, and rapid breathing as signs a baby may be too hot. KidsHealth: dress for the room, do not over-bundle, and do not cover the head in sleep; watch for sweating or skin that feels hot. The AAP's parent safe-sleep advice: in general, dress them in **no more than one extra layer** than you would wear; they may be too hot if they are sweating or the **chest feels hot**. Raising Children Network: feel the **tummy or back**, not the hands and feet (those can feel cool even when the body is warm), and keep the head uncovered indoors.

A slightly sweaty scalp in an otherwise comfortable baby, after you have checked the chest and peeled a layer if needed, is often just that. **Soaking** clothes or bedding in a cool room, sweating with fever or breathing trouble, or a baby you cannot rouse is a clinical question.

Room-temperature numbers disagree (NHS-style 16–20°C bands already reported on the dressing page vs AAP "comfortable for an adult"). We do not average them here.

### A quick check, not a diagnosis

| What you notice | What to do first | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Damp hair, warm but not hot chest, they otherwise look well | Remove a layer; uncover the head; use the dressing page | AAP / KidsHealth / Raising Children |
| Flushed, heat rash, rapid breathing, hot chest | Cool the room or outfit now; do not add hats or loose blankets | AAP / HealthyChildren.org |
| Soaking nights in an already-cool room, or sweat plus illness signs | Call the pediatrician | Clinical follow-up, not a forum label |

## What this page will not redo

The dressing page already explains why a single official room number does not exist and how wearable blankets fit. This page will not repeat that table. Safe sleep stays the same: back, firm, flat, clear, no loose cloths "to mop the sweat," no indoor hats, no weighted layers.

## What parents often worry about

Community threads often describe a very sweaty head and a chest that feels only warm, or a baby who sweats only at night.

- **"Only the head is wet."** Hair shows moisture easily. Check the chest or tummy, breathing, and whether they are hard to rouse. Do not treat the size of a damp patch as a thermometer.
- **"They sweat every night."** If the room and outfit already look right and sheets are soaked, or this is new and they seem unwell, call. Do not invent a "deep sleep sweat" as a medical fact — we do not have an official source that measures that.
- **Illness.** Fever, cough, or a baby under 3 months who is "not right" belongs on [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep) and a same-day call. Persistent snoring or laboured breathing belongs on [baby snoring and mouth breathing](/baby-snoring-and-mouth-breathing).

## When a sweaty night needs a clinician

Call if sweating comes with fever, poor feeding, fewer wet diapers than expected, laboured or noisy breathing, or a baby who is unusually hard to wake. Call if nights are soaking despite a cool room and a light outfit. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a sweaty head at night normal?**  
It can be, especially if the chest is not hot and they settle when you remove a layer. It can also be a warning of overheating. Use the check above, not a forum "all babies do this."

**Should I put a hat on so they stop losing heat?**  
No, not for indoor sleep. KidsHealth and Raising Children: do not cover the head in sleep. Hats can also slip over the face.

**Can I put a towel or muslin under them to catch the sweat?**  
No loose layers in an infant sleep space. Change a damp fitted sheet if you need to.

**Is this the same as a fever?**  
Sweat is not a thermometer. If you think they have a fever, especially under 3 months, use your clinician's fever plan — do not read it from damp hair.
