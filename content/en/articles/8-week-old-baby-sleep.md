# 8 Week Old Baby Sleep: When the Clock Stirs and Naps Stay Short

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is the **week 8 snapshot**. It is not [2 month old sleep schedule](/2-month-old-sleep-schedule) (the month view — week 8 is two months). If the only question is 30-minute crib naps, go to [8 week old short naps](/8-week-old-short-naps). If someone has called this a regression, use [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression).

## Short answer

The 24-hour band is still the 0–3 month one: **14–17 hours** (NSF via KidsHealth), with the same wider ranges already listed on the week-2 and week-6 pages. We will not invent an "8 week official total."

The landmark that *does* belong to this stretch is the body clock. Sleep Foundation: **melatonin and cortisol start following a daily rhythm at about 8–9 weeks**, which is when sleep may begin to look a little more predictable. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby: some babies begin sleeping for longer periods around **8 weeks**. Day-night confusion often eases here; it may not vanish on the 56th day.

Naps, meanwhile, are still allowed to be messy. A bassinet nap that ends at about half an hour, while a contact or carrier nap lasts longer, is the classic week-8 complaint. That is usually one short sleep cycle, not a failed 2-month "schedule." The lengthening plan on [short naps](/short-naps) is written for later months. Do not run it as a program this week.

## What is usually going on this week

Mornings may start to look more like mornings: a feed, a bit of light, a first nap that is the "best" one of the day. Evenings can still be the hard end of the PURPLE / witching stretch. In the U.S., **2-month immunizations** often fall around this week; in the UK, **8-week vaccinations** do. Extra sleepiness or fuss after vaccines is common enough that [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep) already has a short after-vaccine note: dress for the room, do not add extra wraps, and call if you are worried.

A social smile is expected **by 2 months** in usual milestone language. Celebrate it; do not use it as a sleep-training checkpoint.

## What parents often struggle with at 8 weeks

From recurring community threads, in our words:

- The bassinet nap ends at 29–30 minutes, sometimes with a cry, while the same baby will sleep longer on a chest or in a carrier.
- Nights are "actually all right," and days feel impossible — or the opposite, after vaccines.
- Someone has gone back to work or started daycare early, and crib naps there are even shorter than at home. That is an early two-environment problem ([daycare and baby sleep](/daycare-and-baby-sleep)), not proof that you missed a nap-training window.
- Eat–play–sleep graphics make the day feel like a failure because every nap still starts with a feed. At 8 weeks that loop is still allowed to be loose.

## A mixed-nap day (example only)

This is not the 7:00 / Nap 1 / Nap 2 table on the 2-month page.

Imagine a 7:30 a.m. waking and feed, a decent first nap in the bassinet that dies at 35 minutes, a late-morning contact nap that actually lasts, a short afternoon doze in the stroller, and a 4:30 p.m. "nap" that is really a fidget. Bedtime might still be after 8:30. We are not moving you to a 7 p.m. lights-out this week just because an app suggested it. If a late bedtime is wrecking *you*, a slightly earlier wind-down is a comfort experiment, not a rule.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Same backbone as the earlier week pages: poor feeding, too few wet diapers, a baby who is hard to rouse, breathing worries, or a reaction after vaccines that does not sit right with you. Ask before you drop a night feed just because one 6-hour stretch happened.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many hours should an 8 week old sleep?**  
The 0–3 month band, usually quoted as 14–17 hours in 24 hours, including naps. No official week-8 total.

**Is a 30-minute nap enough?**  
Often, at this age, that *is* a nap. Read [8 week old short naps](/8-week-old-short-naps).

**Should I start sleep training this week?**  
Usually no. See [when to start sleep training](/when-to-start-sleep-training).
