# Should You Wake a Baby From a Nap? Daytime Caps Are Not Night Feeds

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This page is about **ending a long daytime nap** — often to protect bedtime or the next feed. It is not [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby), which is the **night, wake-to-feed** question (KidsHealth often discusses about every 3–4 hours in the early weeks; HealthyChildren.org (AAP) formula Q&A discusses waking if more than 4–5 hours in the first month — we do not average those).

## Short answer

There is **no official AAP nap-cap clock** (no "never more than 2 hours") that this page will invent. Families sometimes wake a baby from a very long daytime nap when that nap is pushing bedtime late or crowding out a later nap that still matters at that age. That is a schedule experiment, not a medical protocol.

Do **not** treat a long daytime nap as automatically dangerous. Do **not** wake a newborn from a nap as a "bedtime protection" habit while they still need frequent feeds — the night-feed page and your pediatrician's weight-gain plan come first. If they are unwell, premature, or not gaining well, ask before you start waking them for the clock.

AASM 12–16 hours in 24 hours, including naps, applies from **4 months**. Earlier weeks use the conflicting 24-hour bands already on [how much sleep does a baby need](/how-much-sleep-does-a-baby-need). Neither set of figures is a nap-length cap.

## When families try a gentle wake

Typical reasons: the first nap of the day has run so long that the next one cannot fit, or a late-afternoon nap is heading past the usual bedtime. A dim room, a calm voice, and a few minutes of holding or a feed is how most people do it — this is not a cry-based protocol, and this page will not turn it into one.

If they are groggy and miserable, you may have cut an ordinary nap short. If evenings improve after a few days and they still look well, the experiment may be useful. If they are consistently hard to rouse, or a long nap is new and they seem unwell, stop experimenting and call — see [why is my baby suddenly sleeping more](/why-is-my-baby-suddenly-sleeping-more) and [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep).

## When you should not wake them for the clock

- A clinician has asked you to **let them sleep** or to wake only for feeds.
- They have a fever or are clearly ill.
- You would be waking them from a safe sleep space only to put them in an unsafe one (a sofa, a swing, a car seat used as a bed). Safe sleep stays the same.
- The "long nap" is actually the night, started early — that is a bedtime-clock question, [what time should baby go to bed](/what-time-should-baby-go-to-bed).

## When to get clinical advice

Call if you cannot rouse them for a feed they usually take, if long days of sleep arrived with illness signs, or if weight gain is a concern. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I wake a 6 month old from a 3-hour nap?**  
There is no official 3-hour rule. If it is wrecking bedtime, some families try a gentle wake. If they are well and nights are fine, a long nap can also just be a long nap.

**Is this the same as wake windows?**  
Wake windows are a popular planning tool and not a single official chart — see [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age). A nap cap is only one of the ways people use those ranges.

**Should I wake them from a contact nap?**  
Only if you need to, and only onto a safe surface. See [contact naps](/contact-naps).

**What if they will not go back down after I wake them?**  
Then the cap may have been too sharp. Offer a later short nap or an earlier bedtime rather than stacking experiments. See [overtired baby](/overtired-baby).
