# Twins and Sleep: Safety, Co-Bedding, and Routines

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Two babies does not mean two copies of a generic schedule. The sleep questions that are specific to twins are about **safety, surfaces, and logistics**: can they share a cot, do they stay in your room, and how do you survive a night when one is always awake? This is not a second [newborn](/newborn-sleep-schedule) or [3-month](/3-month-old-sleep-schedule) article with the word "twins" added.

## Short answer

On **safe sleep**, start from the same rules as for one baby — back, firm, flat, clear, in your room, not in your bed — on our [safe-sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies) hub, with extra caution because NHS notes that twins and premature or low-birth-weight babies have a **higher SIDS risk**. On **whether two babies can share one cot**, U.S. and UK advice genuinely disagree. The AAP cannot recommend bed-sharing under any circumstances, **including with twins**, meaning each baby should have their own approved sleep surface. NHS says co-bedding in a single cot is "perfectly safe" while they are small enough, if you follow safe-sleep advice: on their backs, heads toward one another with feet at opposite ends, or side by side with feet at the end of the cot, not close enough to touch, and not in a Moses basket. We will not average those into one Yorix rule. Talk to your pediatrician, and do not share an adult bed with one or both babies.

## Safety first: one surface or two?

**United States (AAP).** The policy is written for sudden unexpected infant death and suffocation risk. "Bed-sharing" here includes a baby sharing a surface with anyone, including a twin. The practical reading is: two approved cribs, bassinets, or play yards, even if they sit side by side in your room. That arrangement still counts as room-sharing.

**United Kingdom (NHS).** Co-bedding in one cot is described as safe while the babies are small, with the placement rules above, and NHS even notes it can help them regulate temperature and sleep cycles and can soothe them. A Moses basket is too small for two. For triplets, NHS says they can be placed next to each other in a cot while they still fit, on their backs, with their feet toward the long side of the cot. When they are older, NHS suggests separate cots close together if you have the room, so they can still comfort each other. Co-bedding, in the NHS telling, can also make it easier to keep both babies in your room for longer.

If you live in the U.S., or your pediatrician follows AAP guidance, plan for two surfaces from the start. If you are under NHS or Lullaby Trust care and considering co-bedding, read those pages in full, follow every placement rule, and do not put anything — towels, pillows, or cot dividers — between the babies. Lullaby Trust says that once any baby can roll and move about the cot, they need their own sleep space so they cannot roll into each other. NHS itself says that if one waking sets the other off, you can try separate cots side by side.

Neither body thinks an adult mattress is a solution. Lullaby Trust is explicit: do not bed-share with more than one baby at a time, and the safest place remains each baby's own space. Sofas are out of the question. See [bed-sharing](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping).

Prematurity is common with twins. Follow any hospital advice about monitors or feeding intervals, and use the baby's **adjusted age** when you compare them to a sleep schedule written for term babies.

## Room-sharing with two cribs

The 6-month room-share recommendation still applies. Two bassinets or a mini-crib plus a bassinet next to your bed is often more workable than trying to fit two full cribs. When they outgrow bassinets — [about 5 months or when they push up on hands and knees](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib), or sooner at the weight limit — you may need a larger room layout, not a premature move to a nursery. A shared nursery after 6 months is a family decision; see [when to move to their own room](/when-to-move-baby-to-own-room).

## Logistics: nights, feeds, and "do I pick them both up?"

NHS twins guidance is more useful here than any generic schedule:

- You can often **coordinate night feeds** so you are not starting from scratch an hour after you have finished.
- You do not always need to rush to cuddle the twin who is crying; sometimes they resettle, and automatically waking the other one makes the night longer for everyone.
- If one baby's waking routinely sets the other off, separate cots (side by side, still in your room) are the NHS next step.

A "twins schedule" is still built from the same age-based pieces as one baby — [wake windows](/wake-windows-by-age), total sleep in [how much sleep a baby needs](/how-much-sleep-does-a-baby-need), a shared [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) — but the routine may run in parallel or slightly staggered. It is fine if one twin is a better napper than the other. Forcing identical naps can be more stressful than useful.

Safe surfaces still apply when you are outnumbered and exhausted: no [car-seat or swing](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat) as a crib, no [stomach or side start](/baby-sleeping-on-stomach), no loose [blankets](/when-can-baby-sleep-with-a-blanket) in the first year.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask, early, whether they want each baby on a separate surface, how long to [wake for feeds](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby), and whether adjusted age should guide naps. Mention if one twin is gaining poorly, if snoring or breathing differs between them, or if you have been bed-sharing because two cribs felt impossible. You should not have to wait for a crisis to have that conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can twins sleep in the same crib?**  
AAP: no shared surface, including with a twin. NHS: yes, in one cot, while they are small, with specific placement and a clear, firm mattress — not a Moses basket. Follow the advice your clinician uses, and do not use an adult bed as the "same crib."

**Should twins follow the same sleep schedule?**  
Aim for a similar day, but expect differences. Coordinating feeds is more important than identical nap lengths.

**Do twins need two bedrooms?**  
No. They need safe surfaces. Those can be in your room, then later in one nursery or two, depending on space and age.

**Is it okay if one twin is a contact napper and the other is not?**  
Yes. Just stay awake for any [contact nap](/contact-naps), and do not put the second baby in an unsafe place while your hands are full.

**When should we move twins to their own room?**  
Not before 6 months, on AAP and NHS advice, unless your doctor has a specific plan. After that, see [when to move baby to their own room](/when-to-move-baby-to-own-room).
