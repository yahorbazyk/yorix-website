# 2 Week Old Baby Sleep: Pieces, Feeds, and 'Sleeping All Day'

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 2 snapshot**, not a timetable and not [newborn sleep schedule](/newborn-sleep-schedule) (the first-weeks overview). It is also not [1 month old sleep schedule](/1-month-old-sleep-schedule). If you are asking whether long sleep can be unsafe, use [can a newborn sleep too much](/can-a-newborn-sleep-too-much) as well.

## Short answer

There is still **no official clock schedule** at 14 days. Hunger, not daylight, mostly runs the day. For the 0–3 month band, the National Sleep Foundation figure quoted by KidsHealth is **14–17 hours in 24 hours**, in pieces. Other ranges are wider: Sleep Foundation and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby give **11–19 hours**; NHS pages describe anything from about **8 to 18 hours** as possible in the newborn months. HealthyChildren.org describes **about 16–17 hours**, often only **1–2 hours at a time**. We report those bands. We do not invent a special "week 2 total," and we do not average the ranges.

Feeds are still frequent. KidsHealth: in the first weeks, wake a newborn who is sleeping a long stretch **about every 3–4 hours to feed** until weight gain is going well — often through the first couple of weeks — then ask your pediatrician whether a longer night stretch is all right. HealthyChildren.org's formula-feeding Q&A: in the first month, wake a formula-fed baby who sleeps **more than 4–5 hours**. Those two clocks do not match perfectly. Your baby's doctor, not this page, sets the alarm.

Parents often describe week 2 as "they sleep all day" or "they suddenly did a 5-hour stretch — did I do something wrong?" Those are the worries of this week. They are not a "2 week sleep regression". A baby who is **hard to wake**, feeding poorly, or jaundiced needs a clinical call, not a wait-and-see schedule.

## What week 2 is usually about

The first nights are already behind you. Feeding still drives almost every waking. Breastfed babies often feed about every 2–3 hours; bottle-fed babies about every 3–4 hours (KidsHealth). A slightly longer stretch can appear and then disappear the next night. That is ordinary, not a new skill you must protect at all costs.

Day and night may still look backwards: long, sleepy stretches in the light, more stirring after dark. The body clock is not in charge yet. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby places the *start* of that clock later, around 4–6 weeks. Do not try to "fix" week 2 with a 7 p.m. bedtime.

Safe sleep does not change because they are 14 days old: **on the back**, firm and flat, clear space, in your room — see [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies). If you find them on their side, roll them onto the back again; that question lives on [baby sleeping on the stomach](/baby-sleeping-on-stomach).

## What parents often ask at 2 weeks

These situations come up again and again in parent communities. We describe them in our own words. They are not statistics, and they are not a diagnosis.

- **Sleeping "all day."** Newborns can look almost always asleep and still be in a normal range. The useful checks are on the too-much-sleep page: they wake to feed or can be woken, they have enough wet diapers, they have some alert moments, and they are not floppy or hard to rouse.
- **A first 4- or 5-hour stretch.** Ask whether your pediatrician still wants night feeds at set gaps. If yes, wake them. If weight gain is already good and you were told a longer stretch is all right, keep the room dark and do not start a noisy "morning."
- **Hard to wake for a feed.** Try unwrapping, a diaper change, skin-to-skin. If they will not rouse, feed poorly, or look yellow or unwell, that is a same-day clinical question, especially in the first two weeks.

## A stretch of an afternoon (example only)

We are not publishing a 7 a.m. timetable this week — that shape already lives on the month pages and would fake a routine you do not have yet. Think in **loops**.

One afternoon might go: a feed at about 2:00, a brief diaper and a look at your face, asleep in the bassinet by 2:25, a short waking around 3:10 that settles with a hold, a proper waking and feed at 4:00, then another messy 40-minute nap on your chest because the bassinet did not work. Tomorrow the same hours will not match. The newborn page already shows a morning loop; this is only to show how scrappy a week-2 afternoon can look and still be ordinary.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Call if you cannot wake them for feeds, if wet diapers are fewer than you were told to expect, if they seem jaundiced or floppy, if breathing worries you, or if you were given a jaundice or weight-gain plan and cannot stick to it. The fuller red-flag list is on the newborn page. In the U.S., 911 or emergency care for a baby who cannot be woken, is struggling to breathe, or is a colour you do not recognise.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a 2 week sleep regression?**  
No official one. Early-days cluster feeding and very broken nights are common. See [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding) and the "second night" note on the newborn page. Do not start a training plan.

**Can a 2 week old sleep too much?**  
Sometimes long sleep is normal, and sometimes it is a reason to wake them or to call. Use [can a newborn sleep too much](/can-a-newborn-sleep-too-much).

**Should I start a bedtime routine now?**  
A short, dim, boring wind-down is harmless. A clock bedtime that you defend is not a week-2 job.
