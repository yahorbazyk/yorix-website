# When to Move Your Baby From Bassinet to Crib

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A bassinet is a short-term, close-at-hand sleep space. It is not meant to last until your baby has outgrown being a baby. This page is about **limits and how to move to a crib**, not about the later problem of a toddler who "won't sleep in the crib." Keep the crib in your room until you are ready to discuss a [move to their own room](/when-to-move-baby-to-own-room); that is a separate decision.

## Short answer

Follow the **manufacturer's weight and length limits**, and move sooner if your baby reaches a developmental limit. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, in its federal bassinet and cradle standard, states that a bassinet or cradle is **not intended to be used beyond the age of about 5 months or when a child is able to push up on his hands and knees**. Many families also move when the baby is close to the top of the sides when lying down, or when they are clearly outgrowing the space, even before the printed weight number. The next sleep space should be a safety-standard crib, mini-crib, or play yard with a firm, flat, tight-fitting mattress and no loose bedding — the same [safe-sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies) rules, just a larger footprint.

### Key numbers at a glance

| Question | What the sources say | Source |

We are not using a universal "15 lb" cutoff. That figure appears on some parent sites and for some play-yard bassinet attachments, but it is not a CPSC rule for every bassinet. Your model's label wins.

## Why bassinets have a short lifespan

Bassinets are smaller, with lower sides than a crib. That is helpful in the first weeks, when you want the baby within arm's reach for night feeds. It becomes a problem once the baby can push up, roll with more force, or — later — sit. Falls, and faces pressed against a solid or padded side, are the incidents CPSC has been concerned with.

Pushing up on hands and knees is the developmental line CPSC names. Rolling on its own is already a reason to [stop swaddling](/how-to-swaddle-a-baby). If your baby is rolling but has not yet pushed up, still check the manual: some models tell you to stop at the first signs of rolling, and you should follow the stricter of the two.

If the bassinet rocks, swings, or has an incline, use it only in the manner the manual allows, and never as a workaround for a baby who only sleeps in motion. See [car seats, swings, and loungers](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat).

For how cribs, mini-cribs, bedside sleepers, and Moses baskets differ — including U.S. vs UK wording on baskets — see [cribs, bassinets, and bedside sleepers](/cribs-bassinets-and-bedside-sleepers). Play-yard types, the supplied-mattress rule, and the 35-inch / climb-out limit are on [pack-and-play sleep](/pack-and-play-sleep).

## How to make the switch

You do not need a long, formal program. A few practical steps, drawn from the same safe-sleep logic used on our other pages:

1. Set up the crib or play yard in **your room** if you are still in the first 6 months (and many families stay longer). The move is about the product, not about giving up room-sharing.
2. Keep the crib boring: firm mattress, fitted sheet, no [blankets, pillows, or stuffed toys](/when-can-baby-sleep-with-a-blanket) in the first year. Lower the mattress to the height the manual specifies for a mobile baby.
3. Start with **naps** in the crib for a few days if nights feel like a big leap, then move nights too. Do not run two conflicting sleep spaces for weeks unless you are mid-transition.
4. Keep the [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) the same. The change is the furniture, not the whole evening.
5. If you have been using a play-yard bassinet insert, remove it when you hit the insert's own, often lower, limit. The play yard underneath may still be usable.

Some babies sleep just as well the first night. Some startle because the space feels bigger. That is usually a [sleep association](/sleep-associations) and novelty issue, not a sign you should go back to an outgrown bassinet.

## What not to do

- Do not "get a few more weeks" by stuffing blankets around a baby who is tall for the bassinet, or by adding a second mattress.
- Do not move them into your bed as a temporary fix. See [bed-sharing](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping).
- Do not use a secondhand crib with missing hardware, drop sides, or a recalled design. Check CPSC recall lists.
- Do not delay the move because the crib is in another room and you are not ready for that separation. Move the crib next to your bed first.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Mention the move if your baby was preterm and is at a different "adjusted" stage than the calendar age of 5 months, or if you are unsure whether what you are seeing is pushing up versus ordinary wriggling. If the baby has already fallen from a bassinet, get them checked and retire the product.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I move my baby out of the bassinet?**  
At the manufacturer's weight or length limit, at about 5 months, or when they can push up on hands and knees — whichever comes first, unless your manual is stricter.

**Can they go back to the bassinet for naps?**  
Not once they have outgrown it. Limits apply to every sleep.

**Is a mini-crib or play yard okay instead of a full crib?**
Yes, if it meets current safety standards and has a firm, flat, tight mattress. The AAP lists these as acceptable sleep surfaces. For types and limits, see [cribs, bassinets, and bedside sleepers](/cribs-bassinets-and-bedside-sleepers). For play-yard rules, including using only the mattress that came with it, see [pack-and-play sleep](/pack-and-play-sleep).

**Do I have to move them to their own room at the same time?**  
No. Room-sharing can, and for the first 6 months should, continue. See [when to move baby to their own room](/when-to-move-baby-to-own-room).

**What if they sleep worse in the crib?**  
Keep the safe product, and work on the settling habit. See [sleep associations](/sleep-associations) rather than returning to an unsafe or outgrown bassinet.
