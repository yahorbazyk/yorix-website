# Siblings Sharing a Room: After the Twins and Own-Room Pages

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is the **logistics** page for two (or more) children who will share a bedroom: an infant and a toddler, two under two, or a baby moving in with an older sibling. It is not [twins and sleep](/twins-and-sleep), which is two infants and, in the first months, often two cribs in the *parents'* room. It is not [when to move baby to their own room](/when-to-move-baby-to-own-room), which is the AAP room-share clock with *you*. It is not [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed). Read those first if the question is still "is this infant ready to leave our room?"

## Short answer

The AAP recommends that infants sleep in the parents' room, on their **own** approved surface, for at least the first **6 months**, and that this room-sharing can continue for a year. A move into a sibling's room should wait until that infant clock, and your family's safety judgment, actually support it. Sharing a *room* is not sharing a *bed*. **Never put an infant in a sibling's bed**, and do not put two children on one adult mattress as a solution to space. Each child needs an approved surface: a crib, bassinet, play yard, or, for an older child, their own bed, with the infant's crib kept clear (CPSC: bare is best; AAP 2022).

Two under two is a special squeeze. If both are still in the age when room-sharing with a parent is preferred, the twins page's logic still helps: two separate approved cribs or play yards, not one shared nest, and the parents' room if you can make it fit. A toddler who can climb into the baby's crib is a reason to delay the share, or to use a crib the toddler cannot get into, until the younger child is past the highest-risk months.

Staggered bedtimes usually work better than a double lights-out. Put the infant down first, or the older child first if that child needs the room quiet, and keep each [routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) short enough that the second one still starts on time.

### Who sleeps where

| Arrangement | All right? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infant in parents' room, own crib; sibling in another room | Preferred in the first 6 months (AAP) | This is the [own-room](/when-to-move-baby-to-own-room) decision, not a sibling-share yet |
| Infant and toddler, two surfaces, after ~6 months | Often the plan | Bare crib for the baby; nothing that can be pulled in; toddler cannot climb into the crib |
| Two infants (twins or two under two) in one child room | Only after you have left the parents-room stage, and still **two** surfaces | See [twins and sleep](/twins-and-sleep) for the first months |
| Infant in the toddler's bed, or both on one mattress | No | Not an approved infant sleep space |
| Toddler visits the baby's crib at night | Unsafe | Delay the share, or use a crib the older child cannot enter |

## Making the share workable

- **Two surfaces, always.** The twins page already says this for two babies; it is the same rule when the ages are different. A sidecar or a floor mattress for the older child can help you fit the room, as long as the infant stays in an approved crib or play yard and the older child's bedding cannot drift into it.
- **Crib safety still belongs to the infant.** If you need standards and recall checks, those will live as a short addition on [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies), not as a second ABC list here.
- **White noise and a dim lamp** can mask a sibling's stirs. Use the existing [white noise](/white-noise-for-baby-sleep) and [night lights](/night-lights-and-baby-sleep) pages; do not turn this room into an all-night projector show.
- **If the older child starts a curtain-call** because the baby is now interesting, use [toddler won't stay in bed](/toddler-wont-stay-in-bed) and [bedtime battles](/toddler-bedtime-battles), not a new sibling-specific method.
- **Feeds at night:** keep them as quiet as you can so the sibling can stay asleep. If daytime intake is slipping because nights got heavier, see [reverse cycling](/reverse-cycling).

There is no official AAP week that says "siblings may share from month X." The infant room-share window is the constraint that *is* official. After that, the constraint is safety and whether either child can actually sleep.

If the share (or the protest) started because a new baby came home, see [sleep regression](/sleep-regression) for the older child’s wobble. This page stays about two children in one room.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if you are considering an early move out of your room because of space, if a toddler can reach the baby, or if one child's snoring or breathing worries you (see [snoring and mouth breathing](/baby-snoring-and-mouth-breathing)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When can a baby share with a sibling?**  
After you have met the AAP parents-room guidance (at least 6 months, and many families wait longer), and only with two approved surfaces. Not in the sibling's bed.

**What about two under two?**  
Two cribs or play yards. In the first months, aim for the parents' room if you can, as on the twins page.

**Should they go to bed at the same time?**  
Usually no. Stagger the routines so one child's settle is not the other's party.
