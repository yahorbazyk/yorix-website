# 1 Week Old Baby Sleep: The First Nights at Home

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This is a **week 1 snapshot** of the days after birth and the first nights at home. It is not the first-weeks overview on [newborn sleep schedule](/newborn-sleep-schedule), and it is not [2 week old baby sleep](/2-week-old-baby-sleep). There is no clock timetable to defend yet.

## Short answer

The first seven days are organised by hunger, recovery from birth, and whatever feeding or jaundice plan you were given — not by a clock. No guideline publishes a special official total for day 3 or day 7. KidsHealth cites the National Sleep Foundation 0–3 month range of **14–17 hours** over a full day, broken into pieces. HealthyChildren.org talks about **roughly 16–17 hours**, frequently only an hour or two at a time. Other published bands for the newborn months are wider: Sleep Foundation and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby at **11–19**, some NHS pages as broad as about **8–18**. This snapshot lists the disagreement. It does not blend the figures into a day-7 target.

You will often be asked to **wake a sleepy newborn to feed**. KidsHealth's early-weeks rule is roughly **every 3–4 hours** until weight gain is going well. A HealthyChildren.org formula-feeding Q&A says, in the first month, to wake a formula-fed baby who has been down **over 4–5 hours**. Those two instructions are not identical. Follow the plan from your midwife or pediatrician this week, especially if jaundice or a weight check is being watched.

The situations that belong to this week are the first night at home, a noisier "second night," and the fear that a 4-hour stretch was too long. None of that is a training problem, and none of it is an official 1-week regression.

### Figures that apply in the first days

- **No week-1 official total.** The 14–17 hour NSF range (via KidsHealth) and the other newborn-month bands above describe the early months, not a special number for day 7.
- **Pieces, not long blocks.** HealthyChildren.org: often only an hour or two at a time in the early days.
- **Waking to feed.** KidsHealth's 3–4 hour early-weeks clock, or the 4–5 hour first-month formula Q&A on HealthyChildren.org, until your clinician says otherwise.
- **Wet diapers.** HealthyChildren.org: at least 5–6 a day after days 4–5 — a feeding check, not a sleep score.

## Coming home, not starting a routine

The hospital may have looked calmer than the first night in your own room. Lights, noise, visitors, and your own exhaustion all change the picture. Milk still comes around often: KidsHealth describes breastfeeds commonly every 2–3 hours and bottles commonly every 3–4. If feeds bunch together, that is [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding), not a separate week-1 method.

There is **no day–night rhythm** to reset yet. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby places the *start* of a body clock around 4–6 weeks. Dim, boring nights and brighter days are still useful — see [newborn day-night confusion](/newborn-day-night-confusion) — but a 7 p.m. bedtime is not a week-1 job.

Safe sleep is the same on day 2 as on day 20: **on the back**, firm and flat, clear space, in your room. Details are on [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies). A chest or sofa nap only while you are awake and watching is comfort, not a sleep space; if you need to sleep, move them.

## What parents often meet in the first week

These situations show up repeatedly on parent forums. We retell them in our words. They are not rates, and they are not clinical evidence.

- **The first night at home feels harder than the hospital.** That contrast is common. It does not mean you are already "behind" on a schedule.
- **A longer stretch appears, then you panic about whether to wake them.** In week 1 the default is usually yes, unless your clinician has already said a longer stretch is all right. If you were given a jaundice or weight-gain plan, follow that plan.
- **They look very sleepy, or slightly yellow.** Sleepiness after birth can be ordinary. Sleepiness plus poor feeding, fewer wet diapers than you were told to expect, or jaundice is a same-day clinical question, not a "let them catch up" experiment. If you cannot wake them, that is urgent.
- **Feeds feel almost constant, especially overnight.** That can be cluster feeding or ordinary first-week hunger. Count wet diapers and keep the weight-check appointment. Do not stretch gaps to "teach" a longer night.

## The first night home (example only)

We are not publishing a 7 a.m. table. One night at home might look like this: a feed at about 10:15 p.m., a brief settle in the bassinet, a stirring at 11:05 that needs a hold, another feed around 12:40 a.m., a short stretch until about 2:20, a messy cluster of feeds and burps until 4:00, then a slightly longer doze until a 6:00 a.m. waking. Tomorrow's hours will not match. The point is only that a broken first night can still be ordinary when they are waking to feed and wetting diapers as you were told to expect.

## When to call in the first week

Get clinical advice the same day if you cannot rouse them for a feed, if diaper counts are below the plan you were given, if their skin looks yellow, if they seem floppy or unwell, or if you cannot carry out a jaundice or weight plan. The longer red-flag list sits on the newborn page. If they will not wake, if breathing looks wrong, or if their colour suddenly changes, that is emergency care (in the U.S., 911).

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I wake a 1 week old to feed at night?**
Usually yes, until weight gain is going well or your clinician has said a longer stretch is all right. Use [should you wake a sleeping baby](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby).

**Can a 1 week old sleep too much?**
Long sleep can be normal, and it can also be a reason to wake them or to call. Use [can a newborn sleep too much](/can-a-newborn-sleep-too-much).

**Is the "second night" a sleep regression?**
No official one. The second night can be noisier and more hungry than the first. See the newborn page and [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding). Do not start a training plan.
