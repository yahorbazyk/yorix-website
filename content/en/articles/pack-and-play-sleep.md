# Pack-and-Play and Play Yard Sleep: Types, Rules, and When to Stop

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A "pack-and-play" is the name many U.S. families use for a **play yard**: a framed enclosure with a floor and mesh or fabric sides. This page is that product — types, overnight use, and the mistakes that create gaps. It is not [when to move baby to crib](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib) (the *timing* of leaving a bassinet). The ABC sleep rules stay on [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies). We do not rank brands.

## Short answer

CPSC treats a play yard as both a play space and a sleep space for a child who **cannot climb out and is less than 35 inches (890 mm) tall**. Inflatable play yards are **not** covered by the federal play-yard rule (16 CFR part 1221, which incorporates ASTM F406). A play yard is not the same as a rigid-sided non-full-size crib.

AAP and CPSC include a **standard-meeting play yard** among intended infant sleep surfaces, with the baby on the back and only a tight fitted sheet. **Use only the mattress that came with that play yard** (or a replacement obtained for that model). CPSC staff warn that a **supplemental / aftermarket thicker mattress** can create a gap and an entrapment hazard. HealthyChildren.org (29 May 2026) also flags extra-plush aftermarket mattresses as a trap-and-suffocation risk.

If the model has a **bassinet insert**, that accessory must meet **both** play-yard and bassinet rules. Remove the insert when you hit *its* limit — often earlier than the play yard underneath — or when the baby can push up on hands and knees (the usual bassinet line; follow the stricter of the manual or that developmental limit).

### Types, in plain language

| Type | What it is | Sleep rule of thumb |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mesh-sided travel play yard | The usual foldable rectangle | Fine for overnight sleep if it meets current standards, is set up correctly, and uses only its own mattress |
| Play yard with a bassinet insert | A raised, smaller bed that sits in the top | The insert is a bassinet, not "bonus padding." Take it out at the insert's own limit |
| Play yard with a changer or organizer | Side accessories | Follow the manual. Accessories have their own entrapment and fall risks; they are not a reason to leave a baby unattended |
| Mini or compact play yard | Smaller footprint | Same mattress and height/climb-out limits; check the printed numbers |
| Inflatable enclosure | Not in 16 CFR 1221 | Do not treat it as an approved infant sleep space |

## How to use one overnight

Set it up fully, on the floor, with every latch engaged the way the manual shows. A half-folded top rail has been a historic play-yard hazard — the current standard addresses rail configuration, and you should still never leave a unit that does not lock. Keep it clear: no bumpers, pillows, loose blankets, or stuffed toys. Do not add a second mattress, a foam topper, or "just a quilt underneath for comfort."

At home or in a hotel, the same rules apply. For travel logistics — inspecting a hotel unit, bringing your own — see [baby sleep and travel](/baby-sleep-and-travel), which now points here for the product itself.

## When to stop

Stop when the child reaches the manufacturer's weight or **35-inch** height line, or can **climb out**, whichever comes first. Stop the *bassinet insert* much earlier, as above. A toddler who is standing and rattling the top rail is telling you the limit is near, even if they are still under 35 inches.

## Common mistakes

- Buying a thicker "comfort" mattress because the original looks thin. The thin, tight pad is the point.
- Leaving the bassinet insert in after the baby can push up.
- Using a broken, recalled, or secondhand unit without checking [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov) / SaferProducts.gov.
- Putting the play yard on a bed, sofa, or table.

## When to get clinical or practical help

If the only sleep they will take is in a sitting or inclined product, that is [baby swings and bouncers](/baby-swings-and-bouncers) and [baby sleeping in car seat](/baby-sleeping-in-car-seat) — move them to this flat space when you can. Urgent care is for a baby who cannot be woken, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a newborn sleep in a pack-and-play?**  
Yes, if the unit meets current standards and you use it as designed — often in the bassinet insert, if it has one, until that insert's limit.

**Is a play yard as safe as a crib?**  
Both can be intended sleep surfaces when they meet their federal standards. They are built differently (mesh sides vs rigid slats). Choose one that is intact and used by the book, not a "softer" hybrid.

**Can I use a regular crib sheet?**  
Use a sheet that fits *this* mattress tightly. A loose sheet is a hazard.

**When do I move from the insert to the floor of the play yard?**  
When the insert's weight, length, or developmental limit arrives — often about the same time you would leave a standalone bassinet. The play yard below may still be usable.
