# When to Move Your Baby to Their Own Room

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Room-sharing — baby in your room, in their own crib or bassinet — is one of the clearer safe-sleep recommendations. Moving them to a nursery is more of a judgment call after that. This page is about **when to make that move and how to do it**. It is not a restatement of the 6-month rule alone, and it is not the same question as [moving from a bassinet to a crib](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib), which can happen in your room.

## Short answer

The AAP, NHS, and Lullaby Trust recommend that babies sleep in the **same room as you, not the same bed, for at least the first 6 months**. Older AAP material often said "ideally the first year"; the 2022 policy uses the six-month minimum. After 6 months, there is no single official week when you "must" move. Sleep Foundation notes there is no consensus on when to stop room-sharing beyond that AAP floor. Many families move sometime between 6 and 12 months, some later. Whenever you go, keep [safe-sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies) rules in the new room, and do not treat the move as a reason to start [bed-sharing](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping) on the nights it feels harder.

## Why the first 6 months are different

Sharing a room makes night feeds and checks easier, and it is associated with a lower SIDS risk — HealthyChildren.org, summarizing the AAP, says room-sharing can decrease that risk by as much as 50%. The baby still needs their **own** firm, flat, clear surface. Room-sharing is not bed-sharing.

The 2022 wording change matters because you will still find "first year" on older pages and on some consumer sites. We are following the current policy: at least 6 months, with room-sharing still a reasonable choice after that if it is working.

## Reasons families wait, and reasons they move

Reasons to stay in your room after 6 months:

- Night feeds are still frequent (common for breastfed babies; see [night weaning](/night-weaning) before you assume they are only a habit).
- You want the safest-known arrangement for a little longer.
- The "nursery" is not actually ready as a safe sleep space (cluttered crib, a heater too close, no working monitor if you plan to use one).

Reasons families move after 6 months:

- Everyone's sleep is worse because of mutual disturbance — a real issue, and one reason the AAP did not require a full year.
- A [bassinet](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib) has been outgrown and a crib will not fit comfortably next to the adult bed.
- Another baby is on the way, or a parent's shift work makes the room unworkable.

Sleep Foundation is honest that experts do not all name the same end date. That uncertainty is not a license to move a 3-month-old to another floor of the house.

## A practical move plan

1. **Do the furniture first, in your room if needed.** If the baby is still in a bassinet, move them to a crib *in your room*, then move the crib later. Two changes at once are harder to read.
2. **Make the nursery boring and safe.** Firm mattress, fitted sheet, no loose [blankets or loveys](/when-can-baby-sleep-with-a-blanket) until after 12 months, out of reach of cords and heaters. The same rules as before, just in a different doorway.
3. **Start with naps.** A few daytime sleeps in the new room let you see how they settle without betting a whole night on it.
4. **Keep the routine identical.** Same [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby), same order, same [sleep associations](/sleep-associations) you are already comfortable repeating.
5. **Decide on checks in advance.** If you are going to look in, plan how you will do it so you are not inventing a new pattern at 2 a.m. A monitor can help you wait before walking in; it is not a substitute for a safe crib.
6. **Give it more than one night.** A worse first night is common. Going back and forth every other day usually confuses the experiment.

If the move collides with an [illness](/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby), a [vacation](/daylight-saving-baby-sleep), or a known [regression window](/sleep-regression/6-months), waiting a week or two is reasonable.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask at the 6-month visit what they recommend for *your* baby, especially if the baby was premature, has a breathing issue, or is still feeding very frequently at night. If you are moving earlier than 6 months because of housing or a medical need, tell them so they can help you reduce risk rather than just saying no.

## Frequently asked questions

**When can a baby sleep in their own room?**  
The official minimum in AAP, NHS, and Lullaby Trust advice is 6 months. After that, it is a family decision.

**Is 6 months too early?**  
It matches the current AAP floor. Staying longer is also consistent with the policy. Neither is "wrong" if the crib itself is safe.

**Should twins share a nursery?**  
Each baby still needs their own safe surface. Whether those cribs are in your room or in a shared nursery is discussed on [twins and sleep](/twins-and-sleep).

**What if they sleep worse after the move?**  
Keep the safe room, hold the routine, and give it several nights. If sleep falls apart for weeks, look at [associations](/sleep-associations) and [night wakings](/survive-night-wakings-new-parents) rather than pulling them into your bed.

**Can they nap in our room and sleep at night in theirs?**  
Yes, as a short transition. Try not to make it a permanent split that you then have to unwind.
