# 2 Month Old Sleep Schedule: What an 8- to 11-Week Day Can Look Like

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Something often shifts around eight weeks. Your baby is smiling back at you, stays alert a little longer, and you may notice the first hints of a pattern: a nap that keeps landing at about the same time, or a night stretch that's suddenly longer. This guide is for babies from about **8 to 11 weeks**. It covers sleep totals, nap count, wake windows, a sample day, and a first real bedtime routine. Younger baby? See the [1 month old sleep schedule](/1-month-old-sleep-schedule). Nearer 12 weeks? The [3 month old sleep schedule](/3-month-old-sleep-schedule) picks up from here.

## Short answer

A 2-month-old typically sleeps **14–17 hours in 24 hours** (National Sleep Foundation panel, quoted by KidsHealth) in **several naps plus a longer night stretch**. Sleep Foundation says the sleep hormones melatonin and cortisol start following a daily rhythm at about **8–9 weeks**, which is when sleep may start becoming more predictable. Wake windows are usually about **1 to 1.5 hours** (Cleveland Clinic gives 1–2 hours for 1–3 months). Bedtime is often still late. A strict clock schedule isn't needed yet; the CDC's advice for this age is simply to **have routines for sleeping and feeding**.

## How much sleep at 2 months?

KidsHealth, quoting the National Sleep Foundation, recommends 14–17 hours over 24 hours for babies up to 3 months, and says the range of normal is still very wide. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby notes that from about 8 weeks babies might start sleeping for longer periods.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Napper describes 14–17 hours at 2 months, with about 8–9 hours at night and 6–8 hours across several short naps. Huckleberry suggests aiming for 5–6 hours of daytime sleep.

## How many naps does a 2 month old take?

Counts vary because naps are still short and uneven:

- **Nationwide Children's Hospital:** 2–4 naps a day at 2 months.
- **KidsHealth:** many 1- to 3-month-olds settle into 2–3 naps followed by a longer night stretch after a late-night feed.
- **App examples:** Huckleberry and Napper both describe 4–5 naps; Napper says each can last from 30 minutes to 2 hours.

The number matters less than the total and how your baby seems. A day of five 40-minute naps and a day of three long ones can both be fine. Short naps at this age are common and usually nothing to fix. If you like, our [contact naps guide](/contact-naps) covers what to do when the only long nap is on you.

## 2 month wake windows

| Source | Age band | Wake window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleveland Clinic | 1–3 months | 1–2 hours |
| Sleep Foundation | 1–4 months | 1–3 hours |
| Huckleberry (app example) | 2 months | 45 minutes–1.75 hours |
| Napper (app example) | 4–12 weeks | 60–90 minutes |

Most 8- to 11-week-olds sit around **60–90 minutes**, feed and diaper change included *(our reading of where the sources overlap)*. The first window after the morning wake-up is often the shortest *(practical suggestion)*. Look for yawning, eye rubbing, faster blinking, or staring to the side (Sleep Foundation) and start the next sleep then, rather than holding out for a number.

## A sample 2 month old schedule (example only)

**This is an example only.** Feeds come on demand, and the times will drift from day to day.

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 7:00 a.m. | Wake, feed, daylight |
| 8:15–9:15 a.m. | Nap 1 |
| 9:15 a.m. | Feed, tummy time on your chest or the floor |
| 10:30–11:45 a.m. | Nap 2 |
| 11:45 a.m. | Feed, outing or play |
| 1:15–2:30 p.m. | Nap 3 |
| 2:30 p.m. | Feed |
| 4:00–4:45 p.m. | Nap 4 |
| 6:00–6:30 p.m. | Short nap 5 if needed; feed |
| About 8:30–9:30 p.m. | Bedtime routine, feed, down for the night |
| Night | Feeds as needed, lights low, little talk |

**Why might bedtime be late?** Huckleberry (app example) says bedtimes after 9 p.m. are common at 2 months and tend to move earlier between 3 and 4 months, as the body clock matures. Napper (app example) says many parents put a 2-month-old down between 7 and 9 p.m. Both can work.

## Bedtime routine for a 2 month old

KidsHealth says this is a good time to start a familiar, relaxing bedtime routine: bathing, reading, and singing can signal the end of the day, and "be consistent" is the key. The NHS lists a bath, night clothes and a fresh diaper, a story, dim lights, a lullaby, and a cuddle as ideas. Cleveland Clinic suggests a routine of about 20–30 minutes, with the feed near the start.

A 2-month-old version takes about 20 minutes:

1. **Dim the room** and close the curtains.
2. **Quick wash or bath** (a bath doesn't have to be nightly).
3. **Fresh diaper, sleepsuit or sleep sack.**
4. **Feed** in low light.
5. **One short book or song,** the same each night.
6. **Into the crib or bassinet on the back,** ideally a little drowsy but still awake.

KidsHealth explains why the last step helps: if you rock your baby fully to sleep at bedtime, they may expect rocking after every night waking. It also suggests that if your baby squirms or fusses a little, you can wait a few minutes to see whether they settle, unless you think they're hungry or unwell. Don't worry if this doesn't work yet. Cleveland Clinic says babies under 3 months still rely on you to help them back to sleep.

## Night sleep at 2 months

- **Longer stretches start for some.** KidsHealth says some babies sleep 5–6 hours at a time by 2–3 months, but some don't.
- **Moving a feed.** KidsHealth suggests that if your baby eats at 7 p.m. and wakes at 2 a.m., you could try waking them for a feed at about 11 p.m., to line up their long stretch with yours. That's a version of a [dream feed](/dream-feed).
- **Formula-fed babies.** HealthyChildren.org says most formula-fed babies no longer need a middle-of-the-night feed between 2 and 4 months or once they weigh over 12 pounds. Breastfed babies may feed more often; check with your pediatrician before cutting feeds.
- **Night vs day.** Keep night wakings dark and dull: change or feed, then straight back down (KidsHealth).

## Comfort, crying, and pacifiers

The CDC's 2-month tips say you can't spoil a baby by holding or responding to them, that infant crying is often worse in the first few months but gets better, and that sucking on fingers or a pacifier can help a baby learn to self-soothe. KidsHealth says you can offer a pacifier at sleep time without forcing it, and that if you're breastfeeding you should wait until feeding is well established.

## Safe sleep at this age

KidsHealth notes that swaddling is OK until a baby starts trying to roll, typically at 3–4 months, and warns against weighted blankets, sleepers, or swaddles. It also says not to let a baby sleep in a car seat, on a feeding pillow, or in an infant lounger. Nationwide Children's suggests deciding where your baby will sleep long term by about 3 months. The AAP recommends room-sharing without bed-sharing for at least 6 months.

## What can make it harder

- **Long stretches awake** to tire your baby out. KidsHealth's newborn guide says overly tired infants often have more trouble sleeping at night.
- **A bright, social night.**
- **Expecting the same schedule every day.** Huckleberry (app example) also advises against a fixed clock schedule at 2 months because nap lengths are still unpredictable.
- **Product shortcuts** like inclined sleepers or loungers (KidsHealth).

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Speak to your doctor if your baby is feeding poorly or has fewer wet diapers, has a fever (any fever under 12 weeks needs a call right away), has noisy or labored breathing in sleep, is unusually hard to wake, or doesn't smile at people or hold up their head on their tummy by around 2 months (CDC milestones).

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a 2 month old sleep schedule look like?**  
Feeds, short wake windows of about 60–90 minutes, and several naps across the day, then a longer night stretch. Times will vary day to day.

**How many naps should a 2 month old take?**  
Anywhere from 2 to 5. Nationwide Children's says 2–4; apps often describe 4–5.

**What time should a 2 month old go to bed?**  
Between about 7 and 10 p.m. is common. Many babies move to an earlier bedtime between 3 and 4 months.

**When does a 2 month old sleep longer at night?**  
Some sleep 5–6 hours at a time by 2–3 months (KidsHealth), many not until later.

**Is it too early for a bedtime routine at 2 months?**  
No. KidsHealth recommends starting one now if you haven't already.
