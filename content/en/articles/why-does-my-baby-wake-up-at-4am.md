# Why Does My Baby Wake Up at 4am? Still Night, Not Morning

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

**4 a.m.** is an awkward clock time. It can feel like the night has failed, and it can also *become* the start of the day if the lights go on and a big feed plus play begin. This page is for that exact hour. It is not [early morning waking](/early-morning-waking) (built around **5 a.m.** and waking **before about 6 a.m.** as a too-early start to the day). It is not [split nights](/split-nights) (a long, wide-awake stretch in the middle of the night). You may need those pages after you decide which pattern you actually have.

## Short answer

A 4 a.m. waking is often **still night**. Sleep pressure is low by the small hours, and sleep is lighter (the same physiology the early-waking page already explains for pre-dawn hours). What you do in the next 15 minutes matters. A brief, boring feed or a diaper in the dark may let the baby go back to sleep until a more reasonable morning. A bright room, a chat, and a "breakfast" can teach 4 a.m. as wake time.

That is why this is not a second 5 a.m. article. At 5 or 5:30, many families are negotiating whether the day has begun. At 4:00, the goal is usually **to keep it night**.

Common reasons, none of them official diagnoses:

- **A feed is still needed** — especially in younger babies, or after a long first stretch. KidsHealth's "through the night" at 1–3 months is often only 5–6 hours; a 10 p.m. start can land a waking near 4.
- **A night feed was just dropped**, and 4 a.m. is now a hungry habit or a genuine gap. Do not drop feeds against medical advice.
- **The start of a split night** — if they then stay up for an hour or more, switch to [split nights](/split-nights).
- **Early rising in disguise** — if they will not go back, and 4 a.m. becomes their regular get-up, use [early morning waking](/early-morning-waking), but still try to keep 4 a.m. dark for a week before you surrender the morning.
- Light, noise, a wet diaper, or a room that is too warm or too cold — the usual environment checks.

## What parents often describe

Recurring community situations, in our words: a 4 a.m. "party" that started after someone turned on a lamp and offered a large feed; a waking that appeared when a 3 a.m. feed was removed; an older baby who is in a split night, not an early morning; a hope that a later bedtime will fix 4 a.m., which sometimes helps and sometimes makes them more overtired. Those are patterns to test, not rules.

## What to try (keep it boring)

- Treat 4 a.m. like a night waking: lowest light, little talk, no play.
- If they are hungry, feed, then put them back on the back in their own firm, flat space.
- Hold the "day" until your target morning (often around 6 a.m. — the early-waking page's usual threshold). A 4 a.m. diaper and a 4:10 a.m. breakfast out of the room is how the hour becomes a habit.
- Check the room: dark enough, comfortable on the chest, no exciting noise. A fan is not a SIDS device; cords stay out of reach.
- Give a change several nights. One lucky morning is not a new schedule.

Do not start melatonin for a 4 a.m. waking. See [melatonin and children's sleep](/melatonin-and-childrens-sleep) if that question has come up for an older child, and talk to the pediatrician first.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if the baby is distressed every night at the same hour, if you are unsure whether a night feed is still needed, if snoring or pauses come with the waking, or if you are exhausted to the point of not coping. A regular, calm 4 a.m. feed in a growing infant is often a timing problem, not an emergency.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 4am the same as early morning waking?**  
It can turn into that. At the moment it happens, try to keep it as night. If the day has truly started, use the 5 a.m. page.

**Will a later bedtime fix it?**  
Sometimes a slightly later bedtime helps; sometimes it produces a more overtired 4 a.m. Test one small change at a time.

**Should I just start the day at 4?**  
You can, but you will usually regret it by afternoon. Protect a realistic morning if you can.
