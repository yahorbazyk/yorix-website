# Baby Monitors: Types, Convenience, and What They Cannot Do

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

"Do I need a baby monitor?" is a gear question, not a safe-sleep requirement. This page is the types and the limits. The two-line FAQ that used to live only on [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies) now points here. We do not review brands, socks, or apps, and we do not sell anything.

## Short answer

**No, you do not need a monitor to follow safe sleep.** AAP 2022: **do not use home cardiorespiratory monitors as a strategy to reduce the risk of SIDS.** There is no evidence that those devices reduce these deaths, and products that claim to increase sleep safety can give a **false sense of security**. KidsHealth and HealthyChildren.org give the same warning about gadgets that claim to prevent SIDS.

A simple audio or video monitor can still be a convenience — so you can hear a cry from another room, or glance at a toddler after you have moved them out of your room. That is not the same as a medical monitor, and it does not replace a firm, flat, clear sleep space in your room for at least the first 6 months (AAP room-sharing). A glowing screen in the crib is still a device in the sleep space; see [screens and toddler sleep](/screens-and-toddler-sleep) if the "monitor" is a phone parked on the mattress.

### Types, generically

| Type | What it usually does | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audio-only | Picks up sound from the sleep room | Does not prevent SIDS or replace room-sharing in the first months |
| Video (with or without audio) | Lets you see movement or a fuss | Same limits; keep cords out of reach and the camera off the crib rail if it can fall or dangle |
| Wearable sock, belt, or sensor mat that tracks heart rate or oxygen | Consumer "reassurance" data | Not a recommended SIDS-prevention strategy per AAP; not a substitute for medical monitoring a clinician has prescribed |
| A phone or tablet used as a camera | A DIY video feed | Same limits, plus screen-light and cord issues; not a medical device |

If a clinician has prescribed a monitor for a specific medical reason (for example after certain hospital stays), that is a *medical* plan, not this consumer category. Follow that team, and still keep the sleep space safe.

## How to use one without undermining safety

Keep cords, straps, and chargers **out of the crib and out of reach**. Do not add a thick sensor mat that changes the firmness or fit of the mattress — [crib mattress safety](/crib-mattress-safety). Do not leave a baby alone in a hotel room "because the monitor is on" — already a travel-page warning. Room-sharing in the first months means you may not need a monitor at all at night.

A parent who is less likely to go in at every rustle can still use an audio unit as a practical tool. A parent who stares at oxygen numbers all night may be getting the false reassurance the AAP is worried about — or a new kind of anxiety. Neither is a reason to skip the pediatrician if breathing or colour worries you. See [baby snoring and mouth breathing](/baby-snoring-and-mouth-breathing).

## When to get clinical advice

A consumer monitor reading is not a diagnosis. If the baby looks unwell, is working to breathe, or you cannot rouse them, call or use emergency care *even if the gadget looks "normal."* If a wearable keeps alarming and the baby looks fine, still ask your pediatrician how to interpret it rather than chasing settings all night.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a monitor for a newborn in my room?**  
No. Room-sharing for at least the first 6 months is the AAP recommendation. A monitor is optional.

**Do I need one once they have their own room?**  
Only if it helps you hear them. It still does not prevent SIDS.

**Is a wearable sock safer than a video unit?**  
AAP does not recommend consumer cardiorespiratory monitors as a SIDS strategy. "More data" is not the same as more safety.

**Can a monitor replace room-sharing?**  
No.
