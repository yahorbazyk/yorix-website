# 12 Month Sleep Regression: Sleep Around the First Birthday

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

The first birthday brings cake, first steps, and, for some families, a sudden run of bad nights or a toddler who refuses the morning nap. It's tempting to read that as a sign your child is ready for one nap. Usually they are not. Here is what tends to drive sleep changes around age one, how much the evidence supports a "12 month sleep regression", and what to try.

If your one-year-old is sleeping fine and you just want a typical day, see the [12 month old sleep schedule](/12-month-old-sleep-schedule) instead.

## Short answer

Sleep Foundation says a **12 month sleep regression** can happen but **not every 1-year-old has one**. Some children's sleep improves or stays the same. When sleep does slip, likely contributors are **walking and other new skills, separation anxiety, teething, and nap changes**. Sleep Foundation says symptoms typically **don't last longer than a few weeks**. A common mistake is moving to one nap too soon: KidsHealth says most toddlers combine two naps into one **around 18 months or sometimes sooner**, and Boys Town places the switch at **15–18 months**.

## Is the 12 month sleep regression real?

Partly. There is no evidence that sleep worsens because a child turns one. There is evidence that **walking** can unsettle sleep. A 2026 study by Waugh and Berger found sleep disruptions around first walking steps and walking mastery, as well as around language milestones like first words. Since many children start walking close to their first birthday, a rough patch at this age is plausible, but it follows the skill, not the date.

Some 1-year-olds are not regressing at all; they simply haven't consolidated their nights yet. Sleep Foundation cites a study in which 72% of 12-month-olds slept six or more hours in a row, which means more than a quarter did not. That study found no link between sleeping through and development.

Huckleberry (app example) reaches a similar view: sleep disruption can happen at 12 months, but the idea that every child has a regression on their first birthday is a myth.

## Signs to look for

Sleep Foundation lists:

- more frequent night wakings;
- trouble getting back to sleep, with crying or agitation;
- fussiness and difficulty calming down;
- longer naps in the day.

Before blaming a regression, look for illness, teething, a change in childcare, or a schedule that no longer fits.

| Factor | How it can affect sleep | Source |

| Separation anxiety | Crying at bedtime and after night wakings | Sleep Foundation; KidsHealth |
| Teething | Discomfort that wakes the child | Sleep Foundation |
| Nap changes | Fighting the morning nap; bedtime drifting later | Sleep Foundation; Mayo Clinic |
| Language and emotional growth | More engagement, communication, and stronger attachments | Sleep Foundation; Waugh & Berger, 2026 |

## Two naps or one at 12 months?

Sources don't agree on the exact age, but they agree it is usually later than the first birthday:

- **Mayo Clinic:** babies 10–12 months old often drop the morning nap, and by age 1 or older most have. It suggests moving the afternoon nap and bedtime earlier by about half an hour after the switch.
- **KidsHealth:** most toddlers combine two naps into one around 18 months, sometimes sooner, and a toddler who fights the morning nap is probably ready for an afternoon nap only.
- **Boys Town:** the transition usually happens between 15 and 18 months.

A few days of skipped morning naps don't mean it's time to switch. Try shortening or slightly delaying the morning nap first. If refusals keep happening and your child copes well through the day, a one-nap day may suit them.

## How to help

### Keep the routine steady

Sleep Foundation says a stable bedtime routine makes it easier for children to fall asleep, and a consistent schedule for naps and bedtime helps them adjust. Leave time to wind down and say goodnight.

### Calm the room

Excess noise, light, or stimulation makes it hard for 1-year-olds to fall asleep and resettle (Sleep Foundation). Keep the hour before bed free of screens. KidsHealth advises keeping toddlers away from screen devices in the hour before bedtime.

### Use the day for energy and light

Sleep Foundation recommends plenty of daytime activity, especially outdoors in natural light, to support a healthy body clock.

### Practice separation in small doses

Sleep Foundation suggests short separations during the day, including time with another trusted adult. KidsHealth adds that a comfort object, such as a small "lovey" blanket or soft toy, is OK from about age one and can help with reassurance.

### Keep night visits brief

KidsHealth suggests quietly reassuring your toddler that you are nearby, then keeping visits short and boring, because too much interaction can backfire.

## Sleep space changes after age one

KidsHealth says that after the first birthday, a light blanket and a small comfort object are OK in the crib. Avoid large soft toys and anything with ties or strings, and remove objects your child can reach from standing.

## What can make it worse

- **Switching to one nap too early,** leaving an overtired toddler at bedtime.
- **Screens or rough play right before bed.**
- **Long, lively night visits** or new sleep props during the rough patch.
- **An inconsistent bedtime** from day to day.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Talk to your child's doctor if sleep problems last more than a few weeks or seem severe, or if poor sleep comes with fever, ear pain, loud snoring, breathing pauses, or a big drop in eating. KidsHealth also advises discussing recurring nightmares or other sleep problems that seem severe to you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a 12 month sleep regression?**  
Some children have a rough patch around their first birthday, often linked to walking or separation anxiety, but many don't (Sleep Foundation).

**How long does the 12 month sleep regression last?**  
Usually no longer than a few weeks (Sleep Foundation).

**Should my 1 year old drop to one nap?**  
Usually not yet. KidsHealth puts the typical switch at around 18 months, sometimes sooner; Boys Town says 15–18 months.

**How much sleep does a 1 year old need?**  
11–14 hours in 24 hours, including naps (AASM; KidsHealth).

**Is there an 11 month sleep regression?**  
It's the same idea: disruptions tend to follow new skills like cruising or first steps rather than a specific month.
