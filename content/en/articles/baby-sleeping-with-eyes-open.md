# Baby Sleeping With Eyes Open: Usually Active Sleep, Not a Trick

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

This page answers a scare: you look over and one or both eyes are partly open, but they are clearly asleep. It is not [baby sleep cycles](/baby-sleep-cycles) (the cycle essay) and not [baby twitching in sleep](/baby-twitching-in-sleep).

## Short answer

It is often ordinary, especially in **active (REM) sleep**, and it does not by itself mean they are in pain or "not really asleep." HealthyChildren.org's newborn sleep-stages page describes REM as the stage when the baby may twitch and the eyes move *under closed eyelids*. Other clinical parent texts on infant states (including Healthy Steps descriptions widely reused in pediatric handouts) say that in **active sleep**, the eyes are usually closed but **may open from time to time**, with irregular breathing and small movements. We report both descriptions. We do not invent a percentage of babies who do this, and we do not treat a parenting blog's "nocturnal lagophthalmos" label as a diagnosis.

You do not need to pry the lids shut. If the eyes look dry, red, or sticky after waking, or if they have trouble closing their eyes while they are clearly awake, that is a pediatrician question.

## What this usually is

Newborns and young infants spend a large share of sleep in active (REM) sleep. That is a noisier, twitchier state than the still "quiet sleep" parents expect from a photograph. Fluttering lids, a half-open eye, a brief stare that is not actually looking at you, and then a return to stillness can all sit in that state. It can continue past the newborn weeks; Parents ask the same question at 6, 8, and 9 months. There is no official week when it "must" stop.

This is different from being awake in the dark and needing a feed or a change. If they start crying, rooting, or looking at you and staying engaged, treat it as a waking.

## What parents often worry about

Parent-community threads treat a half-open eye as a medical emergency or as proof the baby is "fighting sleep with their eyes." Those are fears, not measurements.

- **Should I close their eyes?** You do not have to. A gentle lid close is unnecessary if the eyes look comfortable. Do not poke or tape anything.
- **"They have been like this for months."** Brief opening in active sleep can persist. Persistent inability to close the eyes while awake, or sore eyes, is the clinical question — not a forum duration.
- **Other odd sleep faces.** Smiling, twitching, or a brief "open" look often travel together in active sleep. See the twitching and crying-in-sleep pages if those are the main worry.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if the eyes look dry, red, crusted, or painful, if one lid droops or will not close while they are awake, if you think they cannot close the eye at all, or if the opening is new after an injury or illness. Emergency care is for a baby you cannot wake, who is struggling to breathe, or whose colour changes suddenly — not for a peaceful baby with a half-open lid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it normal for a newborn to sleep with their eyes open?**  
Brief opening in active sleep is described in infant-state guides. It looks odd. It is not, by itself, a reason to wake them.

**Why does my 6 or 8 month old still do this?**  
There is no official stop-date. If the eyes look well and they close them when awake, it can still be ordinary. Ask if you are worried or if the eyes look irritated.

**Does this mean they are not getting deep sleep?**  
No. Active sleep is a real sleep state. Quiet, still sleep happens in other parts of the cycle. See [baby sleep cycles](/baby-sleep-cycles).

**Should I add drops or a humidifier?**  
Do not start eye drops unless a clinician has said so. Room humidity is a comfort choice, not a treatment plan on this page.

**Is this the same as twitching or grunting in sleep?**  
Related only because active sleep is busy. Use [baby twitching in sleep](/baby-twitching-in-sleep) or [newborn grunting in sleep](/newborn-grunting-in-sleep) for those sounds and movements.
