# 2 Year Old Sleep Schedule: Naps, Bedtime, and Totals

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

A two-year-old's "schedule" is mostly a consistent wake time, **one afternoon nap**, and a bedtime that protects the day's total. This is not the [2-year sleep regression](/sleep-regression/2-years) page, which is about stalling, nap strikes, and other bumps. It is also not the [crib-to-bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed) how-to, and it is not a second copy of the [18-month one-nap day](/18-month-old-sleep-schedule).

## Short answer

For ages 1 to 2 years, the AASM (AAP-endorsed) range is still **11–14 hours over a full day, nap included**. At the second birthday, almost all children who still nap take **one nap**. Sleep Foundation, citing research, describes a typical 2-year-old nap as about **1.5 to 3 hours**, and says most children have already dropped to one nap by 18 to 24 months. Night sleep does more of the work than it did at 12 months. Some two-year-olds are already starting to skip the odd nap; that is a nudge toward [when toddlers stop napping](/when-do-toddlers-stop-napping), not an automatic reason to drop it if evenings fall apart.

### The numbers that matter most

| Question | Sourced range or typical figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total sleep at 1–2 years | 11–14 hours over a full day, nap included | AASM; KidsHealth; Cleveland Clinic |
| Naps at age 2 | Usually one, often 1.5–3 hours | Sleep Foundation; KidsHealth |
| Just ahead (age 3–5) | 10–13 hours, which may include a nap | AASM / Cleveland Clinic (preschoolers) |
| When most children stop napping | Mayo Clinic: most have stopped by age 5. Sleep Foundation (citing Weissbluth): almost all still nap at 3; 60% at 4; fewer than 30% at 5 | Mayo Clinic; Sleep Foundation |

## A typical two-year-old day (example only)

Clock times are a sketch. Protect the total, not a famous bedtime on social media.

| Clock time (illustration only) | A possible shape of the day |
| --- | --- |
| 7:00 a.m. | Up for the day |
| Morning | Breakfast, play, or a childcare drop-off |
| Late morning to midday | The stretch since waking is now long; lunch before the rest often helps |
| Roughly 12:30–2:30 p.m. | The nap (Sleep Foundation's typical length is about 1.5–3 hours). A nap that ends very late can shove the evening out |
| Afternoon | A snack, then play or time outside |
| Early to mid evening | The usual [bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby), then lights out |

If this looks almost like the 18-month example, that is because the official sleep range has not changed yet. What is different at two is language, boundary-testing, and sometimes a [crib that is no longer safe](/crib-to-toddler-bed) — not a new AASM number.

## Bedtime, not a battle plan

A short, boring, predictable routine still helps (Mindell and colleagues' bedtime-routine research, as used on our routine page). If the nap was skipped, move bedtime earlier rather than "running down the clock." If nights have turned into curtain calls and stalling, that behavior guide belongs on the [2-year regression](/sleep-regression/2-years) page, not here.

We do not invent a single official 2-year-old bedtime. Count backward from the morning wake so the 11–14 hour total, including any nap, is plausible — the approach Cleveland Clinic describes as "clock math."

If a new baby has just arrived and nights have gone sideways, that wobble is covered on [sleep regression](/sleep-regression) — it is a context, not a new official schedule or a fourth regression age. The 11–14 hour total on this page does not change.

## Naps at two: keep, cap, or question

- **Keep** the nap if your child is clearly better for it and bedtime still works.
- **Cap** a very long or late nap if it is the reason bedtime has slid past a reasonable hour. Sleep Foundation notes that, in general, it is better to shorten a nap than to push bedtime later when the nap is the problem — a point they make in the "stop napping" article, and it already applies at two.
- **Question** the nap only if they consistently cannot fall asleep at nap time, nights are late, or mornings are very early, *and* they are fine on days without it. Then read [when toddlers stop napping](/when-do-toddlers-stop-napping) and [baby won't nap](/baby-wont-nap).

Daycare often has a fixed rest period. You may not control the clock there; you can still protect bedtime at home.

## When a doctor should hear about sleep

Bring it up if the 24-hour total is often under 11 hours and days are a struggle, if snoring, gasping, or very restless sleep worries you, or if you want a safety check before a crib-to-bed move.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much sleep does a 2-year-old need?**  
AASM, KidsHealth, and Cleveland Clinic all use 11–14 hours across the whole day, nap included.

**How long should a 2-year-old nap?**  
Sleep Foundation cites about 1.5 to 3 hours as typical. Individual days vary.

**What time should a 2-year-old go to bed?**  
Pick a lights-out that, together with the morning wake and the nap, can still reach a sensible daily total. No AAP clock hour exists.

**Is a 2-year sleep regression real?**  
Sleep can wobble at this age. We treat that as a separate question: [2-year sleep regression](/sleep-regression/2-years).

**Should I move them to a toddler bed at 2?**  
Only if the crib is no longer safe or they are clearly ready. See [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed).

**I have a 19–23 month old. Is this page too late?**
Use [18 month old sleep schedule](/18-month-old-sleep-schedule) if they are still squarely in the one-nap 18-month picture. Use this page as you approach the second birthday, especially for nap refusals, bedtime stalling, and crib-to-bed questions.
