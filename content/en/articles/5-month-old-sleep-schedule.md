# 5 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Moving to 3 Naps and Steadier Nights

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Five months is often the age when a day finally starts to look like a plan. Many babies can now stay happily awake for a couple of hours, and a fourth catnap begins to feel unnecessary. At the same time, plenty of 5-month-olds still wake at night, take 30-minute naps, or roll onto their tummy and protest about it. This guide covers what is typical, how a 3-nap day can look, and what tends to help.

## Short answer

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (endorsed by the AAP) recommends **12–16 hours of sleep per 24 hours, naps included, for babies 4–12 months old**. KidsHealth says babies of 4–7 months usually take **at least two naps** and about **3–4 hours of daytime sleep** in total. Many 5-month-olds are on **three naps**, with a short late-afternoon nap still common. Night waking is normal for many babies: two NHS services report that around **4–5 months** waking often comes back after an earlier calmer phase.

### Key numbers at a glance

| Question | What the sources say | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total sleep in 24 h | 12–16 hours (4–12 months) | AASM, endorsed by AAP |
| Daytime sleep | About 3–4 hours, at least 2 naps (4–7 months) | KidsHealth |

## How much sleep does a 5 month old need?

The AASM range of 12 to 16 hours is wide on purpose. Some babies thrive near the bottom of it, others near the top. Tresillian puts the typical figure for 3–6 months at about 14–15 hours.

Night length varies a lot at this age:

- Newcastle Hospitals NHS says that by 5 months about half of babies settle for an 8-hour stretch on some nights.
- Derbyshire NHS describes night stretches of up to 8 hours by about 5 months, with a full night of sleep more typical closer to the first birthday.
- Pregnancy, Birth and Baby gives up to 6–8 hours for the longest stretch at 3–6 months.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry aims for about 14.5 hours, with 11–12 hours at night and 3–4 naps. Napper describes 12–15 hours, with about 10–11 hours at night and 2–3 naps.

## Example 3-nap day for a 5 month old

**Example only.** Shift the times to match your baby's wake-up time, and treat the nap lengths as rough guides.

| Time | What happens | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6:45 a.m. | Wake for the day, milk feed | Open the curtains straight away |
| 8:45–10:00 a.m. | Nap 1 | Often the most reliable nap of the day |
| 10:00 a.m. | Milk feed, play on the floor | Practice rolling both ways |
| 12:15–1:45 p.m. | Nap 2 | The longest nap for many babies |
| 1:45 p.m. | Milk feed, outing | Daylight and fresh air |
| 4:00–4:40 p.m. | Nap 3 (short) | Keep it brief to protect bedtime |
| 6:15 p.m. | Bath, pajamas, milk feed, book | Same order every night |
| 7:00 p.m. | Asleep in the crib | |
| Overnight | 0–2 feeds, depending on your baby | Dim lights, no play |

This example adds up to about 3 hours 25 minutes of daytime sleep, inside the KidsHealth 3–4 hour range. Mayo Clinic notes that a third nap is typically dropped by about 9 months, so there is usually no rush to cut it now.

## From 4 naps to 3: signs and timing

You will probably not need to "decide" this. Your baby's day tends to make it obvious.

| Sign | What it may mean |
| --- | --- |
| The last nap of the day is fought or skipped | The day may have room for only three naps now |
| Bedtime keeps drifting later | Too much daytime sleep for the current night |
| Your baby is calm after 2+ hours awake | Longer awake periods are coming in |
| The first two naps lengthen | The rest of the day is shifting |

Huckleberry (app example) links the switch to babies managing about 2–3 hours of awake time. Cleveland Clinic's wake window range for 5–7 months is 2–4 hours. Our [wake windows guide](/wake-windows-by-age) shows the full chart.

For a slightly fuller process (what to do on mixed days, and why this is not the later 3-to-2 drop), see [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age#the-4-to-3-nap-transition) and, when that later drop arrives, [3-to-2 nap transition](/3-to-2-nap-transition).

## Is 3 to 2 naps too early at 5 months?

For most babies, yes. Mayo Clinic suggests two naps (for example, around 9 a.m. and 1 p.m.) from 4 months to a year, and puts the loss of the third nap at about 9 months. App guidance from Huckleberry suggests the 3-to-2 transition by about 8 months. If a 5-month-old seems to "refuse" the third nap, look first at whether the nap is simply scheduled too close to the second one or too close to bedtime.

Signs that two naps are not enough yet include crankiness by late afternoon, a meltdown at bedtime, or more night waking.

## 5 month old wake windows

Five months straddles two chart bands, which explains why advice online ranges so widely:

- **Cleveland Clinic:** 1.25–2.5 hours up to 4 months, then 2–4 hours from 5 to 7 months.
- **Sleep Foundation:** 1.5–4.5 hours for 5–7 months.
- **Huckleberry (app example):** babies awake about 1.5–2.5 hours tend to need 4 naps; once the fourth nap goes, most need 2–3 hours.
- **Napper (app example):** about 2–3 hours, often longest first thing in the morning.

In practice, a 5-month-old on four naps is usually near 1.5–2 hours, and one on three naps nearer 2–2.5 hours *(our reading of the ranges above)*. No medical body sets wake windows, so let your baby's cues have the final say. See the [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) chart for the months before and after.

## Why naps are often 30–40 minutes

Tresillian says sleep cycles at 4–5 months last around 30–50 minutes, and waking after 30–40 minutes is very common. A short nap is not a sign that something is wrong. It may help to:

- Wait a few minutes before going in after a short nap, in case your baby resettles.
- Keep the room similar for every nap: same sleep sack, same space, similar light.
- Look at the full day. If your baby is mostly content and night sleep is fine, short naps may simply suit them right now.

## Night waking at 5 months

Both Newcastle Hospitals NHS and Derbyshire NHS describe night waking often returning at **4–5 months**, after many babies had a calmer spell. HealthyChildren.org says that babies who are held or rocked all the way to sleep may struggle to get back to sleep when they wake at night.

Practical steps:

- **Pause before you respond.** KidsHealth suggests waiting a few minutes before going in, because some babies settle on their own.
- **Put your baby down drowsy but awake** at least for bedtime (KidsHealth, HealthyChildren.org).
- **Keep night feeds calm and quiet.** Whether your baby still needs a night feed depends on many factors; ask your pediatrician if you are unsure.
- **Use comfort that you can keep doing.** Mayo Clinic suggests that from about 4 months, short check-ins with soothing words can help.

## Is there a 5 month sleep regression?

There is no separate clinical "5-month regression." What parents call by that name is usually the same sleep shift that begins around 3 to 4 months (Sleep Foundation) together with a busier body and brain. If sleep worsened suddenly and your baby is also unwell, feeding less, or in pain, talk with your pediatrician. Otherwise, our [4 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/4-months) guide explains what is going on.

## Rolling, solids, and other 5-month changes

- **Rolling.** HealthyChildren.org (AAP) says to keep placing your baby on their back, but once they roll both ways on their own, you do not need to turn them back over. Keep the crib bare. Swaddling should already have stopped once your baby showed signs of rolling (KidsHealth).
- **Solids.** The AAP recommends breast milk only for about the first 6 months. HealthyChildren.org lists readiness signs such as good head control, sitting supported, and interest in food. Cleveland Clinic warns against adding cereal to a bottle. There is no fetched evidence that starting solids early improves sleep, so we don't recommend it for that purpose.
- **Crying peak passing.** Tresillian notes that normal crying peaks earlier in infancy and decreases naturally from about 5 months.

## What can make it worse

- An overtired baby at bedtime because the last nap was dropped too early.
- A third nap that runs late and pushes bedtime back.
- New props at every wake-up (rocking, then feeding, then a car ride), so your baby never gets a chance to learn one way to settle.
- A bedroom that changes from night to night.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Contact your pediatrician if your baby snores loudly or pauses in breathing during sleep, is feeding much less, has fewer wet diapers, has a fever or seems unwell, or if sleep problems leave you struggling to cope. Your own wellbeing matters too.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps should a 5 month old take?**  
Most take 2–3 naps (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby; KidsHealth says at least two). Many have three at this age.

**How long should a 5 month old be awake between naps?**  
Cleveland Clinic suggests 2–4 hours for 5–7 months. Most 5-month-olds are near the shorter end.

**What time should bedtime be for a 5 month old?**  
There is no fixed rule. Pick a bedtime that leaves a reasonable awake period after the last nap and fits your family. Many families choose an early evening time.

**Is it normal for a 5 month old to wake up at night?**  
Yes. NHS services report that night waking often returns at 4–5 months.

**Should I drop to two naps at 5 months?**  
Usually not. The third nap typically goes at about 9 months (Mayo Clinic).
