# 6 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Naps, Wake Windows, Bedtime Routine, and a Simple Plan

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Six months is often the first age at which a day starts to feel plannable. Naps become more predictable, the longest night stretch is usually longer than it was at 3 or 4 months, and solid foods begin to appear on the menu. This page puts everything a parent searches for at this age in one place: how much sleep is typical, how many naps, wake windows, example days, a bedtime routine, and a step-by-step sleep plan. If sleep has suddenly got worse, read our separate [6 month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/6-months) page instead.

## Short answer

Babies from 4 to 12 months need **12–16 hours of sleep in 24 hours, naps included** (American Academy of Sleep Medicine, endorsed by the AAP). KidsHealth says that by 6 months most babies sleep **9 hours or longer at night**, with brief wakings, and get about **3–4 hours of daytime sleep** in **at least two naps**. Many 6-month-olds still take **three naps**, with the third usually short; Mayo Clinic suggests dropping a late-afternoon third nap at about **9 months**. Published **wake windows** for this age run from about **2 to 4 hours** (Cleveland Clinic, 5–7 months). A **20- to 30-minute bedtime routine** in the same order each night is one of the best-supported things you can do (Cleveland Clinic; Mindell et al., 2009).

## How much should a 6 month old sleep?

The official range is wide on purpose. The **AASM recommends 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours** for 4–12 months. Within that, the sources describe a typical shape:

- **Night:** KidsHealth says most babies sleep 9 hours or longer at night by 6 months. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby gives 10–14 hours of night sleep for 6–12 months.
- **Day:** about 3–4 hours across naps (KidsHealth, 4–7 months), or 2–4 hours (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby, 6–12 months).

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry suggests about 14 hours in total, split into 11–12 hours at night and 2.5–3.5 hours over 3 naps. Napper describes 12–15 hours, with 10–11 at night and 2–5 hours over 2–3 naps.

A baby who is growing well, feeding well, and mostly content when awake is getting enough sleep, even if the numbers don't match a chart exactly.

## 6 month old nap schedule: how many naps?

Most 6-month-olds take **three naps**, while some are already managing on two longer ones:

- **KidsHealth** says babies of 4–7 months take at least two naps a day.
- **Mayo Clinic** says babies from 4 months to 1 year nap at least twice a day, morning and early afternoon, and some also need a late-afternoon nap. It suggests cutting out that third nap at about 9 months, which helps move bedtime earlier.
- **App example:** Huckleberry says most babies aren't ready to drop the third nap until 7–9 months and suggests the first two naps last 60–90 minutes each, with the third around 30–45 minutes.

**How long should naps be?** There's no clinical target. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby describes naps of up to 2 hours at 3–6 months. Mayo Clinic suggests letting babies nap as long as they want unless they then struggle to fall asleep at night. A short third nap is normal: its job is to bridge the gap to bedtime.

**Three naps or two?** If your baby fights the third nap most days and copes well without it, try a two-nap day with an earlier bedtime. If two-nap days end in an exhausted, crying baby by 5 p.m., go back to three for now *(practical suggestion)*. Our [baby nap schedule by age](/baby-nap-schedule-by-age) explains the 3-to-2 transition in more detail.

## 6 month wake windows

A wake window is the time from waking up until falling asleep again, feeds included. There is no official AAP or AASM figure. The published ranges are:

| Source | Age band | Wake window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleveland Clinic | 5–7 months | 2–4 hours |
| Sleep Foundation | 5–7 months | 1.5–4.5 hours |
| Huckleberry (app example) | 6 months | 2–3 hours, longest before bed |
| Napper (app example) | 6 months | 2.5–3.5 hours |

**How to use them:** start the first window of the day near the lower end (about 2 hours), lengthen slightly through the day, and make the stretch before bedtime the longest, as Huckleberry suggests. Then let your baby's cues correct the numbers. Nationwide Children's Hospital lists fussing or crying, eye rubbing, staring into space, and pulling on the ears as common sleepy signs, and says babies fall asleep more easily if you put them down as soon as they show them.

## Night sleep and night feeds at 6 months

- **Feeds.** KidsHealth says most babies of 4–7 months no longer need a middle-of-the-night feed, though some, particularly breastfed babies, still wake for one. Nationwide Children's Hospital says that by 6 months most babies are physiologically able to sleep through the night without feeding, yet 25–50% still wake at night. Ask your pediatrician before dropping night feeds, especially if weight gain has been slow.
- **Formula amounts.** HealthyChildren.org (AAP) says that by 6 months a formula-fed baby typically takes 6–8 ounces at each of 4 or 5 feeds in 24 hours.
- **"Sleeping through" isn't universal.** In a study of 388 infants, 27.9% to 57.0% of babies at 6 and 12 months were not sleeping through the night, depending on whether the definition was 6 or 8 hours, and this was not linked to development (Pennestri et al., 2018).
- **All babies wake.** Nationwide Children's explains that all babies wake briefly 4–6 times a night. The difference is whether they can settle back without help.

## Bedtime routine for a 6 month old

A routine is the one sleep habit with direct trial evidence at this age. In a randomized study of 405 families with children aged 7–36 months (slightly older than 6 months), a consistent nightly routine improved how quickly children fell asleep and reduced the number and length of night wakings within weeks, and mothers' mood improved too (Mindell et al., 2009).

A simple 20–30 minute routine, built from what the sources suggest:

1. **Milk feed first, or just before the routine.** Cleveland Clinic suggests feeding at the *start* of the routine so your baby doesn't rely on feeding to fall asleep. Nationwide Children's goes a step further and advises keeping bedtime feeds out of the bedtime routine after 6 months, so some families offer the last feed before the bath.
2. **Bath or wash and clean pajamas.** KidsHealth describes a warm bath followed by stories or singing as a good end-of-day signal.
3. **Brush the first teeth.** The AAP's Brush, Book, Bed program suggests brushing with a smear of fluoride toothpaste once a tooth appears. Don't put your baby to bed with a bottle (HealthyChildren.org).
4. **A short book or song.** Brush, Book, Bed suggests reading aloud together every day, even for 15 minutes.
5. **Into the crib drowsy but awake.** Finish in the room where your baby sleeps, with the lights low (Nationwide Children's).

Keep the steps in the same order every night, including weekends.

## A simple sleep plan for a 6 month old

If you want to improve sleep rather than just track it, here is a gentle plan using only what the sources support:

1. **Fix the wake-up time.** Start the day at about the same time each morning. Newcastle Hospitals NHS says this, with daylight, helps set your baby's body clock.
2. **Offer naps by cues and windows.** Use the windows above as a guide and put your baby down at the first sleepy signs.
3. **Protect the routine.** Same steps, same order, same place, every night.
4. **Practice falling asleep in the crib.** KidsHealth suggests putting your baby down drowsy but awake. If they cry, wait a few minutes, then soothe briefly without picking them up, and repeat as needed, as long as you know they are safe, fed, and well.
5. **Keep night visits brief.** KidsHealth notes that cuddling, feeding, or talking at every waking can teach a baby to wake for that attention.
6. **Give it about two weeks before judging** *(practical suggestion)*. In the Mindell trial, routine effects were measured over two weeks.

If you are considering a more structured method, our [sleep training methods guide](/sleep-training-methods-explained) compares the options.

## What else is changing at 6 months

- **Movement.** CDC says that by 6 months most babies roll from tummy to back, push up on straight arms, and lean on their hands to sit. KidsHealth says that once your baby rolls both ways consistently, they can stay in the position they choose, but always place them on their back to start. Nationwide Children's advises removing mobiles and hanging crib toys by about 5 months.
- **Solids.** The AAP supports starting solid foods at about 6 months alongside breast milk or formula (HealthyChildren.org). We found no evidence that starting solids improves sleep.
- **Cup.** HealthyChildren.org says you can offer a cup when solids start and a little water (4–8 ounces a day) from about 6 months.
- **Where baby sleeps.** The AAP recommends room-sharing without bed-sharing for at least the first 6 months (KidsHealth).

## What can make a 6 month schedule harder

- **Dropping the third nap too early** and ending up with an overtired baby at bedtime.
- **Feeding to sleep at every sleep,** which can make night wakings harder to settle (Cleveland Clinic; Nationwide Children's).
- **Keeping your baby up to "sleep better tonight."** Nationwide Children's warns that cutting naps leads to overtiredness and a worse night.
- **Changing the sleep setup** often, or keeping lighting different at bedtime from the rest of the night (Nationwide Children's).
- **A bottle in the crib** (HealthyChildren.org).

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Talk to your pediatrician if your baby snores loudly or seems to pause breathing in sleep, is unusually hard to wake, is feeding poorly or gaining weight slowly, or if broken nights continue for weeks. CDC also suggests telling your doctor if your baby has lost skills they once had or isn't meeting milestones.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps does a 6 month old need?**  
Usually three, sometimes two. KidsHealth says at least two; Mayo Clinic suggests dropping the late-afternoon third nap at about 9 months.

**What are normal 6 month wake windows?**  
About 2–4 hours (Cleveland Clinic, 5–7 months), shortest in the morning and longest before bed.

**What time should a 6 month old go to bed?**  
There is no official bedtime. Choose a time that follows the last nap by a full wake window, often between about 6:30 and 8 p.m. in the examples above, and earlier on days with short naps.

**Should a 6 month old sleep through the night?**  
Many can, but many don't. Nationwide Children's says 25–50% still wake at night at this age.

**What is a good sleep routine for a 6 month old?**  
A 20–30 minute routine in the same order every night: feed, bath or wash, teeth, a book or song, and into the crib drowsy but awake.

**Is 6 months a good age for a sleep plan?**  
Yes, many families start one now. KidsHealth describes a gentle drowsy-but-awake approach for 4–7 months.

**Can starting solids affect sleep?**
Solids often start around the same weeks as other 6-month changes, so nights can look bumpier at the same time. We do not have an official AAP figure for hours of sleep gained or lost from the first spoons. Do not drop night milk feeds just because solids have started, unless your pediatrician has already agreed a plan. See [6 month old sleep schedule](/6-month-old-sleep-schedule).
