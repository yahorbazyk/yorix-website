# 9 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Settling Into Two Naps

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Nine months is when most schedules shrink to **two naps**. The third, late-afternoon catnap is usually gone or on its way out, wake windows stretch toward three hours or more, and bedtime often moves a little earlier to make up for it. At the same time, many 9-month-olds become clingy and wary of strangers, which can show up at bedtime. This page gives you the numbers, a sample day, and a calm way to handle the evening.

## Short answer

A 9-month-old still needs **12–16 hours of sleep in every 24**, and KidsHealth expects **9–12 of them overnight**, with the rest in **two naps**. Mayo Clinic suggests cutting out a late-afternoon third nap at about **9 months**, which lets bedtime move earlier. Cleveland Clinic's wake-window chart gives **2.5 to 4.5 hours** for this age band. The CDC's 9-month advice is short and practical: **have routines for sleeping and feeding**, because consistent sleep times make it easier.

## How much sleep does a 9 month old need?

The CDC's 9-month milestone page repeats the AASM range: **12 to 16 hours a day for 4- to 12-month-olds, including naps**. KidsHealth puts 9–12 of those hours at night. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby describes 2–4 hours of naps for 6–12 months.

**App examples (not clinical guidance):** Huckleberry's 9-month target is close to 14 hours a day, two of them or a little more in naps. Napper's page pictures two naps of roughly an hour to an hour and a half.

## Saying goodbye to the third nap

Mayo Clinic recommends a morning and an early-afternoon nap from 4 months to 1 year and suggests cutting a late-afternoon third nap at about 9 months. Huckleberry (app example) says most babies are ready to drop it by 9 months and lists signs like shorter naps, resisting one nap (often the last), and resisting bedtime.

**How to drop it** *(practical suggestion)*:

1. Push the morning nap 15–30 minutes later over a few days.
2. Let the afternoon nap start after lunch rather than straight after the morning one.
3. On days when the afternoon nap ends early, bring bedtime forward. Nationwide Children's Hospital warns that cutting naps without replacing the sleep leads to overtiredness and a worse night.
4. If your baby is falling apart by 5 p.m. most days, a 15–20 minute catnap is still fine for a week or two.

## 9 month wake windows

- **Clinical charts (7–10 months):** Cleveland Clinic says 2.5 to 4.5 hours; Sleep Foundation's band is wider, 1.5 to 6 hours.
- **App examples for 9 months:** Huckleberry uses 2.75 to 3.5 hours; Napper says 3 to 4.

That works out to roughly **3 hours before the first nap, a bit more before the second, and 3.5 hours or so before bed** for many babies *(our reading of the app examples, not a clinical figure)*. The two apps even disagree about which window is longer: Napper says babies often stay awake longer in the morning, while Huckleberry's sample has the shortest window first. Follow your baby's signs.

## Clinginess at bedtime: what helps

The CDC lists being **shy, clingy, or fearful around strangers** and **reacting when you leave** as 9-month milestones. KidsHealth says separation anxiety commonly appears between 8 months and 1 year. That can make the moment you walk out of the bedroom the hardest part of the day.

- **Say goodnight, don't sneak out.** The CDC's 9-month tip is to say a quick, cheerful goodbye instead of sneaking away, even if your baby cries.
- **Short, loving check-ins.** KidsHealth describes a few calm words from the bedroom door and a quick exit, lengthening the time between visits.
- **Don't add new comfort habits at night.** KidsHealth notes that cuddling, feeding, or talking at each waking can encourage regular waking for that attention.
- **A comfort object later.** Nationwide Children's Hospital suggests introducing a security object, like a small stuffed animal or blanket, by 12 months. Until the first birthday, KidsHealth advises keeping soft objects and blankets out of the crib.

## Night feeds at 9 months

Nationwide Children's Hospital says that by 6 months most babies are physiologically able to sleep through the night without feeding, but 25–50% still wake. Newcastle Hospitals NHS adds that some babies start waking more often again at 8–9 months, and that most babies are at least a year old before they settle all night, every night. If your baby still feeds at night and you'd like to cut back, check with your pediatrician first, especially if weight gain is a concern. The [dream feed](/dream-feed) page explains one way some families shift a feed earlier.

## Getting ready for the first birthday

- **Pulling up and standing.** The CDC's 9-month tip is to put your baby close to things they can safely pull up on during the day.
- **Crib check.** KidsHealth advises looking for anything your baby can reach from sitting or standing, such as blind cords or curtains.
- **Cup practice.** HealthyChildren.org suggests reducing bottle feeds gradually and finishing the switch to a cup between 12 and 18 months, starting with bottles at naps and bedtime.

## What can make a 9 month schedule harder

- Keeping a late third nap that pushes bedtime toward 8:30 p.m. or later.
- Sneaking out at bedtime, which can make separation worries worse (CDC tip).
- Changing the bedtime routine on alternate nights.
- Handing over a bottle once your baby is lying in the crib, which HealthyChildren.org advises against.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Speak to your doctor if your baby snores loudly or seems to stop breathing in sleep, isn't gaining weight, seems unusually tired or hard to wake, isn't sitting without support, or has lost skills. The CDC's milestone checklist is a useful starting point for that conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many naps should a 9 month old take?**  
Usually two. Mayo Clinic suggests cutting the late-afternoon third nap at about 9 months.

**What are 9 month wake windows?**  
Apps suggest roughly 2.75 to 4 hours; the Cleveland Clinic band for 7–10 months is 2.5 to 4.5.

**What time should a 9 month old go to bed?**  
Anywhere from about half past six to eight is typical; short-nap days call for the earlier end.

**Why is my 9 month old suddenly clingy at bedtime?**  
Separation anxiety is common between 8 months and 1 year (KidsHealth). Keep goodbyes short and cheerful.

**Should a 9 month old still wake to feed?**  
Some do. Nationwide Children's says 25–50% of babies still wake after 6 months. Ask your pediatrician before night weaning.
