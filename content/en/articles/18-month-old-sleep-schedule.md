# 18 Month Old Sleep Schedule: Life on One Nap

**By the Yorix team**  
**Published:** [Month DD, YYYY] · **Updated:** [Month DD, YYYY]

*This article is for general information and is not medical advice. It is not a substitute for care from your baby's pediatrician or another qualified clinician. If you are worried about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, or breathing, contact your pediatrician.*

Eighteen months is often the first stretch of a **settled one-nap day**, not the first time you have heard about one nap. The [15-month sleep schedule](/15-month-old-sleep-schedule) and our [2-to-1 nap transition](/2-to-1-nap-transition) page own the switch itself. The [18-month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/18-months) owns the bump. This page is the ordinary timetable once (or as) that nap has settled — totals, a typical day, and bedtime — not a second sample-day clone of 15 months and not a rewrite of the regression article.

## Short answer

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AAP-endorsed) recommends **11 to 14 hours of sleep in 24 hours** for children ages 1 to 2 years, including naps. KidsHealth and Cleveland Clinic use the same toddler range. By 18 months, **most** toddlers are down to **one daytime nap** (KidsHealth; Sleep Foundation describes one nap as typical by 18 to 24 months). That nap is often in the early afternoon and may last from about 1.5 to 3 hours — Sleep Foundation cites that length for a typical 2-year-old nap; 18-month naps are in the same ballpark, not a separate official number. Nights are usually longer than they were at 12 months. If your 18-month-old is still clearly on two naps and is happy, you do not have to force the drop this week; see the transition page.

## A typical one-nap day (example only)

This is a picture, not a test. Clock times move with your household's morning.

| Time (example) | What might happen |
| --- | --- |
| 6:30–7:30 a.m. | Wake and breakfast |
| Morning | Play, snack, outdoor time if you can |
| 12:00–1:00 p.m. | Lunch, then the nap (often 1.5–3 hours) |
| Afternoon | Snack, play; try not to let the nap run so late that bedtime collapses |
| 6:30–8:00 p.m. | [Bedtime routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) and lights out, set so the 11–14 hour total is realistic |

We are not publishing a separate 18-month wake-window chart. Our [wake windows by age](/wake-windows-by-age) guide already covers the first-year numbers; by this age, a consistent wake time, one well-timed nap, and an earlier bedtime on short-nap days usually matter more than a stopwatch.

## Bedtime at 18 months

A predictable routine still earns its keep (the same Mindell evidence we use on the [routine](/bedtime-routine-for-baby) page). If the afternoon nap was short or skipped, move bedtime earlier the way you would during the [2-to-1 transition](/2-to-1-nap-transition), rather than hoping a late, overtired evening will stretch the night. If the nap ran long and bedtime is a battle, the next day's nap may need a slightly earlier cap — without turning every afternoon into a fight.

Night feeds are no longer a given at this age for many toddlers; that is a [night-weaning](/night-weaning) question for your pediatrician, not a schedule rule.

## When to talk to your pediatrician

Ask if total sleep is regularly under the 11-hour end of the AASM range and your toddler is struggling by day, if snoring or pauses worry you, or if you are unsure whether a remaining night feed is still needed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much sleep does an 18-month-old need?**  
11 to 14 hours in 24 hours, including the nap, according to AASM, KidsHealth, and Cleveland Clinic.

**How many naps should an 18-month-old take?**  
Most take one. Some are still shifting from two. Follow the child's actual day, not the birthday.

**What time should an 18-month-old go to bed?**  
Whatever time, plus the nap, adds up to a reasonable total before a consistent morning wake. Many families land in the early-to-mid evening; we do not have an official AAP clock time.

**Is there an 18-month sleep regression?**  
Sleep can get bumpier around language, independence, and the nap change. That is a separate article: [18-month sleep regression](/sleep-regression/18-months).

**Should I drop the pacifier or the crib now?**  
Not because of the birthday. See [pacifier and sleep](/pacifier-and-sleep) and [crib to toddler bed](/crib-to-toddler-bed).
