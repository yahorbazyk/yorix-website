```markdown
## Camping, and very hot or cold nights away from home

Safer sleep does not pause in a tent. Red Nose and the Lullaby Trust both treat sleep away from home as the same rules in a different building (or in no building): **on the back**, on a **firm, flat**, approved surface, with a clear space and no loose adult bedding. A pillow nest on an air mattress, a baby on a quilt between adults, or a car seat left at the edge of a tent is not a campsite exception.

Bring a travel crib, play yard, or other approved product that still fits the baby, and use only its own mattress. Keep the sleep space off a soggy groundsheet and away from heater vents and stove fumes. In hot weather, Red Nose’s summer advice and the Lullaby Trust’s hot-weather pages agree with [how to dress a baby for sleep](/how-to-dress-baby-for-sleep): light layers, no hat for sleep, shade, and a check of the chest — not an extra blanket “in case.” In the cold, add a layer or a well-fitting sleep bag; do not use a hot-water bottle, a heating pad, or a heavy duvet in the baby’s space, and do not bring the baby into an adult sleeping bag.

If the only option looks unsafe, it is better to change the plan than to improvise a nest. Temperature and dressing details stay on the dressing page; this section is the travel-and-camping frame.
```

```markdown
**Why won’t my baby or toddler sleep after we moved house?**
A new room, new noises, and a temporarily unpacked crib can unsettle sleep. That is a change of place, not a new official regression age. Keep the same safe-sleep rules in the new home: back, firm, flat, clear, in your room if they are still in the first months. Rebuild the same short bedtime routine you already use — see [bedtime routine for baby](/bedtime-routine-for-baby). A travel crib or play yard is fine overnight if it meets safety standards and has only a tight fitted sheet (see the pack-and-play FAQ on [when to move baby to crib](/when-to-move-baby-to-crib)). If an older child is frightened of the new dark room, use [fear of the dark](/fear-of-the-dark) and [night lights and baby sleep](/night-lights-and-baby-sleep), not a new URL.
```

## 7. Pack-and-play / play yard overnight (high volume, still not a new URL)

**Targets:** `/when-to-move-baby-to-crib` (primary; FAQ already asks “Is a mini-crib or play yard okay?”), `/safe-sleep-for-babies`, `/baby-sleep-and-travel`

**Where:** Expand the existing FAQ. Do **not** publish `/can-baby-sleep-in-pack-and-play`.

```markdown
**Can a newborn or older baby sleep in a pack-and-play overnight, at home or when visiting?**
Yes, if the play yard meets current safety standards, the mattress is the one that came with it (or an approved replacement), it is firm and flat, and there is only a tight fitted sheet — no extra mattress, no soft topper, no loose blankets. That is the same rule already on this page and on [safe sleep for babies](/safe-sleep-for-babies). It can be used overnight at home or when you travel ([baby sleep and travel](/baby-sleep-and-travel)). Stop when the child has reached the manufacturer’s height or weight limit, or can climb out. A pack-and-play is not a reason to relax back-sleeping or to add bumpers.
```
