**Paste this heading and body:**

```markdown
## “Eat, play, sleep” — a flexible pattern, not a law

Some parent sites, including Raising Children Network, describe a **feed, play, then sleep** rhythm as one way a day can go once a baby is a little older: a feed, a short stretch of awake time, then a nap, rather than feeding every baby all the way to sleep, every time. It is a **cue-led example**, not an official AAP protocol and not a timetable for a day-old baby.

Newborns on this page are **not** ready for a fixed three-step law. They need frequent feeds, including at night, and they often fall asleep at the breast or bottle — that is ordinary, not a failed routine. If a popular graphic tells you to “never feed to sleep” in week one, ignore the graphic and stay with the flexible day already sketched above. For a later bedtime pattern, see [bedtime routine for baby](/bedtime-routine-for-baby). For frequent evening feeds, see [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding).
```

```markdown
**How do we protect baby sleep when a parent works nights, or when there is only one adult?**
Keep the *baby’s* day and night the right way around — light and ordinary noise by day, a dim, boring night — even if a parent is on a night shift. Do not flip the baby onto the parent’s work clock. The same short bedtime routine can be done by whoever is on duty; the sequence matters more than which adult does it. See [bedtime routine for baby](/bedtime-routine-for-baby). Safe sleep does not relax because you are exhausted: back, firm, flat, clear, and never a sofa or armchair (the sofa risk is already on [bed-sharing and co-sleeping](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping)). If you are dangerously tired, ask for help for you as well — the mental-health and exhaustion advice already on this page still applies when there is no second adult to “take a shift.”
```

### B. `/bedtime-routine-for-baby` — baby massage and sleep

Add a short bullet under “Dos and don'ts” or a brief FAQ. Do not turn this into a massage how-to.

```markdown
**Does a bedtime massage help a baby sleep?**
Some families find a short, gentle massage calming, and NHS baby-massage leaflets treat it mainly as a bonding activity. A Cochrane review of massage for infants under 6 months found the evidence not strong enough to recommend it for mental or physical health in low-risk babies. It is not a sleep-training method and not a reason to add essential oils unless a clinician has told you to. Stop if the baby is unwell, has a fever or broken skin, or is clearly unhappy. Keep the rest of the routine boring and consistent.
```
