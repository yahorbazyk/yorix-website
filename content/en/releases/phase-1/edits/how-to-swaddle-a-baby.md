```markdown
## “Wrapping,” the startle (Moro), and arms-up sleep

In Australia and the UK, **wrapping** is the usual word for the same skill this page calls swaddling. Red Nose, Raising Children Network, and Karitane wrapping advice follow the same safety limits already here: hips free to bend, chest not tight, no hood, and **stop once the baby can roll**. Use this page’s how-to; do not keep a second wrapping URL.

A newborn’s **Moro (startle) reflex** can jerk the arms and briefly wake a baby, including as they drop into light sleep. That reflex is ordinary, and it fades over the early months. Swaddling is sometimes used to make those jerks less of a full wake — it is not a treatment for a diagnosis, and it is not a reason to wrap past the rolling deadline already in the section above.

**Arms-up** (or one arm out) is a common way to come out of the wrap, already listed in the transition steps above. Some babies sleep more happily with hands free or near the face. That is a transition, not a new method. If startle-waking is really a short cycle at the end of a nap, see [baby sleep cycles](/baby-sleep-cycles) and [false starts](/false-starts) rather than tightening the wrap.
```

```markdown
**Should I stop the swaddle cold turkey or take one arm out first?**
Either can work if they are not rolling yet. One arm out, then both, then a wearable blanket with arms free, is a common sequence. Some families go straight to an arms-free sack. There is no official AAP “one-arm” protocol. If they can roll, or are close, stop now — do not wait out a startle. See [startle reflex and sleep](/startle-reflex-and-sleep) and [sleep sacks](/sleep-sacks).

**They still startle after we stopped the swaddle. Can we wrap again?**
Not if rolling has started. The startle reflex can outlast the swaddle; sources also disagree on when the Moro reflex fades (HealthyChildren.org’s table says about 2 months; KidsHealth usually 3–6 months; MedlinePlus after 3 or 4 months). We do not average those. Safety wins: arms free once rolling starts.
```
