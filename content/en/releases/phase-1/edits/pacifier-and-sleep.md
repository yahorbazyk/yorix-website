```markdown
## Thumb sucking at sleep time

Some babies and toddlers soothe themselves with a **thumb or fingers** instead of a pacifier. That habit is common and is not, by itself, a sleep disorder. The AAP’s safer-sleep point about offering a **pacifier** at sleep time is specific to a pacifier; it does not mean you must introduce one, and it does not mean you should pull a comfortable thumb out of a sleeping baby’s mouth.

If you already use a pacifier, stay with the introduction and weaning advice later on this page. If the child prefers a thumb, you do not need a second “thumb-sleep” URL. Bring dental timing to the dentist: KidsHealth and HealthyChildren.org both discuss pacifiers and thumb sucking as oral habits to review as teeth develop, and the weaning-age disagreement already on this page is about **pacifiers**. Do not invent a new official “stop the thumb at X months” rule here. If the skin is very sore, or you have questions about teeth or a habit that is not fading in the later preschool years, ask the pediatrician or dentist.
```
