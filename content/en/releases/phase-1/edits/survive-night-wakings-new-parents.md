```markdown
**Why is a 6–8 week old waking so often at night?**
Frequent night waking is still typical at this age. Hunger, cluster feeding, and the 6–8 week fuss peak described for Period of PURPLE Crying (NCSBS) often travel together. Use [cluster feeding](/cluster-feeding) for bunched evening feeds, [6 week old baby sleep](/6-week-old-baby-sleep) for the week snapshot, and [6 week sleep regression](/6-week-sleep-regression) for that popular name. Do not start a formal training plan at 6–8 weeks (Sleep Foundation generally discusses readiness around 4–6 months). Do not create a `/6-week-night-wakings` URL.
```

## 3. Parental shift work or single-parent nights

**Targets:** `/survive-night-wakings-new-parents` (primary), `/bedtime-routine-for-baby`, `/daycare-and-baby-sleep`

**Where:** FAQ on the survive page. Do not create `/shift-work-and-baby-sleep`. Official AAP/NHS pages do not publish a shift-work infant timetable.

```markdown
**How do we protect baby sleep when a parent works nights, or when there is only one adult?**
Keep the *baby’s* day and night the right way around — light and ordinary noise by day, a dim, boring night — even if a parent is on a night shift. Do not flip the baby onto the parent’s work clock. The same short bedtime routine can be done by whoever is on duty; the sequence matters more than which adult does it. See [bedtime routine for baby](/bedtime-routine-for-baby). Safe sleep does not relax because you are exhausted: back, firm, flat, clear, and never a sofa or armchair (the sofa risk is already on [bed-sharing and co-sleeping](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping)). If you are dangerously tired, ask for help for you as well — the mental-health and exhaustion advice already on this page still applies when there is no second adult to “take a shift.”
```

## 9. Should you change a diaper at night

**Targets:** `/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby` and `/survive-night-wakings-new-parents`

**Where:** FAQ. Do not create `/should-you-change-diaper-at-night`. There is no official “hours a slightly wet diaper may stay.”

```markdown
**Should I wake my baby to change a diaper at night?**
NHS advice on helping a baby to sleep: at night, do not change them unless they need it, and do not play. “Need it” means a soiled diaper or one that is soaking through, not every slightly damp one. If you do change them, keep the lights dim and the visit boring, then put them back on their back in their own space. This is not a reason to skip a night *feed* your pediatrician still wants you to offer.
```

**Source to add:** NHS. “Helping your baby to sleep.” At night you might find it helpful not to change the baby unless they need it, keep lights low, and not play. <https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/helping-your-baby-to-sleep/>. Accessed 16 November 2026.
