```markdown
## Apnea of prematurity — a clinical issue, not a shopping problem

Some preterm babies have **apnea of prematurity**: pauses in breathing, or shorter pauses with a slow heart rate or low oxygen, because the systems that control breathing are still maturing (Cleveland Clinic; KidsHealth). That is diagnosed and managed in hospital, often in the NICU. It is **not** the same as ordinary [periodic breathing](/newborn-grunting-in-sleep#periodic-breathing-a-normal-newborn-pattern-not-a-home-diagnosis), and it is **not** a reason to buy a consumer “SIDS monitor” on the way home (see [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies)).

If your team discharges you with a prescribed monitor, use it as they taught you, and still keep the crib rules in the section above. HealthyChildren.org’s NICU safe-sleep advice is the same direction already on this page: units should model **back-sleeping** before discharge, and positioning aids from the hospital are not souvenirs for the home crib unless a clinician has prescribed a specific device and told you how long to use it. We will not write an apnea-treatment protocol here. Ask the neonatology or pediatric team about any pause you see after discharge.
```

**Sources to add** (opened or confirmed 15 November 2026):

1. Cleveland Clinic. “Apnea of Prematurity.” Clinical definition (pauses of about 15–20 seconds, or shorter pauses with a slow heart rate or low oxygen); diagnosed in the NICU; distinct from periodic breathing. <https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/apnea-of-prematurity>
2. Nemours KidsHealth. “Apnea of Prematurity.” Similar clinical definition; periodic breathing distinguished as short pauses with a return to regular breathing and no color or heart-rate change. <https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/aop.html>
3. HealthyChildren.org (AAP). “Safe Sleep for Babies in the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit.” Model supine sleep before going home; do not bring unprescribed positioning aids home. Add the exact current URL at paste time.

```markdown
**Why won’t my baby sleep after a hospital stay?**
Hospital days are noisy, brightly lit, and full of checks. Nights at home often look bumpier for a while. That is not an official “hospital sleep regression.” Keep the same safe-sleep rules you use every night (back, firm, flat, clear). If they are still recovering from an illness, stay on this page for congestion, fever, and when to call. If the stay was in a NICU, use [preemie sleep](/preemie-sleep) and corrected age, not a new URL. The first night home after birth belongs on [1 week old baby sleep](/1-week-old-baby-sleep) and [newborn sleep schedule](/newborn-sleep-schedule).
```
