## 17. Arching the back / “only sleeps on their side” when reflux is the suspicion — merge into `/reflux-and-baby-sleep`

**Where:** Add a short H2 **immediately after** `## The sleep rule does not change`, and **before** `## What may help while your baby is awake`. Side-sleep position questions still go to `/baby-sleeping-on-stomach`. Do not recommend a rice bag, wedge, or positioner.

**Paste this heading and body:**

```markdown
## Arching, and the “they only sleep on their side” story

Some babies with uncomfortable reflux **arch** or throw their head back during or after a feed, and some parents then conclude the baby will only sleep on their side or tummy. Arching can be a sign of discomfort, and it is a reason to talk to the pediatrician. It is **not** a reason to start nights on the side. The sleep rule in the section above does not change: **back, firm, flat**, no wedge, no incline.

Questions of the form “can they sleep on their side if they have reflux?” belong on [baby sleeping on the stomach](/baby-sleeping-on-stomach) and [safe sleep](/safe-sleep-for-babies), with the same answer: start on the back. If you find them rolled to the side and they can roll both ways, the stomach-sleeping page already says what to do.

Do not put a **rice bag, bean bag, or heated pack** on a baby’s tummy, and do not prop them on a pillow to “settle the reflux.” HealthyChildren.org has had to answer that kind of folk remedy; it is not a safe-sleep solution. Comfort while they are **awake and supervised** (upright after a feed, as the next section describes) is different from overnight position.
```

## 16. Arching the back when sleeping or settling

**Targets:** `/reflux-and-baby-sleep` and `/overtired-baby`

**Where:** FAQ. Not a diagnosis.

```markdown
**Why does my baby arch their back when sleeping or when I try to put them down?**
It can be reflux, tiredness, ordinary stretching, or protest. It is not a diagnosis on its own. If there are feeding-pain signs, frequent vomiting, or poor weight gain, use [reflux and baby sleep](/reflux-and-baby-sleep) and call. If the picture is a “second wind” at bedtime, see [overtired baby](/overtired-baby) and [why does my baby fight sleep](/why-does-my-baby-fight-sleep).
```

## 10. Spit-up during sleep / “will they choke on their back?”

**Targets:** `/safe-sleep-for-babies` and `/reflux-and-baby-sleep` (both already say healthy babies, and most with GERD, will not choke on their backs).

**Where:** Strengthen the existing FAQ or add this sentence. Do not create a third URL.

```markdown
**Can a baby choke on spit-up while sleeping on their back?**
HealthyChildren.org’s “Baby Burping, Hiccups & Spit-Up” (updated 31 December 2024) says spit-up almost never involves choking, coughing, discomfort, or danger to the child, **even if it occurs during sleep**, and that the best position is still on the back with the mattress flat, not elevated. That matches the AAP / NASPGHAN advice already on this page. Do not use a wedge, a rolled towel, or a car seat as a “choking workaround.” If spit-up comes with poor weight gain, forceful or green vomit, or breathing trouble, use [reflux and baby sleep](/reflux-and-baby-sleep) and call. Related: [baby hiccups and sleep](/baby-hiccups-and-sleep).
```
