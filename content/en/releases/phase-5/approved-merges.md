## 1. Swaddle transition

**Target:** `/how-to-swaddle-a-baby`

**Where:** FAQ (the page already has “When to stop, and how to come out of the swaddle”). Do not add a second essay.

```markdown
**Should I stop the swaddle cold turkey or take one arm out first?**
Either can work if they are not rolling yet. One arm out, then both, then a wearable blanket with arms free, is a common sequence. Some families go straight to an arms-free sack. There is no official AAP “one-arm” protocol. If they can roll, or are close, stop now — do not wait out a startle. See [startle reflex and sleep](/startle-reflex-and-sleep) and [sleep sacks](/sleep-sacks).

**They still startle after we stopped the swaddle. Can we wrap again?**
Not if rolling has started. The startle reflex can outlast the swaddle; sources also disagree on when the Moro reflex fades (HealthyChildren.org’s table says about 2 months; KidsHealth usually 3–6 months; MedlinePlus after 3 or 4 months). We do not average those. Safety wins: arms free once rolling starts.
```

## 2. Vaccines and sleep

**Targets:** `/why-is-my-baby-suddenly-sleeping-more` (primary) and `/sick-baby-sleep`

**Where:** FAQ on both if the question is missing. Do not create `/baby-sleep-after-vaccines`.

```markdown
**Can vaccines affect my baby’s sleep?**
They can, for a short time. NHS inform (babies and children up to 3 years 4 months) lists being **sleepy**, irritable, upset, or less hungry than usual as common side effects after a vaccine; these usually pass within a couple of days. The Lullaby Trust: after vaccinations some babies may feel irritable and unwell, and may develop a fever; **dress them for the environment and do not wrap them in extra clothes and blankets**. The Lullaby Trust also states that vaccinations do **not** increase the risk of SIDS, and that babies who are up to date with vaccinations have a **lower** risk of SIDS.

Call your pediatrician or local urgent line if you cannot rouse them, if they seem significantly unwell, or if you are worried about a fever — especially under 3 months. Do not add loose blankets to the crib. See also [sick baby sleep](/sick-baby-sleep) and [baby sweating in sleep](/baby-sweating-in-sleep).
```

**Target:** `/baby-sleeping-on-stomach`

**Where:** FAQ.

```markdown
**Why does my baby sleep with their bum in the air?**
It is a common rolled position, not a separate diagnosis. Always **start** on the back, on a firm, flat, clear surface. If they can roll and they get themselves into a rump-up position, the same rolling rule already on this page applies: you usually leave them if the space is clear and there are no loose blankets or pillows.
```
