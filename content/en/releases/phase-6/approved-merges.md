```markdown
A new room, new noises, and a temporarily unpacked crib can unsettle sleep. That is a change of place, not a new official regression age. ## 3. Parental shift work or single-parent nights
```

**Targets:** `/survive-night-wakings-new-parents` (primary), `/bedtime-routine-for-baby`, `/daycare-and-baby-sleep`

**Where:** FAQ on the survive page. Do not create `/shift-work-and-baby-sleep`. Official AAP/NHS pages do not publish a shift-work infant timetable.

```markdown
**How do we protect baby sleep when a parent works nights, or when there is only one adult?**
Keep the *baby’s* day and night the right way around — light and ordinary noise by day, a dim, boring night — even if a parent is on a night shift. Do not flip the baby onto the parent’s work clock. The same short bedtime routine can be done by whoever is on duty; the sequence matters more than which adult does it. See [bedtime routine for baby](/bedtime-routine-for-baby). Safe sleep does not relax because you are exhausted: back, firm, flat, clear, and never a sofa or armchair (the sofa risk is already on [bed-sharing and co-sleeping](/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping)). If you are dangerously tired, ask for help for you as well — the mental-health and exhaustion advice already on this page still applies when there is no second adult to “take a shift.”
```

```markdown
**Do babies need blackout curtains or a pitch-black room?**
There is no official AAP lux number, and no rule that every baby must sleep in pitch black. What official sources do support is a clear day–night difference: more light and ordinary household noise by day, a dimmer, boring room at night (see [newborn day-night confusion](/newborn-day-night-confusion)). ## 6. Crib or bassinet mattress firmness
```

**Target:** `/safe-sleep-for-babies`

**Where:** FAQ, next to the firm, flat surface section.

```markdown
**How firm should a crib or bassinet mattress be?**
Firm and flat, with only a tight fitted sheet, on a sleep product that meets safety standards for a crib, bassinet, or play yard. That is already the rule on this page. A mattress is too soft if it indents under the baby’s head or chest, or if you have added a topper, memory foam, or a quilt. Do not “make it softer.” If you are unsure, ask your pediatrician and check the manufacturer’s standard — do not add padding.
```

```markdown
**Can a newborn or older baby sleep in a pack-and-play overnight, at home or when visiting?**
```

## 8. Can a baby sleep on my chest

**Targets:** `/bed-sharing-and-co-sleeping` (primary), `/contact-naps`, `/safe-sleep-for-babies`

**Where:** Prominent FAQ. Do not create `/can-baby-sleep-on-my-chest`.

```markdown
**Can a baby sleep on my chest if I am awake?**
Holding a baby on your chest while you are fully awake and alert is not the same as sleeping with them on your chest. The risk is dozing off, especially on a sofa or armchair: HealthyChildren.org (AAP) cites a risk of sleep-related death up to 67 times higher when a baby sleeps with someone on a couch or soft chair — already in the table on this page. If there is any chance you might fall asleep, move them to their own firm, flat, clear surface on their back first. A contact nap in your arms still has to end on a safe surface if you need to put them down; see [contact naps](/contact-naps).
```

## 9. Should you change a diaper at night

**Targets:** `/should-you-wake-a-sleeping-baby` and `/survive-night-wakings-new-parents`

**Where:** FAQ. Do not create `/should-you-change-diaper-at-night`. There is no official “hours a slightly wet diaper may stay.”

```markdown
**Should I wake my baby to change a diaper at night?**
NHS advice on helping a baby to sleep: at night, do not change them unless they need it, and do not play. “Need it” means a soiled diaper or one that is soaking through, not every slightly damp one. If you do change them, keep the lights dim and the visit boring, then put them back on their back in their own space. This is not a reason to skip a night *feed* your pediatrician still wants you to offer.
```

## 10. Spit-up during sleep / “will they choke on their back?”

**Targets:** `/safe-sleep-for-babies` and `/reflux-and-baby-sleep` (both already say healthy babies, and most with GERD, will not choke on their backs).

**Where:** Strengthen the existing FAQ or add this sentence. Do not create a third URL.

```markdown
**Can a baby choke on spit-up while sleeping on their back?**
HealthyChildren.org’s “Baby Burping, Hiccups & Spit-Up” (updated 31 December 2024) says spit-up almost never involves choking, coughing, discomfort, or danger to the child, **even if it occurs during sleep**, and that the best position is still on the back with the mattress flat, not elevated. That matches the AAP / NASPGHAN advice already on this page. Do not use a wedge, a rolled towel, or a car seat as a “choking workaround.” If spit-up comes with poor weight gain, forceful or green vomit, or breathing trouble, use [reflux and baby sleep](/reflux-and-baby-sleep) and call. Related: [baby hiccups and sleep](/baby-hiccups-and-sleep).
```

**Source to add** if it is not already numbered: HealthyChildren.org. “Baby Burping, Hiccups & Spit-Up,” last updated 31 December 2024. <https://www.healthychildren.org/english/ages-stages/baby/feeding-nutrition/pages/baby-burping-hiccups-and-spit-up.aspx>. Accessed 16 November 2026.

## 12. Breath-holding / periodic breathing / apnea signs (pointer)

**Targets:** `/newborn-grunting-in-sleep` and `/baby-snoring-and-mouth-breathing`

**Where:** FAQ if the exact wording is thin. No `/infant-sleep-apnea` diagnosis URL.

```markdown
**Why does my baby seem to hold their breath or gasp in sleep?**
Short pauses followed by a few faster breaths are often **periodic breathing**, already described on [newborn grunting in sleep](/newborn-grunting-in-sleep) (HealthyChildren.org: pauses of less than 10 seconds, usually settling by 1 month, if the baby is otherwise comfortable). Frequent loud snoring, longer pauses, or a chin-out mouth-open posture belongs on [baby snoring and mouth breathing](/baby-snoring-and-mouth-breathing). Breath-holding spells when a child is *awake and upset* are a different clinical picture — ask the pediatrician, do not start a new sleep URL. Call 911 if they cannot be woken, are struggling to breathe, or their colour changes suddenly.
```
